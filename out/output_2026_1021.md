## 0. [Matrix-Game 4.0: Harnessing Video Models for Real-Time Playable Game Worlds](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27999) <a id="link0"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.27999
**Authors:** Jihai Zhang, Runjia Qian, Kaiqin Kong, Biao Jiang, Puyi Wang, Siru Chen, Tianle Li, Guanjie Chen, Junyi Wu, Chubin Chen, Yuanhang Li, Kaichen Huang, Haobo Zhang, Yangguang Li, Wei Yu, Guibin Chen, Yu Cheng, Yahui Zhou

**Abstract:** Moving from controllable videos to genuinely playable worlds requires persistent world state, executable game rules, and low-latency interaction. We outline a roadmap for the next generation of Matrix-Game that separates game execution from visual generation through an executable game harness, and introduce Matrix-Game 4.0 as the first system built on this design. A lightweight game engine maintains world state and executes interaction rules, a multimodal coding agent translates player ideas into game code and dynamic scene captions, and a renderer based on autoregressive video model transforms engine-rendered low-poly proxies into detailed visuals, conditioned on dynamic captions and memory references. This framework provides continuous guidance for visual generation and enables natural-language creation and modification of scenes and gameplay. Our renderer is trained on aligned low-poly and high-fidelity video pairs and distilled into a causal generator that generates in four-frame chunks with one or two denoising steps. Together with targeted inference optimizations, Matrix-Game 4.0 enables 480p, 60 FPS gameplay on a single NVIDIA RTX5090 with a key-press-to-display latency of 112ms. Single-step generation achieves 83FPS steady-state throughput with 18.2GiB of peak GPU memory usage. With fixed prompts, steady-state throughput also reaches 67FPS on an RTX5080 and 58FPS on an RTX5070Ti. We demonstrate customizable shooter and driving games with engine-enforced mechanics, and release code, model weights, and low-poly game assets to support research and creation of playable game worlds.

## 2. [VLA-Retain: Benchmarking Cross-Dimension Effects of Vision Tuning in VLAs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27698) <a id="link2"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.27698
**Authors:** Borui Xu, Han Chen, Bingsheng He, Ming Wu, Jason Zeng, Michael Heinrich

**Abstract:** Vision-language-action (VLA) models transfer knowledge from large-scale pretraining into robot control, but their manipulation performance remains sensitive to changes in observation conditions, which recent work addresses by fine-tuning the visual module on target-domain data. However, it remains unclear how such adaptation affects robustness beyond the target condition, as existing robustness benchmarks only evaluate models without fine-tuning and adaptation studies only report the target gain. Measuring this cross-dimension effect requires separating target gains from off-target changes, isolating adaptation effects from architectural differences, ruling out generic forgetting, and comparing tuning choices. Therefore, we introduce VLA-Retain, a benchmark that compares five open VLA models, tuned on Camera-view or Sensor-noise data, with their own base checkpoints on LIBERO-Plus dataset spanning seven perturbation dimensions. Our study reveals four findings. First, targeted vision tuning is not local, and its target gain does not predict retention. Mean off-target gain is negative in 8 of 10 model--domain configurations. Under Camera-view tuning, X-VLA gains $69.4$ percentage points on the target but loses $4.6$ points on average elsewhere, whereas OpenVLA-OFT gains $39.3$ and $1.4$ points, respectively. Second, these regressions are not explained only by performance loss on the same tasks without perturbations. Third, broader training coverage raises overall gain over target-specific training but does not eliminate dimension-level regressions. Fourth, tuning strategies are model-dependent, as DELTA-style feature preservation raises X-VLA's mean off-target gain to $+1.2$ points but lowers OpenVLA-OFT's to $-23.1$. Overall, these findings show that target-domain success alone is insufficient and highlight cross-dimensional retention as a key criterion for targeted VLA adaptation.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 3 closely: it introduces a new embodied AI benchmark for VLA adaptation, focusing on cross-dimensional retention under perturbations rather than only target-domain success.

## 3. [Decoupling Action from Egocentric Observation for World Simulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27946) <a id="link3"></a>

**Authors:** Yue Ma, Pengjie Song, Xinyu Wang, Yi He, Zeqian Long, Fangneng Zhan, Kaichen Zhou, Hongyu Liu, Hongfa Wang, Peihao Li, Haoyang Huang, Nan Duan, Qifeng Chen

**Abstract:** Egocentric action transfer aims to decouple the semantic action from egocentric observations and reproduce them in new visual contexts, enabling scalable world simulation, embodied policy learning, and robotic data generation. Existing approaches to transferring actions rely on condition-guided video generation, which converts the observation video into explicit geometric controls (e.g., hand poses or meshes) to drive synthesis. However, these methods introduce geometric estimation errors, making it difficult to preserve physically plausible interactions when transferred to new scenes. Alternatively, motion transfer methods directly extract motion patterns from the observation video, yet motion-level features alone cannot encode rich contact dynamics, frequently leading to severe hand structural collapse and implausible contact layout. To address both limitations, we present EgoACT, a test-time framework for egocentric action transfer that operates directly on the denoising process of video diffusion models without requiring additional geometric estimators. EgoACT uses Velocity-guided Structure Anchoring to stabilize reference-consistent hand-object structure in the early denoising stage, and Sparse Correspondence Calibration to refine reliable local correspondences in the mid-to-late denoising stages. Together, these two components preserve transferable action semantics while improving temporal coherence and interaction realism. We further establish EgoActionBench, a benchmark for evaluating action preservation, visual quality, and hand-object plausibility across diverse egocentric manipulation scenarios. Experiments show that EgoACT generates more coherent and physically plausible interaction videos than strong baselines.

## 5. [Robustness of Internal Signals for Hallucination Detection in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27041) <a id="link5"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.27041
**Authors:** Allison Chen, William Yang, Salma Abdel Magid, Jonathan Williams, Olga Russakovsky, Esin Tureci

**Abstract:** When given an image and a text prompt, vision-language models (VLMs) can produce seemingly sophisticated text responses. However, they are also prone to hallucinate objects in their output, which can have detrimental consequences in safety-critical applications. To detect these hallucinations, certain methods interestingly leverage internal signals from the VLM as it generates the text response; potentially, these methods use the same signals that led to a hallucination to detect it. We conduct an in-depth analysis to understand the robustness of these detection methods. First, we show that a naive baseline of a generated word's token position in the VLM text response achieves competitive performance. Further, we reveal how some detection methods rely on token position and use this heuristic as a shortcut. Second, we explore detection methods' sensitivity to language and vision modalities. Our experiments suggest that strong detection performance is often driven by detecting language-based hallucinations, and when methods rely on token position, they tend to be less visually grounded and exhibit reduced robustness when hallucinations occur earlier in a text response. Lastly, we demonstrate that different detection methods can carry complementary signals; when ensembling methods together, adding weaker methods to an ensemble of higher performing ones still offers performance improvements. Altogether, while there may be useful internal VLM signals to detect hallucinations, our investigation reveals important limitations of the current methods that try to leverage them.

## 6. [VisualEvolve: A Skill Self-Evolution Framework for Visual Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28386) <a id="link6"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.28386
**Authors:** Jiawei Wang, Yucun Li, Xueqi Li, Shenghao Wang, Xinquan Gong, Dan Zhang, Zheqi Lv, Yuquan Le, Shaozu Yuan, An Zhang, Da Cao, Tat-Seng Chua

**Abstract:** Visual tool use enables vision-language models (VLMs) to acquire task-relevant evidence, but its effectiveness can be limited by predefined visual operations and the tool-use strategies learned during training. Representing these operations and strategies as reusable external skills allows them to be refined through self-evolution without updating model parameters. However, skill refinement can introduce performance regressions. Meanwhile, inspecting accumulated task execution trajectories together with skill evolution history may lead to evolution evidence overload. To address these challenges, we propose VisualEvolve, a self-evolving framework that evolves executable visual operations and tool-use strategies for VLMs through a Select--Execute--Evolve--Evaluate cycle. Skill-Library Snapshot Selection uses performance on a fixed validation set disjoint from execution tasks to select an effective historical snapshot for subsequent evolution, limiting the propagation of performance regressions. Progressive Evidence Access (PEA) guides capability-gap diagnosis through a coarse-to-fine hierarchy comprising the Skill-Performance Report, Evolution Log, Trajectory Summaries, and relevant raw trajectory segments. Across four VLM backbones and six evaluation sets from three benchmarks, VisualEvolve improves over the Initial Skill Library in 23 of 24 settings, with a maximum gain of 15.1 percentage points on VisualProbe Hard using Qwen3.5-Plus. Ablation studies support the contributions of Snapshot Selection and PEA, while further experiments show that skills can be improved using experience from different data settings and directly reused across backbones. Moreover, VisualEvolve enables effective skill self-evolution with over an order of magnitude fewer task-solving trajectories than estimated for representative RL-based methods.

## 7. [GARRET: Generative Feedback for Region Retrieval in Visual Documents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27829) <a id="link7"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.27829
**Authors:** Weijian Jian, Siyang Chen, Yuguang Yang, Yajun Zhang, Chunyu Xie, Dawei Leng, Yuhui Yin

**Abstract:** Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation over visually rich documents requires evidence at a finer granularity than entire pages. Existing visual retrievers typically index or return full pages, whereas training region-level retrievers often requires costly query-to-region annotations or noisy model-generated labels. We introduce GARRET, a region-retrieval framework trained from (page, question, answer) triplets through differentiable generative feedback. A shared context-preserving retriever produces page and region multi-vector representations. During training, their retrieval scores define a soft routing distribution over candidate region crops. We introduce ViDoRe-V3-Region, a corpus-wide benchmark containing 200,682 fixed candidate regions across eight domains. GARRET achieves a macro nDCG@10 of 0.4126, outperforming the strongest visual baseline by 2.94 points. On page retrieval, it remains comparable to ColQwen2.5-v0.2 while indexing 47.8% fewer vectors per page.

## 13. [PACE: Plan, Attend, Check, and Emit for Training-Free Response Timing in Streaming Video Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28323) <a id="link13"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.28323
**Authors:** Siqi Guo, Yuxia Chen, Wenbo Zhu, Yangguang Ji, Zhenxiang Jiang, Teng Zhong, Yu Zhang

**Abstract:** Streaming video understanding requires a model to decide not only what to answer but also when to answer. Evidence arrives progressively, and recent benchmarks identify premature responses, issued before the queried event has concluded, as a recurring failure mode. Existing approaches often learn response timing through additional training, while training-free alternatives typically rely on scalar gating signals that do not explicitly represent when an answer becomes final. We organize chunk-wise streaming errors into three patterns: sampling variation can make an answer unstable, a stable answer may lack support in the observed video, or an answer may be supported by the current evidence while the event remains unfinished.
