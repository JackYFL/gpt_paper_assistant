## 0. [HiMEM: Hippocampus-Inspired Memory Consolidation, Pruning, and Composition for Vision-Language-Action Models in 3D Scenes](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09355) <a id="link0"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.09355
**Authors:** Boran Zhao, Yanbin Hu, Jun Ye, Zhenxian Hu, Yuxin Zhai, Jin Cui, Pengju Ren

**Abstract:** Memory plays a crucial role in long-horizon task execution and cross-task generalization for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models operating in complex 3D scenes. However, existing VLA memory mechanisms suffer from memory noise, particularly in complex 3D scenes, as stored episodic memories contain extensive redundant details about scene layouts, object configurations, and robot-specific states, which obscure generalizable manipulation principles and hinder cross-task transfer. To address this challenge, we first introduce a hippocampus-inspired memory consolidation mechanism that synthesizes extensive episodic experiences into a compact set of essential semantic memories, enabling VLA models to derive generalizable principles from practical experience. Second, we propose an episodic memory pruning mechanism that removes redundant episodic memories whose contents are captured by consolidated semantic memories, thereby increasing effective memory capacity and improving long-horizon task execution. Finally, to handle complex task scenarios, we develop a flexible memory composition mechanism that jointly leverages multiple episodic and semantic memories to enhance cross-scenario generalization. Experiments on LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus demonstrate the effectiveness of HiMEM across different VLA backbones, improving the average success rate of pi_0 from 91.88% to 98.20% on LIBERO.

## 1. [AtariBench: Evaluating Spatiotemporal Reasoning of Vision-Language Models in Atari Gameplay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08755) <a id="link1"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.08755
**Authors:** Mingjia Huo, Yao Fu, Bo Chang, Yaqing Wang, Dawei Zhu, Dylan Zhang, Xinyang Yi, Lichan Hong, Ed H. Chi, Hao Zhang, Jiaxi Tang

**Abstract:** Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved strong performance on static visual understanding benchmarks, yet their ability to act in dynamic environments remains less well understood. We introduce AtariBench, a controlled benchmark that evaluates VLMs across 20 Atari 2600 games, requiring models to make sequential decisions from high-temporal-resolution gameplay streams. By decoupling fast in-game dynamics from wall-clock inference speed, AtariBench focuses on whether models can perceive rapid visual state changes, reason over spatiotemporal dynamics, and ground actions in the current observation. Our experiments reveal substantial limitations in state-of-the-art VLMs: models often fail to infer object motion and direction, exhibit brittle sequential decision-making, and over-rely on textual interaction history despite newly appended visual feedback. We further introduce a separability analysis to identify which in-game scores provide reliable signals of model competence, and use the benchmark to study how memory design and prompting strategies affect performance. Overall, AtariBench exposes a persistent gap between passive visual perception and active decision-making, providing a lightweight and rigorous testbed for developing VLMs that can reason and act in dynamic environments.

## 2. [TouchAnnotator: Multi-Source Tactile Supervision with Privileged-View Distillation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09129) <a id="link2"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.09129
**Authors:** Ao Li, Zhihao Yuan, Chubin Zhang, Kunrui Huang, Sheng Xu, Zengjue Chen, Junjie Wang, Jiawei Li, Yansong Tang

**Abstract:** Egocentric video captures human-object interaction at scale but rarely includes tactile labels for hidden contact and pressure. We introduce TouchAnnotator, an ego-only bimanual tactile annotator trained with multi-source tactile supervision and privileged-view distillation. A canonical hand field unifies glove pressure, geometry-derived human contact, and robot fingertip deformation from six datasets and three embodiments, while source-aware masks preserve valid targets. During training, an EMA teacher observes synchronized ego and wrist views and distills hand-local representations and tactile predictions into a student that uses only ego video and estimated pose at inference. The model transfers to bare-hand contact and produces qualitatively coherent tactile responses across bare-hand, gloved-hand, and robot embodiments. On measured recordings, it improves temporal accuracy by 5.38 points and Volumetric IoU by 5.02 points under a shared protocol. These results show that heterogeneous human--robot supervision and privileged training views can turn egocentric video into dense tactile annotations.

## 3. [VoluCompose: Constructing Compact Volumetric Visual Representations for Medical Image Understanding](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08649) <a id="link3"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.08649
**Authors:** Zanting Ye, Yunrui Lin, Shengyuan Liu, Jing Hao, Chen Hu, Yefeng Zheng, Lijun Lu

**Abstract:** Medical vision--language models can interpret volumetric images, but processing full volumes is costly because hundreds of slices produce long, redundant visual-token sequences. Existing compression methods often couple content selection with budget determination. We observe that neighboring slices share visual features and that attention concentration varies across visual encoders. We propose VoluCompose, a training-free, model-agnostic framework that separates what visual content to retain from how many tokens to allocate. Its hyperbolic token composition module groups contiguous slices into ordered support units and uses the Lorentz distance to account for feature magnitude and direction during token allocation, anchor selection, and refinement. Its capacity control module uses visual-encoder attention entropy to allocate more tokens when attention is more dispersed. We evaluate VoluCompose on two tasks across three datasets and four backbones. Across nine calibrated settings, VoluCompose attains the highest mean quality--retention hypervolume among the compared methods. It achieves a mean relative quality of 121.68\% against the uncompressed references while retaining 22.78% of visual tokens on average. Compression remains robust even at a 10% token budget. Across settings, the lowest ROUGE-L ratio to the uncompressed reference is 96.4\% for VoluCompose. The corresponding minima for the compression baselines range from 15.0% to 70.5%. Code is available at https://github.com/yezanting/VoluCompose.

## 4. [Seeing vs. Counting: Complexity Twins Show Where Multimodal LLMs Fail on Diagrams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08998) <a id="link4"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.08998
**Authors:** Yin Liu

**Abstract:** When a multimodal LLM miscounts in a diagram, does it fail at seeing or at counting? Existing visual graph and puzzle benchmarks vary visual density and algorithmic hardness together, confounding the two. We pair counting questions about the same drawing that sit in different counting-complexity classes, such as its spanning trees (a determinant) and its simple paths (#P-complete), and use a transcription probe and a text-only control to separate reading from computing. We test Gemini, Gemma and Qwen3-VL models; for the Gemini models, the answer depends on how the model computes. In chain of thought, most of them read small drawings correctly and then fail at counting, at a rate set far more by the size of the answer than by its complexity class (for the primary model, the odds of an exact answer fall 2.38-fold per decade of the answer). ## 5. [T2I Models Rarely Invert Negation but Often Fail to Suppress Negated Concepts: Benchmarking Negation Suppression in Text-to-Image Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09304) <a id="link5"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.09304
**Authors:** Akansha Gautam, Aparna Bharati, Mayank Vatsa, Richa Singh

**Abstract:** Text-to-image diffusion models often fail to suppress explicitly negated concepts, such as a lamp for "a desk without a lamp." We introduce T2I-NSI, a benchmark and metric for measuring negation sensitivity in text-to-image generation. It pairs each negated prompt with a canonical positive counterpart and factorizes negation by linguistic form (lexical/semantic, morphological, syntactic) and target type (objects, attributes, relations, compositions, and nested cases). The benchmark contains 759,869 paired prompt atoms over a 5,082-object catalog, with 916,272 images (911,982 open-weights, 4,290 closed-source), scored by a Basic CLIP estimator and a Robust ensemble of CLIP, VQA and open-vocabulary detection. Negation sensitivity is low: open-weights generators score NSI <= 0.038, while GPT-Image-1.5 and Gemini-2.5-Flash-Image reach about 0.09. The dominant failure is under-suppression rather than misdirection. Inverse interpretation is essentially absent (0.00% across over 450K atom-model evaluations), whereas models render the concept under both prompts on 8-19% of atoms and only partly suppress it on a further 9-25%; most remaining atoms are too weakly rendered to label. A blinded human study (420 atoms, nine annotators) corroborates the near-absence of inversion and the high persistence rate, and a matched-prompt comparison shows the open-versus-closed gap (~3.5x) is not a prompt-generator artifact. Code and data: https://github.com/IAB-IITJ/T2I-NSI

## 11. [Left-Right-Consistent Iterative Stereo Matching via Cross-Injection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09458) <a id="link11"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.09458
**Authors:** Manuel Knott, Diogo Luvizon, Daniel Scharstein, Elias Vansteenkiste

**Abstract:** State-of-the-art stereo matching models predominantly predict the disparity of only the left (reference) view. However, applications such as per-eye AR/VR rendering, novel view synthesis, or two-view depth fusion require disparity for both views, and need those two maps to agree. The usual workaround of running the network a second time on the horizontally flipped and swapped pair gives two independent predictions that can be inconsistent, especially on transparent surfaces, in textureless regions, and at occlusion boundaries. We introduce cross-injection (CI), a lightweight extension for iterative (refinement-based) models that can be added to any pretrained left-only iterative matcher. It couples the two passes by injecting the explicit, signed left-right inconsistency as an additional input cue at every step of the refinement stage. We empirically show that CI substantially reduces left-right disagreement on every benchmark (more than halving it on Booster for FoundationStereo), while largely preserving sharp object boundaries. Furthermore, using two state-of-the-art stereo matching models, we show that CI also reduces the single-view disparity error, making it a worthwhile extension even if only left disparity is needed.

**Abstract:** Sparse query-based 3D detectors commonly encode heterogeneous semantic, geometric, and motion information into unified latent queries, including when explicit 3D anchors are used. ## 13. [Decoupled Mode Connectivity for Base-to-Novel Generalization in Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09655) <a id="link13"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.09655
**Authors:** Imad Eddine Marouf, Khalid Oublal, Enzo Tartaglione, St\'ephane Lathuili\`ere

**Abstract:** Prompt learning adapts vision-language models such as CLIP by optimizing a short learnable prompt. The cross-entropy objective pulls this prompt toward   base-class specialization, whereas generalization to unseen classes benefits from staying close to CLIP's zero-shot feature space. Because both objectives   act on the same prompt, their gradients conflict at convergence, and in our experiments no single-prompt loss design clearly improves on the cosine   baseline. We propose Decoupled Mode Connectivity (DMC), which gives each objective its own prompt. Starting from a base-trained prompt, DMC trains a   specialization prompt and a generalization prompt, and merges their text features with a fixed coefficient at test time, at the inference cost of a single   prompt. Across 11 datasets,   DMC improves the average harmonic mean of base and novel accuracy over MergeTune for both CoOp and KgCoOp, and its interpolation curves pass beyond the   single-prompt operating point, shifting the base-novel frontier rather than trading along it. Code is available at https://github.com/IemProg/DMC.

## 15. [SPSC: Zero-Shot Cross-Subject EEG-to-Image Reconstruction via Semantic Preservation and Subspace Calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09380) <a id="link15"></a>

**Abstract:** Pretrained vision-language models and diffusion generators have driven rapid progress in brain-to-image generation, while EEG-based reconstruction is still constrained by low signal-to-noise ratio and cross-subject semantic shifts. **Abstract:** In this paper, a 3D backbone network (SpconvTransformer) based on the fusion of convolutional architecture branch and Transformer architecture branch is proposed. The convolution branch uses 3D sparse convolution and submanifold convolution to learn local voxel features and semantic information, and the Transformer branch uses the self-attention mechanism to model the remote relationship between voxels, uses the broad receptive field to capture rich context information, and complementing the advantages of the two architectures by fusing the local features extracted from the two branches and the global features solves the problem of limited receptive fields of 3D convolutional neural networks. The experimental results show that the average accuracy of SpconvTransformer in 3D object detection and bird's-eye view detection tasks reaches 81.45% and 88.09%, respectively, and the average directional similarity reaches 91.10%, which is significantly better than other existing algorithms.
