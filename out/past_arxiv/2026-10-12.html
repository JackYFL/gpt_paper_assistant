1 Vision Transformers Learn Word Meaning from Images Alone Anders Gj{\o}lbye, Sebastian Ray Mason, Stella Frank, Lars Kai Hansen
Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
17
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.13252 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 9

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 very closely: a vision foundation model (DINOv3) reveals surprising semantic structure learned from images alone, including word meaning and vector offsets.

Can a vision model learn what words mean from pixels alone? We show that the two largest DINOv3 vision transformers organize words rendered in images by word meaning, despite being pretrained using self-supervision on images only, without paired captions, class labels, or other language supervision. We derive word representations from embedding differences between word images and matched controls, then test whether semantically related words are neighbors within each model's embedding space, while controlling for spelling similarity. We find that brand names in the same industry group together, as do nouns from the same semantic domain. Country-capital pairs exhibit consistent vector offsets, similarly to word embeddings trained on text. These word representations also align with the representations of the visual object categories they name. DINOv3-H and DINOv3-7B are, to our knowledge, the first self-supervised vision models to show substantial semantic structure for rendered words. Comparisons with text and speech encoders reveal shared structure among representations of the same words. Contrastive alignment with either encoder further strengthened DINOv3-H's word-level semantic structure, while leaving the other finetuned vision models largely unchanged. Image-only training can therefore teach vision models not just what words look like, but also how their meanings relate.

2 Images Talk Back: Guiding MMDiTs by Suppressing Image-to-Text Feedback Yucheng Yang, Xiaoxiao Sun, Shusheng Yang, Jiakang Chen, Serena Yeung-Levy
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation Generative Modeling Time-Series LLM

Paper 2 / arXiv:2610.13941 Open arXiv
Relevance 9 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it analyzes and improves an MMDiT/VLLM-style generative pipeline via semantic feedback guidance, with surprising empirical findings about image-to-text feedback.

Multimodal diffusion transformers (MMDiTs) allow the evolving image to update internal text representations through joint attention. These representations then guide subsequent image updates, raising a question: does this feedback help correct deviations from the prompt, or preserve them? We find that image-to-text (I2T) feedback can preserve the objects and attributes already represented in the evolving image, including errors. Transplanting I2T messages can rescue failures or disrupt successes. At matched intervention strengths, suppressing the influence of I2T feedback improves generation, whereas opposite controls degrade it. These findings motivate Semantic Feedback Guidance (SFG), which suppresses the influence of I2T feedback by guiding away from an auxiliary branch that strengthens this feedback and weakens text-to-image conditioning. The in-place variant, iSFG, directly suppresses I2T feedback and strengthens text-to-image conditioning, requiring no extra model evaluations. Temporal interventions motivate running the SFG branch during the first 20% of image sampling steps, requiring about $1.2\times$ the base NFE without classifier-free guidance (CFG). Experiments across three image-generation backbones show gains in alignment and preference-model scores. On FLUX, adding SFG to CFG raises GenEval from $0.6661$ to $0.7479$; on SD3.5M, iSFG adds $+1.33$ HPSv2.1 over CFG at zero extra NFE. The control also transfers to text-to-video and image-to-video generation, improving VBench scores. Code is available at https://github.com/yyc1215lll-creator/SFG.

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: it is an embodied-agent paper on GUI tasks with a new dataset/benchmark and a reusable skill-induction method.

Computer-use agents can perform increasingly complex GUI tasks, yet unfamiliar software and workflows requiring human domain knowledge remain challenging. Human demonstrations convey procedural knowledge through the same GUI interaction space, but reuse requires interpreting actions in context and separating workflow rules from task-specific details. We thus introduce Work2Skill, a framework that captures human GUI experience for reusable agent skill induction. WorkRecorder provides the framework's infrastructure for collecting this experience, aligning screen recordings, mouse and keyboard events, and available accessibility-tree information. Using WorkRecorder, we construct WorkDemo-350, the first synchronized multimodal dataset of human desktop GUI demonstrations for reusable agent skill induction and downstream evaluation. Its 350 demonstrations are screened for completeness and task success. We then propose an agentic skill-induction method that inspects evidence on demand to produce reusable skills capturing workflow rules, step dependencies, and completion checks. When the same four execution models are equipped with skills induced by each method under shared execution settings, Work2Skill achieves the highest scores and success rates. Mean score improvements over the corresponding No Skill baseline are 4.67--10.54 points on MyPCBench Bounded Action and 3.11--7.50 points on a 286-task subset of OSWorld-Verified. Our code and data are available at https://github.com/china-qijizhifeng/Work2Skill.

13 From Glance to Focus: Mitigating Recognition Laziness in MLLMs for Fine-Grained Recognition Yutong Yang, Zhuqi Liu, Mouxing Yang, Xi Peng
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation LLM Interpretability Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv

Paper 13 / arXiv:2610.13154 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: it proposes a training-free method for improving MLLM fine-grained recognition by addressing an inference-time failure mode.

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) generally fall short of expectations in Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR), although they exhibit promising FGVR foundations, i.e., visual perception and broad category knowledge. Most existing studies seek to remedy this deficiency by adopting domain-specific post-training, at the risk of compromising generalization to unseen domains. Different from these efforts, in this paper, we revisit the FGVR mechanism underlying MLLMs from both the empirical and theoretical perspectives and uncover an inference-time phenomenon termed FGVR Laziness. In brief, FGVR Laziness refers to the insufficient association between visual cues and category concepts under certain candidate category lists and recognition guidance, leaving the FGVR foundations of MLLMs largely under-utilized. To mitigate FGVR Laziness, we propose Laziness-resIstant Visual rEcognition (LIVE), a training-free framework which jointly refines candidate lists and recognition guidance to elicit the latent FGVR capability of MLLMs. Specifically, LIVE first performs query-adaptive candidate inference to derive a compact yet reliable candidate list. Meanwhile, LIVE constructs category-specific FGVR skills by mining the intra-category characteristics and inter-category differences. Finally, LIVE selectively invokes detailed skill guidance according to recognition uncertainty and category confusion, shifting MLLM-based FGVR from a cursory glance to a focus on associating discriminative visual cues with category concepts. Extensive experiments on 13 benchmarks against 24 state-of-the-art methods spanning fine-grained category recognition, action recognition, and general category recognition, verify the effectiveness and generalizability of LIVE across different MLLM backbones. The code will be released upon acceptance.

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 partly: it leverages feed-forward vision priors and builds a new multimodal dataset/benchmark for large-scale reconstruction, though it is more 3D reconstruction than foundation-model application.

Feed-forward 3D priors enable high-quality reconstruction from image streams but often lack reliable metric scale and suffer from long-term trajectory drift. In contrast, LiDAR--inertial estimation provides metrically accurate motion but lacks the image-space consistency needed for high-fidelity reconstruction. We propose $MIRAGE$, which anchors feed-forward visual geometry to LiDAR--IMU metric motion and regulates online pose corrections according to correspondence support and cross-modal agreement, jointly enabling accurate metric pose estimation and high-fidelity Gaussian reconstruction. Experiments on Oxford Spires, M2DGR and INS show that MIRAGE achieves accurate metric trajectory estimation, outperforming COLMAP, a widely used but computationally expensive reference for vision-only benchmarks. Motivated by the scarcity of long-horizon monocular benchmarks for large-scale indoor reconstruction, we further build a multimodal data-collection platform and use MIRAGE to establish accurate 6-DoF reference trajectories for a new vision-only benchmark. We benchmark recent monocular large-scale reconstruction methods on this dataset, providing a systematic evaluation of their performance in long-horizon indoor scenes.

Why selected: No strong match to criteria 1-4; it is a multimodal-agent pipeline for video motion editing rather than embodied AI or a new VLLM benchmark.

Video editing models achieve strong performance in modifying appearance, backgrounds, styles, and motions, empowered by large-scale training data. Beyond general scenarios, professional motions are significant in performance, sports, and martial arts. This paper focuses on professional motion editing, which involves fine-grained motion technique and temporal structure. Given professional motion names but lacking detailed descriptions, existing models often fail to generate professional motions, such as incorrectly lifting the front wheel under a Stoppie instruction. To address this challenge, we introduce MotionCoach, a trained multimodal agent that uses multi-turn tools and performs self-verification, producing a grounded prompt and a reference clip as reliable and sufficient multimodal guidance to inject external professional motion knowledge into video editing models. To supply training data, we build a data pipeline to construct trajectories. We cold-start the agent on MotionCoach-SFT-10k and apply Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) on MotionCoach-RL-2k with rewards on the motion guidance prompt, the motion outcome video, and the format. For evaluation, we introduce VPMEBench, comprising 314 professional motion editing tasks with fine-grained references and annotations. Extensive experiments show that MotionCoach improves the overall score from 55.1 to 65.6 on Seedance 2.0 and from 51.2 to 64.5 on MiniMax-H3. Learning to construct multimodal guidance enables precise professional motion editing without retraining video editing models.

3 SpatialASM: Internalizing Active Mentalization for Spatial Reasoning Yuhong Liu, Shenxi Wu, Junyi Chi, Beichen Zhang, Xuanlang Dai, Jiaqi Wang, Yuhang Zang, Dahua Lin

Paper 3 / arXiv:2610.13832 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 8

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 closely: it targets spatial reasoning in LVLMs by internalizing active spatial mentalization and viewpoint-based scene reconstruction.

When solving complex spatial reasoning tasks, ordinary humans naturally revisit the scenes from task-relevant viewpoints and mentally reconstruct the spatial layouts from those viewpoints. But can Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) proactively think in the same way? We term this ability Active Spatial Mentalization (ASM), an intrinsic component of visual intelligence that remains substantially underdeveloped in current LVLMs. To bridge this gap, we introduce SpatialASM, a scalable framework to internalize ASM into LVLMs through training-time spatial simulation. SpatialASM employs an allocentric novel-view generator to ground hypothetical viewpoints in visual evidence and produces two complementary forms of supervision: i) explicit ASM trajectories that teach LVLMs when and how to adaptively invoke view planning and imagination during spatial reasoning, and ii) automatically-constructed perspective-taking tasks that further strengthens viewpoint-conditioned scene imagination. On seven benchmarks spanning relatively perception-oriented spatial understanding to more challenging tasks requiring multi-image, cross-view and viewpoint-transformation reasoning, SpatialASM yields performance gain on LVLMs of four LVLM backbones in spatial reasoning while retaining their original general vision capabilities. The experimental results demonstrate that our approach improves average accuracy across the seven spatial benchmarks. And the synthesis of ASM trajectories provides a scalable pathway to alleviate the scarcity of spatial Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision for eliciting active viewpoint exploration and mental scene simulation.

6 InstantFusion: A Shared Latent Interface for Heterogeneous Image Generators Xuanlang Dai, Jiazi Bu, Yujie Zhou, Yuhong Liu, Beichen Zhang, Penghui Yang, Ziyu Liu, Yuhang Zang
Vision-Language Reasoning Generative Modeling LLM Interpretability
ArXiv
15
Paper 6 / arXiv:2610.13944 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 8

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: a new latent interface for heterogeneous image generators, with strong relevance to generative modeling in vision foundation models.

Diffusion/flow models have emerged as the de facto paradigms for image generation, giving rise to a diverse family of modern generators with increasingly heterogeneous architectures and representations. Despite their remarkable generation capabilities, the cross-model alignment of latent representations and the consistency of their underlying denoising dynamics remain underexplored. In this work, we uncover an intriguing cross-model compatibility: independently trained generators exhibit similar latent spatial organization, while directly handing off intermediate states between unaligned models can still preserve coarse visual content. Motivated by these observations, we propose InstantFusion, a lightweight shared latent interface that connects the denoising trajectories of heterogeneous image generators. Specifically, to enable flexible cross-model state translation, model-specific Latent AutoEncoders (LAEs) are introduced to project intermediate states from different generators into a shared latent space and decode them into the latent space of a target generator. This shared space is constrained by two complementary objectives: aligning intermediate states derived from the same image at matched noise levels and synchronizing their corresponding local denoising velocities. Once trained, InstantFusion allows denoising to seamlessly transition across frozen generators without requiring a dedicated mapping for every model pair. The versatility of InstantFusion is demonstrated across acceleration, multi-preference composition, cross-architecture on-policy distillation, and cross-model composition, achieving competitive performance across these diverse settings.

8 Feature Competition and Its Representational Origins in Vision Language Models: Evidence from the Stroop Paradigm Nurb\"uke Teker, Rui Xiao, Zeynep Akata, Shuchen Wu
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation Generative Modeling Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
13
Paper 8 / arXiv:2610.12650 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it studies vision-language model behavior and representation through a classic Stroop-style benchmark.

When you ask a vision-language model (VLM) a question with an image, it answers your question based on the image. But to act means to choose: saying one thing means choosing to respond not say another. This becomes an issue when the image contains multiple features that equally relate to the question at hand: how would VLMs choose to respond? This problem is precisely studied by the classical Stroop task in psychology: among a set of deliberately designed images whose text and color conflicts (e.g., \textcolor{blue}{RED}), participants are instructed to report the color. We adapt this psychological paradigm to a benchmark and prompted a range of contrastive and generative VLMs on their behavior. Across models, we observe a persistent bias toward the feature of text in Stroop images: when text and color disagree, models preferentially generate responses centered to the text rather than color. We then opened the black box and examined the source of this bias and why certain features influences the model's behavior more than others. In the embedding space, we found that textual cues are represented more strongly and separably than color cues. This representational asymmetry predicts intervention success: steering models toward text-related directions is substantially easier than steering them toward color-related directions. Using a classical psychological paradigm, we demonstrate, at both behavioral and representational levels, that distinct features within the same image can exert unequal influence on VLM behavior. Our findings suggest that VLMs assign different degrees of saliency to different features within the same image, highlighting the need to understand how cue-specific saliency shapes their performance in widely used downstream applications.

9 NOFACE3D: NOise-scheduled Focused Anchoring and Controlled Enhancement for 3D Generation James Rose, Jingwen Liang, Wenchao Ma, Oren Jacob, Haomiao Jiang
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation Generative Modeling LLM Interpretability

Paper 9 / arXiv:2610.13050 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: it introduces a new benchmark for detail-critical 3D assets and a training-free method for improving 3D generation at inference time.

Recent image-to-3D models generate complete, high-quality assets from a single image, with global shape and appearance that are already convincing; what remains is the fine scale, where small, thin, elongated, or detail-rich regions are reconstructed softly and under-resolved. This gap can arise in generators with fixed-resolution spatial grids: the same spatial sampling density is used for a large, smooth, low-curvature surface and for a thin, high-curvature structure that carries most of the perceived detail. To this end, we introduce NOFACE3D, a training-free framework that refines these detail-critical regions while preserving the rest of the asset. Given a generated mesh, its input image, and a refinement box, NOFACE3D extracts the selected submesh, scales it up to fill the generator's input volume, and re-encodes it into the full-resolution latent grid. Conditioned on the image crop, it refines the region through flow-based sampling with a spatially varying noise schedule that anchors boundary latents. The refined region is then integrated back into the original asset, preserving the surrounding geometry and appearance. We implement NOFACE3D on TRELLIS.2 and its sparse voxel representation; all of it operates purely at inference time on the frozen pretrained generator. We evaluate NOFACE3D on PAniC-3D, TexVerse and BigDetail, a new benchmark of 339 assets with annotated detail-critical regions. On all three, it generates sharper geometry and finer texture detail than the baselines. Project page: https://idkcouscous.github.io/NoFACE3D/

10 When MLLMs miss what humans read: diagnosing and learning to read squint text Chen Peng, Shan Huang, Jianbo Huang, Linrui Qin, Qibo Sun, Mengwei Xu
Vision-Language Reasoning Multimodal LLM Medical AI LLM Interpretability
ArXiv

Paper 10 / arXiv:2610.13465 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: it diagnoses a specific MLLM reading failure on embedded text and proposes a training method to improve it.

Multimodal LLMs can fail to read text embedded in an image's coarse structure (text a human recovers by squinting) and report that no text is present. We name this class Squint Text and study it as a recognition deficiency, motivated by cases from a production moderation pipeline. We introduce SquintBench, comprising 1076 production-derived moderation images, and SquintCtrl, a controlled synthetic suite for mechanism diagnosis. Frozen-weight analysis shows that hidden text leaves a coarse signal while glyph identity is weakly represented; identity becomes substantially more accessible when the correct spatial regions are supplied, implicating spatial selection as a major bottleneck. Suppressing high-frequency content also improves reading at a fixed visual-token budget, while visual token count may also contribute. These findings motivate Squint OPSD: a frozen teacher receives a downscaled view and the ground-truth transcription as privileged context, and its token-level distributions supervise a student along on-policy trajectories. At inference, the student reads the original image alone. Squint OPSD reaches 88.61 ANLS on SquintBench, versus 26.50 for the unadapted backbone, 24.44 for the best general-purpose model on the original view, and 68.60 at best for an off-the-shelf model under a downscaled view. These results substantially narrow the gap between available visual evidence and its generated read-out without auxiliary views at inference.

11 ThinkDrag: Semantic Drag-Based Image Editing with Visual Reasoning Armando Fortes, Tianyi Wei, Chindanai Trakantannarong, Shangchen Zhou, Xingang Pan

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: it is a multimodal image-editing framework that uses visual reasoning and adds a new benchmark for drag-based editing.

Drag-based image editing provides an intuitive interface for spatially controlled image manipulation by allowing users to specify handle and target points. Existing methods have made substantial progress through optimization-based, one-step, and feed-forward formulations, but they often interpret drag instructions as geometric displacements. This limits their effectiveness when the desired edit depends on interpreting image content and inferring a plausible semantic transformation from sparse point constraints. We introduce ThinkDrag, a unified multimodal framework for drag-based image editing with visual reasoning. ThinkDrag is trained to associate point constraints with meaningful object- and scene-level transformations, and can optionally produce an explicit reasoning trace that interprets the intended edit before image generation. This reasoning trace improves interpretability, particularly for ambiguous drags. Drag Endpoint Markers, motivated by an analysis of cross-image attention, provide an explicit correspondence signal that grounds each drag in image space. To support this framework, we construct a supervised dataset of semantic drag transformations paired with reasoning traces and introduce DragBench++, a benchmark targeting challenging drag-based editing scenarios with reference edit solutions. Experiments show that ThinkDrag achieves state-of-the-art performance, improving generation quality and plausibility while maintaining competitive point-following precision.

14 Learning Holistic Scene Layout against View Shifts in Cross-View Geo-Localization Chao Yang, Songlian Li, Zhigang Tu
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation LLM Interpretability

Paper 14 / arXiv:2610.13553 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 moderately: it targets spatial context and layout reasoning for cross-view geo-localization, a spatial understanding problem, though not embodied agents.

Cross-view geo-localization remains challenging due to drastic appearance variations and geometric distortions caused by viewpoint changes. Existing methods primarily rely on discriminative feature learning or explicit geometric alignment, yet often overlook an important observation: humans tend to match scenes by integrating the spatial context of multiple landmarks rather than relying solely on isolated local visual cues. Inspired by this, we propose a Spatial Context Modeling (SCM) module, which traverses feature maps along four orthogonal directions and models interactions among the resulting directional representations at each spatial position. By aggregating complementary directional observations, SCM constructs richer scene layout representations. Since reliable spatial context depends on robust visual features, we introduce a High-Frequency Information Guidance (HFIG) module that extracts stable structural cues from the frequency domain. By emphasizing edges and contours that are less sensitive to appearance variations, HFIG encourages the model to learn more robust representations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our method achieves competitive performance on standard cross-view geo-localization benchmarks while exhibiting strong robustness under more challenging scenarios with unknown orientations and limited fields of view (FoVs).

16 Shortcut Learning in Geospatial Foundation Model Embeddings for Cross-Disaster Damage Assessment Ali Shibli, Andrea Nascetti, Yifang Ban

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: it evaluates vision foundation model embeddings for geospatial damage assessment and exposes shortcut learning/generalization issues.

Geospatial foundation models can support disaster damage assessment, but high accuracy on familiar disasters may hide shortcut learning: prediction from event or scene characteristics rather than evidence that transfers to a new disaster. We study embeddings from TerraMind, Prithvi-EO-2.0, DOFA, and DINOv3 on the multi-disaster datasets xView2 and BRIGHT. Holding out an entire disaster, instead of randomly splitting image tiles, reduces mean balanced accuracy from 0.807 to 0.638 on xView2 and from 0.818 to 0.568 on BRIGHT. Four sets of tests examine what information supports prediction: event and acquisition context, before/after image inputs, building count, and correct image pairing. Pre-event images remain predictive, and a classifier given only the annotated number of buildings reaches balanced accuracy of 0.629 and 0.653. Combining before/after features performs best on average on xView2, but does not improve over pre-only features on BRIGHT. Shuffling image pairs within the same disaster and damage class causes modest losses. Motivated by these findings, we test whether feature transformations learned only from training disasters improve generalization to unseen disasters. These transformations improve performance, with supervised contrastive learning performing best on xView2 and PCA on BRIGHT. Our results highlight the importance of evaluation on unseen domains for assessing model robustness and show that simple feature-level transformations can improve generalization. Code and experiments can be found at https://github.com/alishibli97/gfm-shortcut-learning.

21 CoVA: Composing VAE Representations for Generative Learning Zhaowei Chen, Yikai Ge, Linze Li, Yao Tang, Xiang Li

Why selected: No very close match to the listed criteria; this is a generative modeling paper about composing VAE representations, relevant to vision foundation models only in a general sense.

Representation alignment with pretrained visual encoders improves diffusion transformer training but requires auxiliary encoders and extra training overhead. Recent approaches instead exploit representations from the generative model's own variational autoencoder (VAE). However, reconstruction-oriented VAE features are not optimized for the semantic discrimination and spatial structure needed for generative learning. We introduce \textbf{CoVA} (\textbf{Co}mposed \textbf{VA}E Representations), a lightweight framework that composes VAE features online into stage-wise guidance for flow-matching training. A small latent composer is jointly trained with class supervision, masked latent reconstruction, and horizontal-flip consistency, yielding semantically discriminative, spatially structured dense features and sample-dependent class posteriors. CoVA aligns intermediate SiT features with the composed representations early in training, then removes the increasingly detrimental alignment and reuses detached composer posteriors for sample-dependent soft label conditioning. We further uncover a hidden confound in soft label conditioning: averaging class embeddings attenuates their magnitude, coupling semantic interpolation with conditioning strength. This attenuation alone can substantially improve FID while degrading Recall, revealing a non-semantic source of the apparent gain. CoVA corrects this confound by restoring the hard-label magnitude, isolating posterior-dependent directional semantics. CoVA adds only 8.5M training-only auxiliary parameters (1.3\% of SiT-XL/2) and introduces no inference-time overhead. On ImageNet 256$\times$256, CoVA reduces SiT-XL/2 FID from 17.2 to 7.4 (\textbf{57\%}) in 400K steps. With classifier-free guidance, it achieves an FID of \textbf{2.03} in only 400 epochs, versus 2.06 after 1,400 epochs for vanilla SiT-XL/2.

Modeling human reactions to spatial audio requires understanding both what is heard and where it comes from. Prior work generates motion offline from complete acoustic and spatial input sequences. Humans, however, react to sounds without knowing what they will hear next. We present Sonic-AMG, to our knowledge the first framework for streaming human motion generation from spatial audio. The model encodes acoustic content and body-relative source geometry as separate tokens. Conditioned on these tokens, a causal transformer with a flow head generates continuous motion latents, from which a causal decoder reconstructs motion segments. Standard teacher-forced training derives motion histories and body-relative source locations from ground-truth motion, whereas streaming inference relies on generated motion for both, resulting in a training-inference mismatch. On SAM, Sonic-AMG achieves state-of-the-art FID and R-precision. It further supports real-time online generation of coherent reactions across sound events and source locations. 25 SplatQ: Interaction-Aware Pruning of 3D Gaussian Splatting via Quadratic Selection Hongbin Ye, Haixu Liu, Shenwei Kang, Wenxiu Wei, Tinglan Wang, Hongdong Zhu, Fan Tang, Qi Gao, Yin Ma, Kai Wen, Hai Wei, Nuo Qian, Wei-Shi Zheng, Shaobo Chen, Fa-Ting Hong, Jingke Meng

Paper 25 / arXiv:2610.13503 Open arXiv

Why selected: No direct match to the listed criteria; it is a 3D Gaussian Splatting pruning method, though it may interest readers who like clever optimization/statistical tricks.

Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) pruning methods primarily rely on global Top-K selection based on independent importance scores, lacking regional budget control and ignoring joint deletion effects. Consequently, they cannot ensure balanced reconstruction quality across a scene. We propose SplatQ, a combinatorial pruning algorithm that coordinates regional budget allocation and joint Gaussian selection under a fixed budget. SplatQ partitions Gaussians by spatial proximity and appearance similarity into size-limited blocks, decomposing global selection into subproblems of a specific size. It then smooths importance-based block retention quotas to prevent excessive budget concentration. Finally, it constructs a quadratic approximation of joint deletion effects using the responses of representative pixel colors to changes in Gaussian opacity. Individual responses, pairwise interactions, and quota penalties are incorporated into a quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) objective, solved using simulated annealing (SA) or a coherent Ising machine (CIM) to jointly select Gaussians. Experiments on 5 benchmarks demonstrate that SplatQ outperforms state-of-the-art pruning methods in aggregate reconstruction quality, spatial quality balance, and rendering speed. Ablations confirm that budget smoothing improves local detail reconstruction, while interaction modeling improves Gaussian selection quality under a fixed budget.

Why selected: No very close match to the listed criteria; it is a context-aware re-identification method rather than embodied AI, VLLMs, or foundation models.

Person re-identification is typically addressed by learning identity-discriminative embeddings from individual person crops. However, player re-identification in broadcast sports videos presents a unique challenge: players from the same team often share similar appearances, while video sequences provide repeated observations of the same players under different viewpoints and surrounding contexts. Existing crop-based approaches largely ignore this contextual information, which can be critical for resolving visually similar identities. Here, we propose ContextReID, a contextualized embedding framework that represents a player by jointly considering other players in the same frame and observations from different frames of the broadcast sequence. By incorporating both co-occurring players and cross-frame observations, the model learns identity features relative to the surrounding visual context rather than relying only on isolated appearance cues.
