1 ControlFlow3D: Distilling Multi-View Knowledge into Latent Flow Matching for Point Cloud Upsampling Yuang Liu, Zhi Zuo, Zhengkai Zhao, Lirui Zhang, Pan Gao, Hao Feng, Zhengzhe Liu
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation Generative Modeling LLM Interpretability

14
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.21190 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: it uses pretrained foundation models to distill multi-view knowledge for 3D point cloud upsampling and introduces a new multi-modal benchmark.

Point cloud upsampling is essential for 3D applications but remains challenging due to sparse, under-constrained observations. Multi-view images can resolve geometric ambiguity, but existing multi-modal methods do not fully exploit visual cues. We propose ControlFlow3D, a framework that distills knowledge from pretrained foundation models into a lightweight generation network during training, enabling strong performance without multi-view images available at test time. Specifically, our method performs conditional flow matching in a structured latent space defined by a frozen 3D encoder. Multi-view image tokens and 3D latent tokens are jointly clustered into shared prototypes via unsupervised soft KMeans; the resulting prototypes guide the flow trajectory through velocity control and are aligned by a consistency loss that progressively transfers image knowledge into the base network. A Mamba-based geometry decoder then maps latent features to dense coordinates with linear complexity. We also construct ShapeNetPU, a 35K-object multi-modal benchmark that is 31 times larger than prior datasets. Extensive experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of our method and its strong generalization capability.

2 CART: Compatible Routing for Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning Ling-Xiang Zhang, Tao Hu, Da-Wei Zhou
Multimodal LLM Medical AI
ArXiv
11
Paper 2 / arXiv:2610.22137 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: it proposes a method for multimodal continual instruction tuning of MLLMs, with a new routing strategy for projector/LM coordination.

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) achieve strong performance through instruction tuning, yet real-world applications often require them to continually acquire new capabilities from sequential tasks, i.e., Multimodal Continual Instruction Tuning (MCIT). In typical MLLMs, a projector is employed to align visual representations with the language model. However, existing methods primarily protect the language model from forgetting while continuously updating the projector across tasks. Such asymmetric adaptation can cause substantial representation drift in the projector, increasing the mismatch between the projector and language model and consequently exacerbating catastrophic forgetting. To address this issue, we propose Compatible Routing (CART), which explicitly coordinates the adaptation of the projector and language model. Specifically, CART maintains task-specific experts for both modules and combines them through weighted mixtures. CART jointly infers their weights by considering both expert relevance and cross-module compatibility, thereby promoting coordinated adaptation between the two modules. To further control model growth, we introduce relevance-guided expert consolidation, which merges experts exhibiting similar relevance profiles across samples within each module. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CART achieves state-of-the-art performance.

3 Mind the Tail: Data Selection Through Sparse Concepts Ziheng Zhang, Neha Hulkund, Colin Lee, Zheda Mai, Hui Shen, Ying Ding, Tanya Berger-Wolf, Sara Beery, Wei-Lun Chao, Jianyang Gu
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation
ArXiv

Paper 3 / arXiv:2610.21007 Open arXiv
Relevance 4 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 moderately well: it uses sparse autoencoders for concept-based data selection in multimodal pretraining and studies vision concept support.

Data selection reallocates training support across visual concepts, including objects, attributes, color, texture, and backgrounds. Different allocation strategies may produce varied downstream model behavior, especially for tail concepts supported by only a few samples. Discarding them can hurt test-time robustness, while overemphasizing samples lying on category boundaries may introduce unstable training signals. This work explicitly investigates this relationship across fine-grained, spurious-correlation, and general image classification, as well as multimodal pretraining. Specifically, we use sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to extract concept activations from individual images. During selection, we accumulate the dataset-level concept support for each added sample. Applying diminishing returns to this distribution down-weights well-represented concepts and shifts the focus to the distribution tail. We then incorporate local support to find samples that reliably strengthen under-supported concepts. Empirical results show consistent improvements over strong data selection baselines across all benchmarks. We also reveal the connection between concepts our method enhances and the induced behavioral changes, including improved robustness against out-of-distribution and spurious contexts. This work establishes concept support, beyond individual sample importance, as a useful principle for data-efficient and robust learning.

4 Beyond Generation: Using Multimodal LLMs as Detectors of LLM-Generated Deepfake Facial Images Olivia Salamon, Virginia N. L. Franqueira, Shujun Li
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation Generative Modeling Safety & Backdoors

Paper 4 / arXiv:2610.21760 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it studies MLLMs as detectors of LLM-generated deepfake facial images and evaluates their forensic behavior.

As image generation models become increasingly capable of producing highly realistic content, traditional forensic techniques are struggling to keep up. This paper investigates whether MLLMs can instead be used as a form of forensic analysis by identifying synthetic media. Specifically, the study aims to determine whether these models detect fake images by recognising physical inconsistencies, or whether they rely on digital artefacts such as embedded watermarks.Six MLLMs: GPT-4o, GPT-5, GPT-5.2, Gemini 2.5, Gemini 3, and Seed 1.6, are benchmarked using a dataset of 400 images. Fifty images are authentic photographs, while the remaining 350 are generated using seven leading image models: Gemini 3, Gemini 2.5, Flux.2 Pro, GPT-Image-1, GPT-Image-1.5, Seedream 4.0, and Seedream 4.5. The evaluation is structured in three stages: single-image classification, pairwise comparison, and a focused "self-detection" experiment on Gemini. Several MLLMs achieved a high detection accuracy, with GPT-5.2 reaching a peak of 97.9% in the pairwise experiment, against specific generators and an overall mean pairwise accuracy of 82.5% by identifying structural or physical inconsistencies within images. However, results from the self-detection experiment indicate that the presence of invisible SynthID watermarks leads to a 10% reduction in Gemini's detection accuracy compared to the clean control group. One possible explanation is that SynthID embedding reduces some of the visible artefacts the models rely on for detection.These results imply that MLLMs can detect structural artefacts without relying on watermarks, but performance varies substantially across model generations. The findings from comparison C5 also suggest that SynthID embedding may suppress some of the structural artefacts used during detection.

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 only partially: it is a new benchmark for real-time interactive agents with temporal constraints, but it is not specifically embodied AI or spatial understanding.

Real-time interaction requires agents to perceive evolving environments and act under temporal constraints, yet existing evaluations of real-time streaming models focus on conversational quality and streaming perception, leaving effective action under strict temporal constraints untested. We introduce Realtime-Bench, a suite of carefully curated dynamic tasks where delayed actions directly affect success. Under Wall Clock, where environments advance according to actual inference latency, the best success rate reaches only 44% and the best Pass^3 is only 36%, demonstrating that real-time interaction remains challenging. To separate serving latency from models' real-time streaming capability, we compare Wall Clock with Paused Clock, which removes inference delays as an idealized reference, and introduce Token Clock, which advances simulated time according to generated token counts, testing temporal awareness independently of physical serving speed. We find that temporal costs limit success in evolving environments and that higher reasoning effort has mixed effects under time pressure, reflecting a trade-off between decision quality and timely execution. These findings suggest directions for improving real-time multimodal agents, including adaptive reasoning with temporal awareness and continuous perception and action.

Why selected: No close match to the listed criteria; this is a review and empirical overview of RL-driven diffusion models rather than a new VLLM/MLLM, embodied benchmark, or vision foundation model application.

Diffusion models (DMs) have steadily demonstrated outstanding performance in high-fidelity, controllable generation across diverse scenarios, following the "pretraining-finetuning" paradigm established in LLMs. Despite these advances, conventional DMs still face critical limitations in sophisticated reasoning, autonomous exploration of generation pathways, and adaptability to complex, human-aligned objectives. Inspired by RLHF's success in LLMs, RL has recently emerged as a pivotal post-training mechanism to iteratively refine DMs through reward-driven trajectory optimization. This paper presents the first comprehensive review of RL-driven DMs and an empirical analysis of how RL reshapes the diffusion process towards experience-centric, goal-directed generation. We propose a novel technical panorama elucidating RL's multifaceted roles in diffusion-based language, multimodal, robotic, and scientific models, with a granular focus on diffusion trajectory optimization and reward engineering. Through extensive experiments on multiple task-specific benchmarks, we systematically evaluate representative methods to intuitively highlight the advantages of different RL post-training strategies. Furthermore, we reveal and dissect three crucial bottlenecks that currently hinder the real-world deployment of RL-driven DMs: reward design fragility, exploration-exploitation trade-off in high-dimensional generation spaces, and training instability under sparse rewards. Based on these perspectives, we provide a principled roadmap for the integration of RL into DMs and outline promising future research directions for robust, interpretable RL-diffusion systems. We also construct a public repository to continuously track the latest developments in this rapidly evolving field: https://github.com/MAIR-Lab-HUST/Awesome-RL-for-Diffusion-Models.

6 FDE-Gym: The Last Mile of AI Delivery Dongxu Yang, Hongda Yan, Renxin Wang, Baoli Zhang, Haojia Zuo, Ying Zhao, Youmin Chen, Xiaojian Liao, Congming Gao, Tianyang Jiang, Yixiao Chen, Qing Wang, Zhiguo Yu, Xiaqing Li, Yuhao Zhang, Jiawen Sun, Renzhe Xu, De Mi, Tiancheng Wu, Xianya Fu, Xuanlu Zhao, Bo Feng, Meixuan Ren, Guanming Lin, Ziheng Zhou, Xinhua Wang

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 only loosely: it is a new benchmark with simulated customer environments and interactive agents, but it is not embodied AI or spatially grounded.

Existing agent benchmarks typically evaluate agents on well-specified tasks defined by issue reports, specifications, or task tickets. Real-world customer delivery is different: forward-deployed engineers (FDEs) must clarify ambiguous requirements, work in live systems, and uncover unstated constraints. We introduce FDE-Gym, a benchmark of 95 end-to-end delivery tasks from 20 customer scenarios across 18 sectors, each adapted by industry experts from real-world delivery projects. In simulated customer environments with scripted stakeholders and modifiable customer systems, agents interact with stakeholders and operate on production-like systems to deliver working solutions. FDE-Gym scores a delivery by how much it improves the customer's own success metric, normalized between 0 (unchanged system) and 1 (reference solution), with penalties for stakeholder interactions (messages, trial runs, and waiting time). An online platform lets humans practice FDE skills under the same grading criteria. We evaluate 13 model configurations from seven providers. In the realistic setting, where unstated constraints must be discovered, the best configuration achieves a mean FDE-Gym score of 0.56 and resolves only 14 of the 95 tasks (a score of at least 0.8 with no incident). Discovering unstated constraints is the primary bottleneck: performance drops sharply when models must uncover them without explicit guidance. Yet information discovery alone does not account for the gap: even on the 23 tasks where the best configuration obtains every key fact from stakeholders, it reaches a normalized business uplift of 0.8 or above on only 10. These results reveal a substantial gap between solving well-specified tasks and delivering effective AI systems for real customers.

7 Progress Reward Model for Reinforcement Learning via Large Language Models Xiuhui Zhang, Ning Gao, Xingyu Jiang, Yihui Chen, Yuheng Pan, Mohan Zhang, Yue Deng

Why selected: No close match to the listed criteria; this is RL reward shaping for robotics control, but the abstract does not emphasize a novel embodied benchmark or spatial intelligence angle.

Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms face significant limitations in handling long-term tasks with sparse rewards. Recent advancements have leveraged large language models (LLMs) to enhance RL by utilizing their world knowledge for task planning and reward generation. However, planning-based approaches often depend on pre-defined skill libraries and fail to optimize low-level control policies, while reward-based methods require extensive human feedback or exhaustive searching due to the complexity of tasks. In this paper, we propose the Progress Reward Model for RL (PRM4RL), a novel framework that integrates task planning and dense reward to enhance RL. For high-level planning, a complex task is decomposed into a series of simple manageable subtasks, with a subtask-oriented, fine-grained progress function designed to monitor task execution progress. For low-level reward generation, inspired by potential-based reward shaping, we use the progress function to construct a Progress Reward Model (PRM), providing theoretically grounded optimality and convergence guarantees, thereby enabling effective policy optimization. Experimental results on robotics control tasks demonstrate that our approach outperforms both LLM-based planning and reward methods, achieving state-of-the-art performance. Code is available at https://github.com/deng-ai-lab/PRM4RL.
