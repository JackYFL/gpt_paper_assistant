1 3D-SceneQ: Empowering 3D LLM With Query-Guided Adaptive Pruning and Multi-Modal Feature Enhancement Yongqi Shan, Lu Zhang, Jiazuo Yu, Yunzhi Zhuge, Huchuan Lu
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation LLM Interpretability
ArXiv
17
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.37202 Open arXiv
Relevance 9 Novelty 8

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 and 2 closely: it is a new 3D scene understanding method for a 3D LLM with query-guided pruning and multimodal feature enhancement, directly aimed at spatial reasoning.

Current 3D scene understanding pipelines typically concatenate vast numbers of 3D object tokens with text tokens and feed the resulting sequence to a Large Language Model (LLM). However, existing 3D-LLMs for scene understanding encounter two major limitations: (i) excessive inclusion of task-irrelevant object data, introducing noise that reduces reasoning accuracy and increases hallucinations; and (ii) reliance solely on point cloud data, which inherently lacks the rich semantic information available in complementary 2D modalities, such as color, material properties, texture, and high-level contextual relationships. To address these challenges, we introduce 3D-SceneQ, a 3D LLM that unifies adaptive token pruning with multimodal semantic enrichment, markedly advancing scene understanding, reasoning, and grounding. Specifically, we propose a Query-Guided Adaptive Pruning (QGAP) module that adaptively selects task-relevant objects based on the intended meaning of user instructions, leveraging a learnable latent query to retain critical information while effectively suppressing irrelevant noise. In addition, we introduce a Multi-modal Object-level Feature Enhancement (MOFE) module, which integrates 2D feature embeddings from pre-trained models into 3D representation to enrich object-level semantic information. Evaluations show that 3D-SceneQ outperforms current state-of-the-art methods across multiple benchmarks (e.g., +5.7 CIDEr on Scan2Cap and +17.0 EM@1 on SQA3D), demonstrating its remarkable capabilities in various 3D language reasoning tasks.

9 Who Knows What - and What Does It Support? Perspective-Bounded Evidence Calibration for Multimodal Theory of Mind Ziqi Liao, Jiaqi Shen, Yiming Na, Kedi Lyu, Shao-Yuan Lo, Hongxia Xie
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation Embodied Interaction LLM Interpretability
ArXiv
11
Paper 9 / arXiv:2610.36824 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2: it is a multimodal theory-of-mind system built on top of a VLLM/MLLM-style backbone (GPT-4o) with a new evidence-calibration method for multimodal reasoning.

Theory of Mind (ToM), the ability to infer others' beliefs, goals, and intentions, is a cornerstone of social intelligence. Multimodal ToM requires inferring an agent's mental state from evidence that is available to that agent, rather than from the complete episode observed by the solver. Existing LLM-based and Bayesian model-based approaches reconstruct global event traces, yet they do not explicitly distinguish objective events from agent-admissible evidence during hypothesis evaluation. Consequently, agent-inaccessible events or semantically misaligned actions and utterances may influence the inferred belief, goal, or social intention, leading to perspective leakage and source or route confusion. We formulate this limitation as an evidence-admissibility problem: evidence should contribute to a mental-state hypothesis only when it is accessible to the relevant agent, preserves its information source, and is diagnostic of the queried mental-state variable. To operationalize this principle, we propose PIP-ToM, an evidence-admissibility interface that constructs agent-specific evidence stores, aligns them with structured mental-state claims, and calibrates model-based hypothesis scores while preserving the underlying Bayesian inference process. PIP-ToM with GPT-4o outperforms existing ToM methods across MuMA-ToM, MMToM-QA, and Hi-ToM, achieving average accuracies of 92.56%, 95.00%, and 95.00%, respectively.

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: this is a new embodied/world-model method for free-form interactive world generation with multi-turn instruction following and camera control.

Interactive world generation requires models to follow successive commands while preserving relevant history and action consequences. We present Hy-Nomas, a video world model with an 8B diffusion backbone that supports free-form text instructions and continuous camera control across multiple interaction turns. It generates variable-length segments conditioned on current inputs and cached history. Our central design aligns structured video captions with action-aware causal attention: descriptive context remains accessible across segments, while action instructions are restricted to their intended temporal windows. A vision-language Prompt Enhancer (PE) translates user requests and multimodal interaction histories into structured conditions for the next segment. A multi-stage curriculum progressively introduces causal generation, text interaction, camera control, and training on model-generated histories, followed by diffusion distillation. In human evaluation, Hy-Nomas shows superior performance over existing world models. We release model weights, code and benchmarks, together with a lightweight adaptation workflow, to support reproducible research on interactive world generation.

3 LearnPrunerV2: Learnable Progressive Token Pruning for Vision-Language Models Rinyoichi Takezoe, Yaqian Li, Weiren Wang, Zihao Bo, Huihong Shi, Anzhou Hou, Mo Guang, Jiale Yan, Kaiwen Long
Vision-Language Reasoning Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation
ArXiv
15
Paper 3 / arXiv:2610.36986 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: a new method for Vision-Language Models focused on progressive token pruning and efficiency.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated remarkable multimodal capabilities but suffer from prohibitive computational overhead due to the massive number of visual tokens. While token pruning is a promising solution, existing methods still suffer from two major drawbacks: 1) unreliable pruning criteria that fail to accurately localize task-specific regions due to inherent attention biases or query-agnostic designs, and 2) suboptimal pruning strategies that employ rigid, non-adaptive schedules, overlooking the dynamic evolution of token redundancy across different model depths. To address these challenges, we propose LearnPrunerV2, which extends the previous two-stage heuristic framework (LearnPruner) into a fully learnable architecture. Specifically, we integrate lightweight learnable modules into every layer of the VLM backbone, which estimate token importance by fusing intrinsic visual saliency with extrinsic query relevance captured via a shadow token. By uncovering a consistent staircase redundancy pattern across VLM layers, we further introduce Budget-Deterministic Pruning (BDP) to establish a hardware-friendly schedule with fixed per-layer budgets. Extensive experimental results across various benchmarks and VLMs demonstrate that LearnPrunerV2 consistently outperform previous state-of-the-art methods. Notably, LearnPrunerV2 preserves 99.8% of the original performance when pruning 77.8% of visual tokens on LLaVA-v1.5, achieving a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

5 MVRCap: Towards Comprehensive Video Captioning via Hierarchical Multi-Dimensional Verifiable Rewards Weihuang Lin, Xianing Chen, Zhibin Wang, Congzhi Zhang, Yiwei Ma, Jiayi Ji, Miao Chen, Jun Song, YuCheng, Bo Zheng, Xiaoshuai Sun, Rongrong Ji
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation LLM Interpretability Time-Series LLM

Paper 5 / arXiv:2610.36838 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: a vision foundation-model-style video captioning system with reward-based fine-grained supervision and strong application to video understanding.

Despite significant progress, current models struggle to generate comprehensive video captions, constrained primarily by coarse-grained supervision, which often fails to capture spatial-temporal dynamics and the underlying logical intent. To address this issue, we introduce MVRCap, a novel framework that reformulates video captioning as a reinforcement learning task steered by structured verification. Central to our approach is a Fine-Grained Verifiable Reward mechanism, serving as dense supervision to enforce rigorous visual-semantic alignment. Specifically, we construct this reward system anchored by three complementary pillars: a Hierarchical Concept Coverage Reward utilizing our newly constructed MVRCap-QA dataset to ensure multi-dimensional completeness and semantic depth, a Factuality Verification Reward to enhance factual consistency, and a Conciseness Constraint to maximize semantic density. Furthermore, we curate the high-quality MVRCap-SFT dataset, providing structured annotations across six key dimensions, to serve as a robust foundational initialization for the RL policy in this training pipeline. Extensive experiments demonstrate that MVRCap achieves state-of-the-art performance on authoritative benchmarks including VDC, CAPability, and VC4VG.

8 Evaluation-Guided Demonstration Refinement for Vision-Language-Action Policies: The Winning Entry in the MetaFine Open Competition Lifan Zheng, Guotao Chen, Zhenyang Li, Shujie Cui, Qiang Huang, Fan Liu

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: it is an embodied vision-language-action policy paper for a simulator/competition setting, with a new method for refining demonstrations based on evaluation feedback.

We fine-tuned a separate $\pi_{0.5}$ policy for each of the five tasks in the MetaFine Open Competition, with a task-specific training mixture and number of actions executed per prediction. Training started from the official demonstrations and expert procedures. Local evaluation exposed recurring grasping, pushing, and insertion failures. We addressed them by revising the demonstrated motions and by adding expert continuations from states the policy reached, while keeping nominal demonstrations in training. Checkpoints were compared on every instruction, so that a gain on one instruction could not hide a loss on another. Our submission, MAI, ranked first overall with a score of 96.66.

12 Efficient Remote Sensing Image-Text Retrieval via Interaction-Free Recall and Text-Guided Reranking Xi Chen, Xu Chen, Xiamgyang Jia, Wei Wang, Xu Zhang, Zhenyuan Shun
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation LLM Interpretability Retrieval & Grounding

Paper 12 / arXiv:2610.36756 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 5

Why selected: No very close match; it is a remote sensing retrieval method, which is related to vision-language applications but not one of the specific criteria.

Remote sensing (RS) image-text retrieval plays a critical role in understanding massive RS imagery. However, the dense multi-object distribution and complex backgrounds in RS imagery make it difficult to simultaneously achieve fine-grained cross-modal alignment and efficient retrieval. Existing methods either rely on complex cross-modal interactions that lead to low retrieval efficiency, or depend on large-scale vision-language adaptation, which requires massive data and computational resources. To address these issues, we develop a fast-then-fine (FTF) retrieval framework centered on interaction-free multi-granular recall and parameter-free text-guided cross-modal reranking with the Balanced Text-guided Interaction Block (BTIB). Specifically, interaction-free multi-granular recall pre-computes gallery embeddings and matches them with the corresponding query embedding without full-gallery token-level text-image interaction, while text-guided cross-modal reranking with BTIB refines local semantic correspondence within the top-K candidate set. Furthermore, an inter- and intra-modal loss is designed to jointly optimize cross-modal alignment across multi-granular representations. Extensive experiments on public benchmarks demonstrate that the FTF achieves competitive retrieval accuracy while significantly improving retrieval efficiency compared with existing methods.

4 Right Click, Wrong Box: Grading GUI Grounding by What a Click Does Xiaokang Yuan, Binhui Yao, Luwei Yang, Ruimin Chen, Zeng Wang
Benchmark & Evaluation
ArXiv
14
Paper 4 / arXiv:2610.36831 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: it proposes a new benchmark/evaluation angle for GUI grounding by judging clicks via outcomes rather than box overlap, which is an embodied-agent-style evaluation issue.

Leading GUI grounders score 93 to 96% on ScreenSpot-v2, and reported gaps between new and prior models are about a point. At this level the useful question is what the remaining errors are. Benchmarks count a click as correct only inside one annotated box, but interfaces often expose one action through several elements: a duplicated link, the label of a checkbox, the title next to a logo. The box is not wrong. The rule is incomplete. We call a click correct when executing it produces the same outcome as clicking the annotated target. A probe that executes and fingerprints about a thousand clicks per page turns this definition into outcome maps and an execution judge for live web applications, with no new annotation. We execute the predictions of three strong 7--8B grounders on a code forge and an online shop. About one in five and one in three of the clicks that box scoring rejects reach the intended outcome, and the shares remain one in seven and one in three when the instructions are rewritten as natural tasks. A blind audit of 11 models on ScreenSpot-v2 (three 7--8B grounders and eight 3B variants) finds the same effect on web screenshots, with two independent reviewers on a 60-unit subset. The share is highest for the strongest grounders. Training seeds and test-set sampling add noise that more runs and paired intervals control. Box scoring instead under-counts correct clicks, which repetition does not remove, and the audit-based correction of 0.2 to 1.8 points on ScreenSpot-v2 is as large as the gaps the field reports. The correction is needed in evaluation. In our 3B GRPO study, crediting equivalent clicks in the reward gives no stable gain over box rewards across three seeds and four test sets. We release the probe, the outcome maps, the audit and every prediction.

6 TeamGym: A Gym for Team Decision-Making by AI Agents with Distributed Information Chanwoo Park, Jaywoong Jeong, Zachary Wojtowicz, Yubin Kim, Taekyun Lee, Kaiqing Zhang, Asuman Ozdaglar
Benchmark & Evaluation Embodied Interaction Medical AI Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
13
Paper 6 / arXiv:2610.37571 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 closely: it introduces a new benchmark/environment for team decision-making with distributed information, though it is not embodied AI specifically.

Many real-world decisions require teams to integrate information distributed across participants. Yet AI agents are still primarily trained and benchmarked in isolation. We introduce TeamGym, an environment for evaluating and training language model-based agents on team decision-making problems constructed from real-world legal, finance, medical, and software engineering corpora. TeamGym introduces a method to identify a minimal set of facts, each element of which is necessary and all of which are collectively sufficient for a correct final decision, which we call pivotal information. This enables TeamGym to both control the distribution of pivotal information and track how well agents identify and integrate pivotal information when making team decisions. Examining 8 language models on 12 source corpora, we find that current AI agents often fail to effectively share information: Distributing pivotal information across participants lowers success rate by 22.9 percentage points on average relative to giving the same information to a single model (a communication gap). We also document that participants do not effectively retrieve decision-relevant information on their own: A team's success rate is lowered by a further 19.9 points when participants must identify and extract pivotal information from source material rather than receiving it directly (a retrieval gap). To help remedy these deficits, we use TeamGym to train policies through reinforcement learning on team decision loss. Across two policy-training experiments, we find that training improves final-decision quality and induces emergent coordination in participation, speaker selection, and communication, even though the reward evaluates only the final decision.

10 When to Intervene? An Empirical Study of Errors and Self-Recovery in LLM Agents Chiming Duan, Tong Jia, Yingxi Chen, Minghua He, Lingzhe Zhang, Ying Li
Benchmark & Evaluation Embodied Interaction LLM Interpretability
ArXiv
9
Paper 10 / arXiv:2610.36471 Open arXiv
Relevance 4 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 only loosely: it builds a new benchmark/dataset for LLM agents (ReInBench) and proposes an intervention policy, but it is web/computer-use agent evaluation rather than embodied AI or spatial intelligence.

LLM agents interact with Web services to perform tasks on users' behalf. Errors during these interactions can disrupt execution and prevent task completion, motivating external intervention. However, agents may self-recover through continued execution, raising the question of when to intervene and when to wait for self-recovery. We present an empirical study of errors and self-recovery in LLM agents. We construct ReInBench, a dataset containing 1,259 agent traces and 1,403 error events across computer-use and Web tasks. Our study examines how often and how quickly agents self-recover, how self-recovery relates to subsequent execution and task completion, and whether self-recovery can be predicted when an error occurs. We find that 725 error events self-recover, often within a few steps, with self-recovery performance differing across Execution LLMs. Self-recovery can also help agents recover from subsequent errors of the same type and support overall task completion. Intervention should therefore prioritize errors unlikely to self-recover, while allowing agents to continue when self-recovery is likely. Building on these empirical findings, we propose ReCoF, a method that predicts whether an error will self-recover to inform whether additional intervention is needed. ReCoF achieves an average relative improvement of 9.02% in Macro-F1 over the strongest baseline on ReInBench. Our code and data can be found at https://method-notes.github.io/ReCoF/.

7 A First-Step Bias Restores Lost Diversity in Diffusion Model Distillation Wenwu Tang, Dong Wang, Yun Cheng, Olga Saukh
Generative Modeling
ArXiv
11
Paper 7 / arXiv:2610.37110 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: it is an application/improvement for diffusion-based generative vision foundation models, specifically improving diversity in distilled text-to-image/text-to-video models.

Distribution-matching distillation enables high-quality image generation in only a few denoising steps. However, distilled student models often map different initial noise samples to similar outputs under the same prompt. To locate where diversity is lost, we compare the teacher and student along the sampling trajectory. The largest measured reduction in the response to different initial noises occurs during the first, high-noise step. When we replace the first step with the teacher's corresponding trajectory and leave the final three student steps unchanged, most of the output diversity returns. This suggests that the first step is a key point for restoring diversity. Motivated by this finding, we introduce \emph{ReSeed}, a post-distillation method that adds a seed-response bias adapter only to the first student step and aligns its velocity with the teacher's first-interval secant velocity. All student weights and later denoising steps remain unchanged. We evaluate ReSeed on three text-to-image models (SD3-Medium, SD3.5-Medium, and FLUX.1-dev) and one text-to-video model (Wan2.1-T2V-1.3B). For a distilled 4-step FLUX.1-dev model, \emph{ReSeed} requires only $0.00196\%$ additional trainable parameters relative to the base model and is trained on 8,000 prompt--noise pairs, while keeping the distilled student fully frozen and requiring no target images, perceptual losses, or adversarial losses. It improves DINO diversity by $48.3\%$ while maintaining competitive visual quality. These results show that substantial diversity can be recovered through a lightweight, data-efficient tuning of only the first denoising step, without finetuning the full student model.

11 Natural Hard-Negative Mining from Positives for Instruction-Following Retrieval Chaolang Li, Jingyu Li, Zhaocheng Du, Xuyang Xie, Gang Wang, Pengwen Dai
Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
9
Paper 11 / arXiv:2610.36900 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 6

Why selected: No very close match; this is retrieval training data construction and hard-negative mining, relevant to multimodal learning only indirectly.

Instruction-following information retrieval (IFIR) requires retrievers to rank documents by both a query and its instance-specific instructions, yet instruction-conditioned contrastive supervision remains scarce. Existing synthetic methods typically fix a positive passage and generate additional negatives, overlooking the hard-negative potential of the original positive. We propose Natural Hard-Negative Mining (NHNM), which promotes convertible negatives under query-compatible instructions and repurposes original positives as natural instruction hard negatives. Its automated Candidate Cleaning, Reversal Construction, and Geometry Filtering pipeline produces compact, high-contrast supervision aligned with the pretrained retrieval space. Applied to MSMARCO, NHNM yields NHNM-MARCO, an IFIR training set of nearly 7K instances. On InstructIR and FollowIR, models with 568M to 4B parameters fine-tuned on NHNM-MARCO achieve strong instruction-following performance and match or surpass substantially larger retrievers in several settings. Controlled analyses and ablations attribute these gains to natural hard-negative supervision rather than model scale or training-data volume.
