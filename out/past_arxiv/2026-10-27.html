1 StreamVibe: Proactive Video Agents for Evolving User Intent Chengzhi Liu, Yuxiang Lai, Yuzhe Yang, Yanqing Liu, Jiheng Hou, Yue Cao, Haonan Wang, Ruixi Fan, Lan Wang, Sheng Liu, Songyou Peng, Yale Song, Kevin Qinghong Lin, Xin Eric Wang, Jui-Hsin Lai, Youbao Tang
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation Embodied Interaction LLM Interpretability
ArXiv
15
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.34662 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criteria 2 and 3: it proposes a new proactive video VLM and introduces a new benchmark for evolving explicit/implicit/dynamic user intent, which is a novel angle in embodied-style interactive vision-language evaluation.

Vision-language models are shifting from offline passive understanding toward online proactive interaction. However, prevailing methods largely rely on explicit requests while miss the implicit user intent (e.g., "where should I click next?" while a user navigate a website), meanwhile fail to update the state simultaneously as user requests might update continuously. These limitations make them less suited to intent-aware interaction in practical. To address these challenges, we introduce StreamVibe-Bench, a new benchmark serving as testbed for user intent interaction, with 4.1K evaluation samples and 85K training samples spanning explicit, implicit, and dynamic intents. Building on this benchmark, we develop StreamVibe-VL, a streaming proactive model that infers implicit user intents from continuous observations and dynamically tailors its interactions as these intents evolve. We further introduce StreamVibe-Harness, which maintains user intents as persistent states with a fleixble lifecycles via a hierarchical memory to track the latest user intent and environmental state. Our experiments reveal three key findings: (i) StreamVibe-VL preserves strong general video understanding while improving response timing and answer quality by 10.7% over other baselines; (ii) existing proactive models remain limited in understanding user intent and deciding when to respond, especially under implicit and dynamic intents; and (iii) StreamVibe-Harness further improves response consistency and adaptation over sustained interaction, resulting in a 12.3% gain in proactive interaction.

2 The Locality Trade-off in Masked Patch Self-Distillation Xi Weng, Zhaoyu Liu, Lianyu Hu, Jin Song Dong
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
13
Paper 2 / arXiv:2610.33223 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: this is about masked patch self-distillation in vision foundation models, analyzing locality trade-offs in DINO-style representations with insightful empirical diagnostics.

Masked patch self-distillation is a key component of modern vision foundation models, including DINOv2 and DINOv3, yet its implications for local visual information remain unclear. We show that its semantic benefits can coexist with a scale-dependent locality cost. Using two annotation-free diagnostics, we distinguish recoverable local appearance from spatial correspondence. In smaller models, adding iBOT substantially weakens patch correspondence while largely preserving appearance decodability. In larger models, correspondence improves, but pixel and texture evidence becomes less linearly accessible in deep features. Thus, scaling changes the form of the locality cost rather than uniformly eliminating it. Our analysis of Gram anchoring further shows that preserving patch relations does not guarantee simultaneous improvements in both properties. These findings motivate evaluating and designing visual representations around the complementary demands of semantic abstraction, appearance preservation, and spatial correspondence. Our code is available at https://github.com/winci-ai/jea.

3 Qwen-Image-PE: Learning Visual Design for Text-to-Image Prompt Enhancement Zekai Zhang, Yanran Zhang, Shengming Yin, Yan Shu, Zhening Liu, Bingxuan Zhao, Chenyang Zhu, Donghao Zhou, Jiahao Li, Jiahao Wang, Jie Zhang, Kaiyuan Gao, Kun Yan, Lihan Jiang, Ningyuan Tang, Qihang Ai, Rui Song, Shuo Cao, Xiao Xu, Xiaoyue Chen, Yuxiang Chen, Zhendong Wang, Zhouyang Li, Zihao Liu, Zikai Zhou, Bo Zheng, Huishuai Zhang, Dongyan Zhao, Chenfei Wu

Paper 3 / arXiv:2610.33472 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: it is a vision-language / multimodal generative modeling paper focused on prompt enhancement for text-to-image systems, with a new method that improves visual design and generalizes across multiple T2I backbones.

Learning visual design is essential for prompt enhancement (PE) to turn underspecified user requests into coherent visual specifications for text-to-image (T2I) generation. Existing methods preserve user intent and improve overall visual appeal, with less explicit emphasis on fine-grained design dimensions. To address this gap, we introduce Qwen-Image-PE, which learns visual design through two-stage training. Supervised fine-tuning with Caption-Based Reverse Distillation (CRD) uses dense captions of well-designed images as design supervision. Reinforcement learning with Groupwise Design Reward (GDR) jointly compares all candidate images for the same request to refine lighting, composition, layout, and detail. On Qwen-Image-Bench with Qwen-Image-2.1, Qwen-Image-PE-397B improves over original requests by 7.81 points, while Qwen-Image-PE-9B outperforms all evaluated PE baselines. Further evaluations show gains in visual design, aesthetics, image quality, and human preference. We find that PE scales along three axes: training data, model size, and information density, with sufficient information density as a prerequisite for effective enhancement. Moreover, we demonstrate that Qwen-Image-PE generalizes to four additional T2I backbones, even surpassing native prompt enhancers on HunyuanImage-2.1 and FLUX 3 Image Klein. We release Qwen-Image-PE-9B at https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1-PE-T2I.
