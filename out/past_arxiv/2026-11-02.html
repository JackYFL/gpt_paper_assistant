1 When Depth Lies: Benchmarking Vision-Language Models on Mirror-Induced RGB-D Ambiguity Hao Yin, Tianchen Guo, Heming Du, Yan Ke, Yanbin Liu, Xin Yu
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation LLM Interpretability
ArXiv
12
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.41651 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: it is a benchmark for vision-language models on mirror-induced RGB-D ambiguity, directly testing spatial reasoning failures in VLMs.

Vision-language models (VLMs) have made significant progress in spatial reasoning, particularly by integrating RGB images with depth data, which is often assumed to faithfully reflect physical distances. However, mirrors violate this assumption: they distort depth readings while reproducing object appearances, making reflections difficult to distinguish from real objects. As existing benchmarks rarely consider such reflective scenarios, VLM performance in mirror scenes remains largely unexplored. In this work, we introduce a mirror-centric RGB-D dataset from reflective scenes to evaluate the spatial reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art VLMs in these scenarios. Specifically, we collect 1,011 RGB-D images from 525 real-world scenes, spanning diverse indoor and outdoor environments, six reflective surface types, and both daytime and nighttime conditions. All images are annotated with reflective surface region masks and mask annotations for both directly observed and mirror-reflected objects. Then, we curate a benchmark named MIRA-Bench (Mirror-Induced Reasoning Ambiguity Benchmark), comprising 14,140 QA pairs across eight sub-tasks organized into three cognitive levels: Reflection-aware Perception, Spatial Reasoning, and Scene-level Decision-making. We evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs on MIRA-Bench and find consistent failures across tasks: models misclassify whether objects are directly observed or reflected, fail to match reflected instances to their real-world counterparts, and produce erroneous spatial inferences. Remarkably, providing depth data actually degrades reasoning performance, implying that VLMs cannot identify incorrect depth in mirror scenes. The code and data are available at https://github.com/EvaYin-cloud/MIRA-Bench

3 Beyond Trial-and-Error: A Geospatial-Guided Active Reasoning Framework for Robust Aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation Jiawei Ge, Wenkang Gong, Jiuxin Cao, Bo Liu, Lei Cui, Haoran Wang, Xuelin Zhu, Biwei Cao, Chang Liu, Chen Feng, Ioannis Patras

Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 closely: it proposes a new spatially grounded reasoning method for aerial embodied navigation, shifting from reactive search to geospatial-guided active reasoning.

Aerial Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) requires UAVs to locate language-specified goals in large-scale urban environments, where objects often appear small and visually ambiguous from high-altitude viewpoints. Existing methods largely follow a reactive paradigm common in indoor VLN, repeatedly updating navigation states from local observations while underutilizing the geospatial structure that remains stable during navigation. As a result, visually similar distractors can cause a reactive goal search to repeatedly reinforce locally plausible goal hypotheses based on passively accumulated evidence, leading to unreliable localization. To address this, we propose GeoReasoner, a robust framework that shifts the paradigm of Aerial VLN from reactive trial-and-error to geospatial-guided active reasoning. Specifically, GeoReasoner first performs pre-search geospatial reasoning to derive region-level priors and constrain the goal hypothesis space. Building on these, it further conducts hypothesis-driven exploration to actively acquire discriminative evidence and refine competing goal hypotheses. Finally, consistency-aware goal verification adversarially assesses complementary evidence sources before stopping, hence reducing premature or erroneous goal confirmations. Experiments on the real-world CityNav benchmark demonstrate that GeoReasoner sets a new SOTA among zero-shot Aerial VLN methods while competing favorably with supervised approaches.

2 GeoFlip: Benchmarking Geometry-Sensitive Physics in Video Generation Yuqi Li, Junming Liu, Bowen Hu, Ruize Niu, Huiran Duan, Yingli Tian, Hao Wu

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 and also aligns with the friend’s interest in surprising empirical results: it benchmarks geometry-sensitive physical consistency in video generation models and exposes strong failures.

Video generation models synthesize realistic motion, but can they respond correctly to changes in initial geometry? We introduce GeoFlip, a video generation benchmark centered on physical outcome flips induced by geometry. With shared physical settings and textual prompts held fixed, GeoFlip applies controlled interventions to support extent, object placement, and mechanism configuration, constructing paired scenes with contrasting reference events, such as remaining stable versus falling or tipping. These pairs test whether models translate geometric differences in the first frame into correct physical evolution. The benchmark comprises 482 scene pairs and 964 reference videos across six physical categories, with reference outcomes established through procedural scene construction, physics simulation, and numerical validation and verification of physical events. We evaluate 16 models for image to video generation along three dimensions: event correctness, structural integrity, and similarity to reference videos. We examine evaluation consistency with independent vision language judges. On the 442 conditions shared by all models, the highest event correctness averaged across tasks is 27.98%, revealing substantial limitations in physical generation conditioned on geometry. Model strengths vary across physical mechanisms, and the three evaluation dimensions capture distinct capabilities. Typical failures include ignoring geometric changes, generating incorrect object motion, bending rigid objects, and introducing additional components. Rank correlations between judges reach 0.906 for event correctness and 0.929 for structural integrity. By combining controlled geometric interventions, verifiable physical references, and multidimensional evaluation, GeoFlip assesses progress from visual realism toward physical consistency conditioned on geometry.

5 DrawSplat: Atlanta World-Guided Implicit Gaussian SLAM for Indoor Reconstruction Fan Zhu, Zhisong Xu, Ziyu Chen, Zhenjun Zhao, Takayuki Okatani

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 and criterion 4 partially: it is a new SLAM method for indoor reconstruction using Gaussian primitives and geometric priors, with clear vision foundation model-style dense mapping relevance.

RGB-D SLAM requires accurate online tracking, high-quality rendering, and geometrically consistent reconstruction. Existing Gaussian SLAM methods mainly optimize Gaussian primitives with photometric, depth, or local frame-to-model constraints, which are often insufficient in indoor scenes with weak textures, large planar structures, noisy depth, and long trajectories. The resulting maps may suffer from pose drift, surface thickness artifacts, noisy normals, and blurred structural boundaries. We propose DrawSplat, an Atlanta World-guided implicit Gaussian SLAM framework for indoor reconstruction. DrawSplat follows a drawing-like process: explicit point-line geometry first sketches the structural layout, and implicit neural Gaussians then fill in high-fidelity appearance and continuous geometric details. It estimates camera poses, sparse geometry, and gravity direction through point-line joint tracking, infers structural planes from tracked lines using the shared-gravity component of the Atlanta World prior, and converts them into differentiable regularization terms on Gaussian centers and normals. To improve compactness and efficiency, DrawSplat further combines an anchor-based implicit neural Gaussian representation with adaptive structural sampling, which allocates Gaussian capacity to structurally informative regions. Experiments on multiple datasets show that DrawSplat achieves robust tracking, high-fidelity rendering, and strong geometric reconstruction among Gaussian-based SLAM systems, while maintaining a practical balance among quality, efficiency, and model size.

4 WorkIng: A Real-World and Synthetic Highway Work-Zone Benchmark Dataset for Autonomous Navigation Xujun Xie, Dominik R\"o{\ss}le, Adithya Mohan, Daniel Cremers, Torsten Sch\"on

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 closely: it is an embodied/autonomous navigation benchmark with a new simulator-based synthetic component and a novel highway work-zone angle that existing benchmarks miss.

Highway work zones are critical edge cases for autonomous vehicles (AVs), combining atypical geometries and jurisdiction-specific regulations that existing benchmarks fail to capture: real-world datasets lack 360{\deg} multi-modal, full-scene 3D work zone annotation, and no single recorded site can span the layouts and regulatory variants needed for generalizable models. We present WorkIng, a dual-domain benchmark and training resource. WorkIng-Highway provides a 360{\deg} multi-modal work zone benchmark with synchronized 128-channel LiDAR, six RGB cameras, and RTK-GPS across 32 km, yielding 12,000+ annotated frames and 227,399 tracked 3D bounding boxes. WorkIng-CARLA complements this with procedural simulation spanning 24 regulation-aware scenarios across six canonical topologies and six international traffic standards, populated by 100+ custom 3D assets. Benchmarking representative 3D detection and end-to-end planning architectures shows that real-world data remains the primary performance driver, and that scenario-stratified synthetic augmentation improves architectures across 2D recognition, 3D detection, and trajectory planning. Resources: https://github.com/cvims/WorkIng
