Relevance 8 Novelty 8

Why selected: Strong match to criterion 3: it introduces a playable-world system built around an executable game harness, multimodal coding agent, and video-model renderer for interactive game worlds.

Moving from controllable videos to genuinely playable worlds requires persistent world state, executable game rules, and low-latency interaction. We outline a roadmap for the next generation of Matrix-Game that separates game execution from visual generation through an executable game harness, and introduce Matrix-Game 4.0 as the first system built on this design. A lightweight game engine maintains world state and executes interaction rules, a multimodal coding agent translates player ideas into game code and dynamic scene captions, and a renderer based on autoregressive video model transforms engine-rendered low-poly proxies into detailed visuals, conditioned on dynamic captions and memory references. This framework provides continuous guidance for visual generation and enables natural-language creation and modification of scenes and gameplay. Our renderer is trained on aligned low-poly and high-fidelity video pairs and distilled into a causal generator that generates in four-frame chunks with one or two denoising steps. Together with targeted inference optimizations, Matrix-Game 4.0 enables 480p, 60 FPS gameplay on a single NVIDIA RTX5090 with a key-press-to-display latency of 112ms. Single-step generation achieves 83FPS steady-state throughput with 18.2GiB of peak GPU memory usage. With fixed prompts, steady-state throughput also reaches 67FPS on an RTX5080 and 58FPS on an RTX5070Ti. We demonstrate customizable shooter and driving games with engine-enforced mechanics, and release code, model weights, and low-poly game assets to support research and creation of playable game worlds.

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: it is a multimodal large language model agent framework for chart reasoning with recursive self-improvement.

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have substantially advanced chart understanding and reasoning, yet their performance remains highly uneven across chart types. While modern MLLMs perform well on common charts, their accuracy drops sharply on long-tail charts with diverse visual structures and reasoning requirements. Tool-augmented agents can improve complex chart reasoning by introducing specialized visual capabilities, but manually engineering strategies and tools for the heterogeneous and evolving long tail is difficult to scale. We introduce \textbf{ChartEvo}, a recursive self-improvement framework that enables chart agents to adapt to underperforming chart types through experience-driven harness-level self-improvement. ChartEvo jointly evolves reusable chart-specific Skills and executable Tools, while learning reusable local tool-call chains to compose evolved capabilities and control the inference overhead of tool-augmented reasoning. On long-tail charts from ChartBench and ChartX, ChartEvo improves accuracy over its base MLLM, Qwen3.7-Plus, by \textbf{45.6\%} and \textbf{32.5\%}, respectively, demonstrating the effectiveness of recursive self-improvement for long-tail chart adaptation.

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 closely: it proposes a novel simulator/synthetic-data pipeline for LiDAR-camera calibration with controllable extrinsics and benchmark-style evaluation.

Online LiDAR-camera calibration remains a fundamental yet challenging task in the field of robotics. Recently, deep learning-based data-driven approaches have emerged, offering high-precision, targetless calibration capabilities. However, existing methods often suffer from a critical dataset shortcut phenomenon due to the lack of extrinsic diversity in their training data. Networks tend to overfit to fixed nominal extrinsic configurations, leading to severe performance degradation and poor generalization in real-world deployments. To address this limitation, we propose a highly generalizable data generation framework tailored for training LiDAR-camera calibration networks. Our framework leverages existing omnidirectional image datasets annotated with depth ground truth to synthesize multi-modal calibration data under rigorous extrinsic constraints. Extensive experiments demonstrate that the data generated by our pipeline significantly enhances the generalization and robustness of typical learning-based calibration networks. Furthermore, we conduct comprehensive real-world evaluations across various camera-LiDAR configurations, validating the zero-shot transfer capability of models trained exclusively on our synthetic data. To facilitate future research and community use, the code for data generation, the pre-trained models, and online benchmark datasets will be made publicly available.

15 A Principled Optimal Transport Framework for Frame Selection in Long Video Understanding Ziwei Zhao, Yuhang Liu
Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
11
Paper 15 / arXiv:2610.27692 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2: it studies multimodal LLM frame selection for long video understanding with a principled optimization method.

Long-video understanding with multimodal LLMs is fundamentally constrained by the mismatch between video length and the model's limited visual context budget, making frame selection essential for practical reasoning. Existing query-aware methods typically rely on heuristic pipelines: they assign one-dimensional query-conditioned scores to frames and then apply sampling, ranking, or extraction rules to form the final subset. While effective in practice, such methods are generally not derived from an explicit optimization objective and struggle to capture the heterogeneous evidence required for complex reasoning. In this paper, we revisit long-video frame selection from a principled optimization perspective. We formulate it as a structured evidence-allocation problem and propose OTFS, an optimal transport framework that maps multiple evidence sources onto video frames. To reflect the asymmetry of this problem---important evidence should be preserved, whereas redundant frames need not absorb mass---we instantiate this view with a semi-unbalanced entropic optimal transport objective and an efficient solver, OTFS-Sinkhorn. Combined with an exact dynamic program for coverage-aware subset extraction, OTFS yields a practical two-stage training-free pipeline. We further show that, in the single-source case, our formulation induces a Gibbs-type distribution over frames, making Q-Frame's temperature-scaled distribution a special case of our framework while also providing a broader perspective for understanding related prior methods. Experiments on three long-video understanding benchmarks show that OTFS consistently outperforms strong training-free baselines. Codes are available at https://github.com/barry664/OTFS.

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 weakly: it studies multimodal multi-agent mathematical reasoning, but it is not specifically a new VLLM/MLLM architecture or embodied AI paper.

Mathematical reasoning is a fundamental capability for educational applications of Large Language Models (LLMs), where reliable problem solving requires not only correct answers but also coherent and verifiable reasoning processes. Multi-agent systems (MASs) have shown growing potential in this domain by leveraging collaboration and verification among multiple LLM agents. However, existing evaluations primarily focus on the correctness of final answers in text-only settings, offering limited insight into multimodal reasoning capabilities, the reliability of intermediate reasoning steps, and robustness against reasoning errors. To address these limitations, we introduce MAMath-Bench, a process-aware benchmark designed to evaluate the multimodal mathematical reasoning capabilities of MASs. MAMath-Bench comprises 1,003 problems spanning 11 mathematical categories, multiple difficulty levels, and several modalities, including text, images, and audio. It features fine-grained milestone annotations to evaluate systems across three dimensions: problem-solving correctness, reasoning-process reliability, and robustness to adversarial reasoning-chain perturbations. Our evaluation yields three key findings. First, multi-agent collaboration does not consistently improve final-answer accuracy over strong single LLMs. Second, collaboration can substantially enhance the coverage of intermediate reasoning, but such gains may come at the cost of increased reasoning redundancy. Third, current systems remain vulnerable to adversarial reasoning errors, particularly those involving subtle structural perturbations. The dataset is available at https://github.com/mira-ai-lab/AgentMath.

3 VLA-Retain: Benchmarking Cross-Dimension Effects of Vision Tuning in VLAs Borui Xu, Han Chen, Bingsheng He, Ming Wu, Jason Zeng, Michael Heinrich
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation Embodied Interaction Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
15
Paper 3 / arXiv:2610.27698 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 closely: it introduces a new embodied AI benchmark for VLA adaptation, focusing on cross-dimensional retention under perturbations rather than only target-domain success.

Vision-language-action (VLA) models transfer knowledge from large-scale pretraining into robot control, but their manipulation performance remains sensitive to changes in observation conditions, which recent work addresses by fine-tuning the visual module on target-domain data. However, it remains unclear how such adaptation affects robustness beyond the target condition, as existing robustness benchmarks only evaluate models without fine-tuning and adaptation studies only report the target gain. Measuring this cross-dimension effect requires separating target gains from off-target changes, isolating adaptation effects from architectural differences, ruling out generic forgetting, and comparing tuning choices. Therefore, we introduce VLA-Retain, a benchmark that compares five open VLA models, tuned on Camera-view or Sensor-noise data, with their own base checkpoints on LIBERO-Plus dataset spanning seven perturbation dimensions. Our study reveals four findings. First, targeted vision tuning is not local, and its target gain does not predict retention. Mean off-target gain is negative in 8 of 10 model--domain configurations. Under Camera-view tuning, X-VLA gains $69.4$ percentage points on the target but loses $4.6$ points on average elsewhere, whereas OpenVLA-OFT gains $39.3$ and $1.4$ points, respectively. Second, these regressions are not explained only by performance loss on the same tasks without perturbations. Third, broader training coverage raises overall gain over target-specific training but does not eliminate dimension-level regressions. Fourth, tuning strategies are model-dependent, as DELTA-style feature preservation raises X-VLA's mean off-target gain to $+1.2$ points but lowers OpenVLA-OFT's to $-23.1$. Overall, these findings show that target-domain success alone is insufficient and highlight cross-dimensional retention as a key criterion for targeted VLA adaptation.

Why selected: Strong match to criterion 1: this paper proposes a spatial reasoning agent with tool use and persistent scene memory to improve 3D spatial understanding.

Spatial reasoning asks a model to work out where objects are in 3D space, how they relate to one another, and how they move. Agentic VLMs have recently brought large improvements on such tasks by letting the model actively gather evidence with 3D perception tools instead of answering in a single pass. Even so, on many questions the tools return the right evidence and the model still does not arrive at the correct answer: the gap between model and tools, where raw readings become usable conclusions, is left unfilled. We present S-Agent 2.0, a spatial reasoning agent that equips an off-the-shelf general coding agent with a command-line perception toolkit, and we design the interface around one principle: help the VLM close the evidence loop: acquire scene evidence, remember it in a persistent scene memory, and interpret each reading in plain spatial language. This design keeps the full flexibility of a coding agent, and every reading it acts on stays grounded in the tools' geometry. On MMSI-Bench and ReVSI, the toolkit improves a strong open-source model, Qwen3.8-27B, by +13.9 and +20.5 points over the same agent without tools, and a strong closed-source model, Gemini-3.8-Flash, by +11.2 and +9.6 points. With the open backbone S-Agent 2.0 reaches 66.5 on MMSI-Bench and 82.3 on ReVSI, ahead of the specialized system SpatialClaw on the same backbone (62.6 and 74.5), and with Gemini-3.8-Flash it reaches 74.1 and 90.5 against its 67.0 and 79.7. Both tool gains shrink as the backbone grows more capable, but SpatialClaw's shrinks far more on ReVSI, from +13.2 to +2.7, while ours stays at +9.6. S-Agent 2.0 also works across different general coding agent harnesses, such as Codex and Claude Code.

6 Robustness of Internal Signals for Hallucination Detection in Vision-Language Models Allison Chen, William Yang, Salma Abdel Magid, Jonathan Williams, Olga Russakovsky, Esin Tureci
Vision-Language Reasoning Safety & Backdoors Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
13
Paper 6 / arXiv:2610.27041 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it studies hallucination detection in vision-language models and provides surprising empirical findings about internal signals.

When given an image and a text prompt, vision-language models (VLMs) can produce seemingly sophisticated text responses. However, they are also prone to hallucinate objects in their output, which can have detrimental consequences in safety-critical applications. To detect these hallucinations, certain methods interestingly leverage internal signals from the VLM as it generates the text response; potentially, these methods use the same signals that led to a hallucination to detect it. We conduct an in-depth analysis to understand the robustness of these detection methods. First, we show that a naive baseline of a generated word's token position in the VLM text response achieves competitive performance. Further, we reveal how some detection methods rely on token position and use this heuristic as a shortcut. Second, we explore detection methods' sensitivity to language and vision modalities. Our experiments suggest that strong detection performance is often driven by detecting language-based hallucinations, and when methods rely on token position, they tend to be less visually grounded and exhibit reduced robustness when hallucinations occur earlier in a text response. Lastly, we demonstrate that different detection methods can carry complementary signals; when ensembling methods together, adding weaker methods to an ensemble of higher performing ones still offers performance improvements. Altogether, while there may be useful internal VLM signals to detect hallucinations, our investigation reveals important limitations of the current methods that try to leverage them.

7 VisualEvolve: A Skill Self-Evolution Framework for Visual Reasoning Jiawei Wang, Yucun Li, Xueqi Li, Shenghao Wang, Xinquan Gong, Dan Zhang, Zheqi Lv, Yuquan Le, Shaozu Yuan, An Zhang, Da Cao, Tat-Seng Chua

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: this is a new VLM framework with self-evolving visual reasoning skills and tool use.

Visual tool use enables vision-language models (VLMs) to acquire task-relevant evidence, but its effectiveness can be limited by predefined visual operations and the tool-use strategies learned during training. Representing these operations and strategies as reusable external skills allows them to be refined through self-evolution without updating model parameters. However, skill refinement can introduce performance regressions. Meanwhile, inspecting accumulated task execution trajectories together with skill evolution history may lead to evolution evidence overload. To address these challenges, we propose VisualEvolve, a self-evolving framework that evolves executable visual operations and tool-use strategies for VLMs through a Select--Execute--Evolve--Evaluate cycle. Skill-Library Snapshot Selection uses performance on a fixed validation set disjoint from execution tasks to select an effective historical snapshot for subsequent evolution, limiting the propagation of performance regressions. Progressive Evidence Access (PEA) guides capability-gap diagnosis through a coarse-to-fine hierarchy comprising the Skill-Performance Report, Evolution Log, Trajectory Summaries, and relevant raw trajectory segments. Across four VLM backbones and six evaluation sets from three benchmarks, VisualEvolve improves over the Initial Skill Library in 23 of 24 settings, with a maximum gain of 15.1 percentage points on VisualProbe Hard using Qwen3.5-Plus. Ablation studies support the contributions of Snapshot Selection and PEA, while further experiments show that skills can be improved using experience from different data settings and directly reused across backbones. Moreover, VisualEvolve enables effective skill self-evolution with over an order of magnitude fewer task-solving trajectories than estimated for representative RL-based methods.

8 GARRET: Generative Feedback for Region Retrieval in Visual Documents Weijian Jian, Siyang Chen, Yuguang Yang, Yajun Zhang, Chunyu Xie, Dawei Leng, Yuhui Yin
Vision-Language Reasoning Multimodal LLM Benchmark & Evaluation Generative Modeling

Paper 8 / arXiv:2610.27829 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: it is a document-vision retrieval method with a new benchmark and a generative-feedback training signal for region retrieval.

Multimodal retrieval-augmented generation over visually rich documents requires evidence at a finer granularity than entire pages. Existing visual retrievers typically index or return full pages, whereas training region-level retrievers often requires costly query-to-region annotations or noisy model-generated labels. We introduce GARRET, a region-retrieval framework trained from (page, question, answer) triplets through differentiable generative feedback. A shared context-preserving retriever produces page and region multi-vector representations. During training, their retrieval scores define a soft routing distribution over candidate region crops. An auxiliary vision-language model attends to each crop separately, aggregates crop-specific contexts using the routing weights, and predicts the question followed by the answer under a next-token prediction objective. Gradients through the routing distribution assign query-conditioned credit to the indexed region representations, without requiring query-to-region annotations. At inference, the auxiliary model is removed. GARRET also reuses the contextualized region representations, together with page summary tokens, to construct a compact page representation. We introduce ViDoRe-V3-Region, a corpus-wide benchmark containing 200,682 fixed candidate regions across eight domains. GARRET achieves a macro nDCG@10 of 0.4126, outperforming the strongest visual baseline by 2.94 points. On page retrieval, it remains comparable to ColQwen2.5-v0.2 while indexing 47.8% fewer vectors per page.

Why selected: Directly matches criterion 2: this paper studies VLM behavior and proposes an inference-time steering method for context-aware privacy-aligned generation.

Vision-language models (VLMs) can infer sensitive information such as geolocation from subtle visual cues, creating privacy risks when responses disclose more detail than the visual context warrants. We study visual-contextual privacy in geolocation disclosure, where a VLM should reveal location information only at the granularity appropriate to the visual context depicted in the image. We first show that this contextually appropriate granularity is recoverable from pre-generation hidden states, even when generated responses are misaligned, with disclosure levels exhibiting an ordered structure along a common hidden-state direction. It further transfers to another dataset with a distinct data distribution and privacy-risk taxonomy.

19 PACE-MoE: Adaptive Domain Sampling and Domain-Specific LoRA for Medical Visual Grounding Zhenxuan Wang, Jisheng Dang, Ronghao Lin, Bimei Wang, Jingwen Zhao, Wenjun Ma, Bin Hu, Qi Tian, Tat-Seng Chua

Why selected: Moderate match to criterion 3: it is an embodied-style grounding method, but the focus is medical visual grounding rather than novel spatial intelligence or simulation/benchmarking angles.

Medical visual grounding requires models to align clinical descriptions with spatial evidence across heterogeneous imaging domains. However, existing multi-domain post-training strategies typically rely on fixed domain sampling and shared parameter updates, overlooking the need to adapt both which domains receive training and where their updates are applied. To address this issue, we propose PACE-MoE, a policy-guided framework that jointly performs adaptive domain sampling and domain-specific adaptation for medical visual grounding. PACE dynamically assesses the training value of each imaging domain from current-policy feedback and progressively adjusts domain coverage through domain admission, replay, and final all-domain balancing. Meanwhile, domain-specific LoRA adapters route updates to the corresponding imaging domains while preserving the pretrained backbone and visual encoder. This coordinated design aligns training-data allocation with parameter adaptation without modifying the original reward formulation or Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective. Experiments across seven medical imaging domains show that PACE-MoE consistently improves grounding performance over fixed-domain training, achieving a 3.64% relative improvement in mean box IoU over the Vanilla baseline. Under matched domain-specific adapter architectures, adaptive sampling alone further improves mean box IoU by 2.07%, demonstrating the benefit of policy-guided domain allocation. These results establish coordinated data-parameter routing as an effective strategy for multi-domain medical visual grounding.

21 CrystalAR: Autoregressive Modeling for Symmetric Crystal Generation Ziqiao Meng, Rongsheng Chen, Zhi Zheng, Nikita Kazeev, Zhenkun Wang, Andrey Ustyuzhanin, Wee Sun Lee
Vision-Language Reasoning Benchmark & Evaluation Generative Modeling

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: a generative modeling paper for crystal structures, but not a direct fit for embodied AI or VLLMs/MLLMs.

Deep generative modeling has achieved remarkable progress in crystal generation. Given the rich symmetries inherent in crystalline structures, recent work has sought to explicitly incorporate space-group symmetry into generative models, enabling more effective exploration of novel symmetry templates. Yet introducing symmetry templates increases the combinatorial complexity of stoichiometric relationships between atomic species and crystallographic sites, while creating a fundamental tension between flexible exploration of discrete crystallographic templates and faithful realization of these templates as continuous three-dimensional structures. To address these challenges, we propose CrystalAR, a unified autoregressive framework that models Wyckoff symmetry templates and complete crystal structures within a single generative sequence. Specifically, CrystalAR reformulates continuous coordinate generation as a symmetry-conditioned hierarchy of discrete spatial localization decisions, autoregressively refining the positions of all symmetry-inequivalent sites across resolution levels while jointly modeling their spatial configuration within each level. This discrete hierarchical formulation further enables constraint-aware search over both crystallographic templates and geometric realizations. As a result, CrystalAR retains strong symmetry-template generation capability while achieving competitive de novo generation performance and template-free crystal structure prediction (CSP) accuracy. These results suggest that symmetry-template generation can be retained alongside broadly competitive de novo generation performance and competitive CSP without reliance on symmetry templates retrieved from prior data, providing a promising alternative paradigm for symmetry-preserving crystal generation.

4 Decoupling Action from Egocentric Observation for World Simulation Yue Ma, Pengjie Song, Xinyu Wang, Yi He, Zeqian Long, Fangneng Zhan, Kaichen Zhou, Hongyu Liu, Hongfa Wang, Peihao Li, Haoyang Huang, Nan Duan, Qifeng Chen

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: embodied AI/world simulation method plus a new benchmark (EgoActionBench) for egocentric action transfer.

Egocentric action transfer aims to decouple the semantic action from egocentric observations and reproduce them in new visual contexts, enabling scalable world simulation, embodied policy learning, and robotic data generation. Existing approaches to transferring actions rely on condition-guided video generation, which converts the observation video into explicit geometric controls (e.g., hand poses or meshes) to drive synthesis. However, these methods introduce geometric estimation errors, making it difficult to preserve physically plausible interactions when transferred to new scenes. Alternatively, motion transfer methods directly extract motion patterns from the observation video, yet motion-level features alone cannot encode rich contact dynamics, frequently leading to severe hand structural collapse and implausible contact layout. To address both limitations, we present EgoACT, a test-time framework for egocentric action transfer that operates directly on the denoising process of video diffusion models without requiring additional geometric estimators. EgoACT uses Velocity-guided Structure Anchoring to stabilize reference-consistent hand-object structure in the early denoising stage, and Sparse Correspondence Calibration to refine reliable local correspondences in the mid-to-late denoising stages. Together, these two components preserve transferable action semantics while improving temporal coherence and interaction realism. We further establish EgoActionBench, a benchmark for evaluating action preservation, visual quality, and hand-object plausibility across diverse egocentric manipulation scenarios. Experiments show that EgoACT generates more coherent and physically plausible interaction videos than strong baselines.

13 Efficient SAM3 Adaptation for Multi-Class Semantic Segmentation via Dense Competitive Representations Wenbin Liao, Hao Zhu, Yike Ma, Hao Jiang, Feng Dai

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: it adapts SAM3, a vision foundation model, for multi-class semantic segmentation with a new dense competitive representation design.

The Segment Anything Model 3 (SAM3) has achieved significant progress in Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS) by processing short noun phrases to generate segmentation masks with unique instance identifiers. While SAM3 excels at single-concept segmentation, it falls short in complex, real-world environments where multiple concepts must be segmented simultaneously. In particular, SAM3 typically fails to simultaneously process concepts with semantic overlap, leading to semantic misclassifications and redundant mask predictions. Moreover, as the number of concepts increases, the independent inference paradigm introduces prohibitive inference latency. To address these limitations, we propose the Dense Competitive Representations SAM (DCR-SAM) framework. By decoupling cross-modal interactions, the proposed method alleviates the inference latency. To resolve semantic misclassifications, DCR-SAM dynamically aligns textual concepts with visual representations and incorporates a dense competition head to enforce explicit inter-class competition. Furthermore, a learnable background token is applied to absorb non-target objects dynamically. These mechanisms effectively suppress overlapping predictions and generate high-fidelity masks. Extensive experimental evaluations across multiple benchmarks demonstrate that the proposed framework: (1) achieves a 2.3% $\sim$ 10.7% performance improvement over state-of-the-art methods, (2) delivers a 33$\times$ inference speedup compared to the native SAM3, and (3) exhibits strong robustness across diverse scenarios.

14 PACE: Plan, Attend, Check, and Emit for Training-Free Response Timing in Streaming Video Understanding Siqi Guo, Yuxia Chen, Wenbo Zhu, Yangguang Ji, Zhenxiang Jiang, Teng Zhong, Yu Zhang
Benchmark & Evaluation Retrieval & Grounding
ArXiv
11
Paper 14 / arXiv:2610.28323 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 and criterion 3 loosely: it targets streaming video understanding with an off-the-shelf video LLM and a training-free timing method.

Streaming video understanding requires a model to decide not only what to answer but also when to answer. Evidence arrives progressively, and recent benchmarks identify premature responses, issued before the queried event has concluded, as a recurring failure mode. Existing approaches often learn response timing through additional training, while training-free alternatives typically rely on scalar gating signals that do not explicitly represent when an answer becomes final. We organize chunk-wise streaming errors into three patterns: sampling variation can make an answer unstable, a stable answer may lack support in the observed video, or an answer may be supported by the current evidence while the event remains unfinished. We propose PACE (Plan, Attend, Check, and Emit), a training-free framework designed around these three failure modes. Given a question, PACE drafts a plan specifying what to observe and what will signal completion. As the stream unfolds, it invokes an off-the-shelf video LLM at informative moments, evaluates each draft for stability, grounding in its cited frames, and completion, and records verified observations in an event ledger. Experiments on ProactiveVideoQA and OVO-Bench Forward show that PACE improves over the official protocols by up to 5.9 average PAUC points and 6.2 average accuracy points across proprietary and open-source backbones, yielding state-of-the-art performance.

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: it uses real-data self-pairing for scene text editing and also creates a downstream dataset from the edited images.

Aiming to modify textual content while preserving the original style, Scene Text Editing (STE) has significant practical value in many applications. STE typically requires paired images before and after editing for training. However, recent approaches rely heavily on synthetic paired data and complex disentanglement strategies. They struggle to faithfully capture the diversity and complexity of natural scenes, often producing artifacts or hallucinations. In this work, we challenge the common practice and investigate whether STE can be driven by turning each real sample into its own paired supervision.
