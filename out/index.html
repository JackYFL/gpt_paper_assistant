1 A Failure-Driven Data Flywheel for Post-Training Vision-Language-Action Models Yuhao Luo, Zihao Sheng, Zilin Huang, Zhengyang Wan, Boyue Wang, Ruolin Yang, Shuai Wang, Sikai Chen
Embodied AI Vision-Language-Action Models Data Flywheel Simulation
cs.AI
13
Paper 1 / arXiv:2611.34698 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 closely: an embodied AI / VLA method with a simulation-and-real-world post-training loop and a new failure-driven data generation framework.

Pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) models rarely satisfy all behavioral requirements of a new platform, and teams without a large deployed fleet lack the data to address the resulting failures. We propose a failure-driven data flywheel for post-training such models. It constructs target scenarios absent from the available data by inserting task-relevant entities into reconstructed real-world scenes, and evaluates the model against each behavioral requirement of the target task. Failure rates form a failure map across task conditions and difficulty levels, which is re-evaluated after each round and assigns larger data generation quotas to conditions where the model still fails. We instantiate the framework in autonomous driving for occluded-pedestrian emergence and yielding at stop-sign-controlled intersections, and evaluate it on two VLA models and one non-VLA planner in simulation and on a real vehicle. For occluded-pedestrian emergence, five rounds of post-training improve the critical-phase collision-free rate on the sealed test set from 0.261 to 0.579 for AutoVLA and from 0.390 to 0.765 for ReCogDrive, while the unnecessary braking rate in paired control scenarios never exceeds 0.013. On a full-scale vehicle without real-world training, post-trained ReCogDrive achieves 4 of 5 safe passages, versus 1 of 5 for the base model. Fine-grained evaluation reveals a pattern obscured by the collision-free rate alone: both VLA models learn anticipatory deceleration before reaching the occluding vehicle, while braking after pedestrian emergence remains limited. For stop-sign yielding, the stop-before-line rate rises from 0.100 to 0.897 for AutoVLA and from 0.000 to 0.667 for ReCogDrive. With sequential training from the pedestrian-capability models, it reaches 0.599 and 0.358, respectively, while the pedestrian collision-free rate decreases by at most 0.032.

5 Keep or Roll Back? Out-of-Loop Judges for Self-Improving VLA and End-to-End Embodied Policies Zilin Huang, Zhengyang Wan, Kangan Qian, Zihao Sheng, Sikai Chen
Embodied AI Policy Evaluation Self-Improvement
cs.AI cs.CV cs.RO
11
Paper 5 / arXiv:2611.33794 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: it is an embodied AI self-improvement/evaluation framework for VLA and end-to-end embodied policies with a new judge protocol.

Self-improvement is now a practical recipe for embodied policies: a policy collects experience, is retrained, and is kept when a judge rates it higher. However, the judge is usually trained or chosen inside the same loop, so its evidence is not independent of the policy. We introduce SealedLoop, an evaluation framework that adds a sealed judge: an evaluator outside the loop that the improver cannot see or optimize against. A new policy is kept only when a sequential test on the sealed judge confirms the gain, with a bounded probability of keeping a worse one. Running the same loop under each judge, we separate two sources of a false gain, which we call judge error (the loop's judge is wrong on its own data) and transfer gap (a real gain there does not carry over to unseen conditions). We evaluate on LIBERO manipulation, NAVSIM driving, and a full-scale real vehicle. In manipulation, a success classifier accepted 65 of 196 failures in one generation. In driving, policies that gain on the loop's own frames lose in an unseen city, so even an exact in-loop judge would keep 8/15 of them. When the sealed test decides, a driving loop built to improve ends with a better policy than under its own critic (0.674 against 0.649 PDMS, from 0.638), and on driving pairs with known sealed gain the test accepted 11 of 12 real gains and none of 59 losses. In a real-world field experiment on a published improvement step, the reinforced version drove further into a curve than the version it started from in all 6 blind pairs, though neither completed it. These results show that evidence from inside the loop, however accurate, is not enough to decide whether a policy has improved. SealedLoop offers a foundation for trustworthy self-improvement of embodied policies, in which progress is judged by evidence the improving system cannot influence.

2 The Platonic Representation Hypothesis in Jacobian-Space: Language and Vision Models Agree Approximately Up to a Rotation Ross Murphy, Gabriel Brostow
Vision Foundation Models Representation Learning Mechanistic Interpretability
cs.AI cs.CL cs.CV
12
Paper 2 / arXiv:2611.33304 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: it studies shared representations across vision and language models and shows a rotation-based transfer phenomenon.

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) posits that AI models converge toward a shared representation of the world, even when trained on different data modalities. Previous work has found evidence for the PRH in the activations of vision and language models. In this work, we ask whether vision and language models also converge in how they act on these representations: precisely, in how these internal representations affect the models' output. At a chosen layer of a transformer, the Jacobian lens gives every word in the vocabulary (or class in an image classifier) a vector in the hidden state: the vector that most increases the model's tendency to generate that word, at that position or later, or to predict that class. Collectively, these vectors make up what is referred to as the model's J-space. We find evidence of the PRH in J-space across vision and language models, and furthermore, we find that J-spaces mostly converge up to a rotation. That is, a single rotation, fitted on a subset of words, approximately maps one model's J-space onto another's, and so applies to words and classes it was not fitted on. A word's vector, rotated from a text-only language model's J-space into the visual patch tokens of a vision-language model, changes the object the vision-language model says is in the image (and only in the image region where it is added). Rotated into an image generator's text conditioning, a word's vector changes the object in the generated image. Rotated into an ImageNet classifier (trained without text), it raises that object's class score above almost all others, and vice versa. This suggests that models converge not only in how they represent the world, but also in how they act on it.

3 WhatIf: Counterfactual World Models over Action-Grounded Causal Graphs for Robotic Manipulation Xu Yang, Kailai Sun, Qianchuan Zhao
Robotic Manipulation World Models Counterfactual Reasoning
cs.AI
12
Paper 3 / arXiv:2611.33745 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 moderately: it proposes a new counterfactual world model and planning method for robotic manipulation.

World models predict what happens under an action, but choosing between actions requires comparing their outcomes in the same physical situation. Factual transitions alone determine action-wise marginal futures and average effects, but not individual effects or the probability that switching actions causes harm. We introduce WhatIf, a counterfactual world model for manipulation that couples candidate futures through shared latent physics and transition noise inferred from factual history. Each candidate action induces a sparse object-factor causal graph describing the physical pathways it activates. Rolling out these graphs under the shared situation anchors predictions to observed outcomes and confines action contrasts to the objects each action reaches. Shared type-conditioned functions support varying numbers of objects, while a round-trip loss enforces action-invariant abduction without counterfactual labels. We show that replacing an individual effect with its conditional mean incurs mean-squared error equal to its conditional variance, motivating comparisons between coupled futures. Building on these comparisons, Counterfactual Advantage Planning (CAP) ranks plans by their advantage over an incumbent within shared situations and constrains the probability of harm. Paired advantages reduce estimator variance by a factor of $1-\rho$, where $\rho$ is the correlation between branch outcomes, and cancel model errors shared by both branches. Each executed transition provides hindsight advantages to update the physics posterior, the next plan, and an amortized policy without a learned critic. We further establish when observational transitions identify interventional dynamics. Paired simulator interventions evaluate counterfactual accuracy, graph recovery, compositional generalization, and decision quality.

4 Few-Shot Adaptation of Vision-Language Models via Agentic Model Editing Yusheng Zhao, Xiao Luo, Zhiping Xiao, Ming Zhang
Vision-Language Models Model Editing Few-Shot Adaptation
cs.AI cs.LG
11
Paper 4 / arXiv:2611.34036 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely, and also relates to criterion 4 through adaptation of a vision foundation model (CLIP) for downstream recognition.

Few-shot adaptation transfers pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to new domains using a small set of labeled images. Most methods fit prompts, representations, or classifiers within an adaptation procedure specified in advance. Yet the visual distinctions needed for recognition can differ substantially across domains, motivating the question: Can an LLM agent use few-shot examples and model observations to design the adaptation procedure itself? We propose Agentic Model Editing (AME), an agentic framework that jointly designs input transformations, encoder edits, feature computation, and classifier fitting as an executable, domain-customized adaptation program. The agent inspects images, class semantics, model parameters, intermediate representations, and activations to formulate hypotheses about the target task. It implements and tests candidate programs on the support set, with aggregate validation accuracy guiding refinement and selection. Each program fits a complete domain model that is fixed before testing. The agent and its tools are shared across domains, while each domain receives its own adaptation program. Across eleven datasets with CLIP ViT-B/16, AME achieves the highest average accuracies of 81.2% without back-propagation and 81.9% with back-propagation among the compared methods. Further experiments demonstrate applicability across VLM backbones and LLM agents.

6 Navigent: A Geometry-Grounded Agentic Framework for Vision Language Navigation Xinyu Zhou, Weiren Cai, Yitong Chen, Shenghai Yuan, Zihang Mu, Boyu Ma, Jiahua Dong, Yanshuo Lu, Jiaqi Bai, Jianfei Yang
Vision-Language Navigation Embodied AI Geometry Reasoning
cs.AI
11
Paper 6 / arXiv:2611.34249 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 very closely: it proposes a geometry-grounded method for vision-language navigation with explicit execution checking and recovery.

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) with general-purpose multimodal large language models (MLLMs) offers a flexible alternative to task-specific navigation policies, while reliable long-horizon execution remains challenging. Agents must track navigation progress, detect discrepancies between commanded and actual motion, and recognize when movement has passed relevant landmarks or entrances. We present Navigent, an agentic VLN framework built around a frozen MLLM and a Geometry-Grounded Execution Critic (GEC). GEC performs two complementary checks: action-to-motion consistency, which evaluates measured displacement against commanded movement, and motion-to-landmark consistency, which tracks agent-selected visual anchors and entrance candidates in world coordinates. Detected discrepancies interrupt the remaining action sequence and trigger reobservation, local adjustment, or node-based recovery. Historical visual memory supports progress tracking across extended routes. An offline self-improvement procedure further analyzes successful and failed training trajectories to refine navigation prompts and GEC logic while keeping model weights fixed. On the R2R-CE validation-unseen split (1,839 episodes) and RxR-CE validation-unseen split(3,669 episodes), Navigent achieves 84.0%/86.0% SR and 72.9%/69.1% SPL, respectively, demonstrating the value of geometry-grounded execution verification for reliable agentic navigation. 7 ZeroHero: Training Small Language Agents from Scratch Nikita Sorokin, Zoya Volovikova, Alexey Skrynnik, Aleksandr Panov
LLM Agents Reinforcement Learning Interactive Decision Making
cs.AI
10
Paper 7 / arXiv:2611.34635 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it introduces a new small language agent training framework with expert pretraining and PPO post-training.

Large pretrained language models provide capable policies for interactive tasks, but how much model capacity is necessary for a specialized language agent? We introduce ZeroHero, a framework for training small language agents (SLAs) from scratch. In each environment, a large language model acts as the expert, and we collect its successful trajectories. We pretrain each SLA, which has fewer than 50 million parameters, to predict the expert's responses given the interaction history. We then post-train the agent on its own interactions using proximal policy optimization (PPO). During post-training, reasoning alignment supplements environment rewards with expert supervision on the language the agent generates alongside its actions. We evaluate the agents in three multistep environments where acting requires both understanding and generating language. In Crafter, agents interpret textual observations and generate reasoning before acting. In WebShop, they translate shopping instructions into search queries. In Alfworld-Dialogue, they ask clarifying questions and use the answers to guide household actions. The agents approach expert performance on WebShop and surpass their respective demonstration experts on Crafter training rollouts and held-out Alfworld-Dialogue tasks. An interactive demo is available at https://zoyav.github.io/-ZoyaV-zerohero-demo.

8 Can Its Own Misses Teach an MLLM to Find Small Brain Metastases? Yixuan Duan, Kexin Geng, Hanwen Xu, Wei Qiu
Medical AI Multimodal LLMs Error-Driven Learning
cs.AI
9
Paper 8 / arXiv:2611.33836 Open arXiv
Relevance 4 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 loosely: it uses an MLLM self-improvement loop for medical image lesion finding, but the core contribution is domain-specific.

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) are becoming general readers of medical images, yet they remain weak at locating small abnormalities. Brain metastases expose this gap: at 384 px a 0.1 mL lesion covers less than a quarter of one visual token, and at one false positive per scan frontier MLLMs find fewer than 30% of lesions of 0.1-1 mL. Fine-tuning on one dataset helps but transfers poorly, and improving it further is slow trial and error steered by a single score. We introduce LANTERN (Lesion-finding AgeNt Through Error-driven Recursive learNing), a framework in which an MLLM improves recursively from its own misses. LANTERN recasts model development as an error-driven loop: a failure census attributes every missed lesion to one of four causes, an LLM mentor revises one of four levels (evidence acquisition, visual representation, learning policy, decision aggregation) per round, and a rule frozen before the first round decides what is kept, without test data and with every tool call audited. Over 15 unattended rounds, LANTERN raises a fine-tuned 4B MLLM from finding 75% to 94% of lesions at one false positive per scan on BraTS-MET, and 91% of those of 0.1-1 mL, compared with at most 51% and 29% for five frontier MLLMs read under the starting protocol; the gains hold on an external cohort (89%) and on unseen meningiomas (77%). These results show that an MLLM's own misses, attributed to their causes, can drive its reliable recursive self-improvement.

9 Improving Building Energy Efficiency with an Indoor Environmental World Model and Counterfactual Adaptive Learning Xu Yang, Kailai Sun, Dianyu Zhong, Irfan Qaisar, Yu Yang, Qianchuan Zhao, Jian Kang
World Models Control & RL Building Energy
cs.AI
8
Paper 9 / arXiv:2611.33764 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 5

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 loosely: it uses world models and counterfactual learning for building energy control, but this is not a core embodied-AI/navigation benchmark or method.

Optimizing indoor climate systems plays a critical role in achieving the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goals 11 and 13. However, indoor delayed thermodynamic responses and partial observability severely hinder existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods, which are primarily limited by implicit thermal inertia, occupancy dynamic prediction, and cumulative prediction errors during long-horizon rollouts. Thus, we propose a novel occupant-centric indoor climate control framework. It develops indoor environmental world models to predict future physical thermal baselines and dynamic occupancy, explicitly capturing latent thermal inertia. By formulating adaptive regulation as counterfactual advantage learning against future environments via a Branching Dueling Q-Network, and integrating a Held-Action TD-{\lambda} target, it effectively mitigates delayed credit assignment. Additionally, a proposed physics-aligned smoothness regularization suppresses high-frequency actuator fluctuations, potentially extending equipment service life. Experiments on SemiBuildingSim and Sinergym demonstrate that our method achieves a superior multi-objective trade-off. Compared to the best-performing methods, it further reduces energy consumption by 11.2%, occupant discomfort by 44.2%, and action fluctuation by 47.3%, achieving better sample efficiency and cross-region effectiveness. This interdisciplinary study offers an energy-efficient, occupant-centric AI solution for control science, building science, and energy sustainability.

10 ExtractBench Pro: The Long Tail of Document Extraction Boyang Zhang, Justin Xu, Adrian Lyjak, Eli Stewart, Simon Suo
Benchmark & Evaluation Document Understanding Information Extraction
cs.AI
7
Paper 10 / arXiv:2611.33791 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 4

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 only loosely: it is a new benchmark for document extraction, but not really embodied AI or spatial understanding.

We present ExtractBench Pro, a schema-guided document extraction benchmark that tests six challenging modes of extraction underrepresented in existing datasets. It is created following a framework for synthetic document generation, in which deterministic document generators assemble challenging components into documents that are designed to be difficult at a level calibrated for frontier systems. We synthesize 100 realistic documents spanning a diverse range of styles. On this data, we evaluate ten coding agents and eight extraction API configurations for accuracy, grounding, and their ability to perfectly extract documents. No extraction system extracts more than 61% of documents error-free. The strongest general-purpose coding agents, Claude Code Opus 5.5 and Codex GPT-6 Astra, outperform specialized extraction APIs on both perfect-document rate and Value F1, but at a higher cost per page. Among specialized extraction APIs, LlamaExtract spans the lower-cost frontier, with Agentic Plus Max Effort approaching the strongest coding agent on both perfect-document rate and Value F1 at lower cost per page. Our analysis of coding agent traces identifies intelligent image processing and programmatic reasoning, but also failures in applying document-specific definitions and recovering relationships from diagrams. We release ExtractBench Pro and these evaluation results as resources for the future development of improved frontier extraction systems.
