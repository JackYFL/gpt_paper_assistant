1 The Answer Knows Where to Look: Zooming before MLLMs Answer under Visual-Token Budgets Zhensheng Hu, Guanglong Xu, Guanqiang Hu
Multimodal LLMs Visual Token Efficiency Document & Chart QA
cs.CV
13
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.32664 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it introduces a new MLLM-based zooming/pointer mechanism for answering under visual-token budgets.

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) read images through visual tokens, and under the small per-image budgets that efficient deployment requires, the fine text of documents, charts and infographics becomes illegible. Zooming in recovers the evidence, but the region must be chosen before the model answers. We find that MLLMs know where the evidence is, but not in a form that can be read before they answer: the answer tokens localize it from the middle layers on, whereas the attention available before answering does so only in the top layers, at a depth that varies across models, and diffusely. Supervising the pre-answer attention to point degrades reading, and the benefit of zooming is governed by each image's information gap. Building on these findings, we introduce ours, which lets an MLLM look where its answer would. A dedicated look token, processed in a single prefill and kept out of the answering pass, learns in hindsight to attend as the model's own correct answers do, extracting this knowledge into a concentrated pointer before any answer exists. Its hidden state then selects how to look, from no zoom to crops, square windows, windows that adapt to the pointer and foveated warps, supervised by the counterfactual outcome of every view. ours needs no box annotations, per-model layer search, external localizers or generation before the answer. On Qwen3-VL at three scales and five text-rich benchmarks, it surpasses the strongest baseline by 4.8-9.1 points at 98 visual tokens per view and attention-guided zooming by 11.1-17.0 points, while running faster than attention-guided and grounding-based zooming.

3 Separating Angular Alignment from Radial Ordering in Hyperbolic Vision-Language Models Mingyang Gao, Yuwei Wu, Shiyu Luo, Wei Wu, Xiaomeng Fan, Qihao Lv, Haoyu Zhang, Rui Gao, Bingzhen Liu, Zhi Gao, Che Sun
Vision-Language Models Hyperbolic Representation Hierarchical Reasoning
cs.CV
12
Paper 3 / arXiv:2610.32146 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: it is a vision-language foundation model framework using hyperbolic geometry for hierarchical multimodal representation and reasoning.

Hyperbolic vision-language models are well suited to modeling the hierarchical structure of multimodal data, because hyperbolic volume grows exponentially with radius, enabling low-distortion representations of tree-like taxonomies from general to specific concepts. However, existing pretraining objectives for hyperbolic vision-language models entangle semantic alignment with entailment modeling, forcing radial coordinates to simultaneously encode semantic matching and hierarchical structure, leading to conflicting geometric constraints. In this paper, we introduce AURORA, a hyperbolic vision-language framework that decouples the objectives through angular alignment and a novel Radial-Angular Order Cone (RAOC) for entailment modeling. Angular alignment captures semantic similarity while preserving the radial structure for representing hierarchies. Unlike conventional entailment cones, RAOC determines angular allowance from relative parent-child radial gaps. It is incorporated into hierarchical entailment contrastive training to capture partial-order relations. We further introduce a norm regularization term that prevents undesirable radial degeneration for stable training. We train models on millions of image-text pairs and evaluate them across zero-shot classification, image-text retrieval, hierarchical reasoning, and compositional understanding. Our model consistently outperforms strong Euclidean and hyperbolic vision-language baselines, demonstrating the effectiveness of decoupling semantic alignment from hierarchical entailment modeling.

4 PROVE: Probabilistic Visual Grounding via Patch-Level Evidence for Vision-Language Models Mayank Nautiyal, Tianru Zhang, Li Ju, Ekta Vats, Prashant Singh
Visual Grounding Vision-Language Models Uncertainty Estimation
cs.CV cs.LG
12
Paper 4 / arXiv:2610.32440 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 very closely: it is a new method for probabilistic visual grounding with patch-level spatial evidence in vision-language models, adding uncertainty-aware localization.

Visual grounding with vision-language models such as CLIP typically produces a single deterministic relevance map for an image-query pair, without indicating whether the localisation is reliable or whether the referent is present at all. We introduce \textbf{PROVE} (\textbf{Pro}babilistic \textbf{V}isual Grounding via Patch-Level \textbf{E}vidence), a post-hoc evidential adapter that maps frozen CLIP patch features to per-patch Beta distributions without modifying the backbone. PROVE casts grounding as per-patch membership: each patch accumulates positive and negative evidence for whether it belongs to the referent, so a query may match zero, one, or many patches. We train PROVE with an evidential objective on soft patch-coverage targets, and define a prior-pull objective that keeps absent-referent examples near an uninformative prior, so that vacuity serves as an absence signal without a dedicated presence classifier. During inference, a single forward pass yields, in closed form, a posterior-mean grounding map, a spatial vacuity map, and an image-level absence score. We evaluate PROVE on referring-expression benchmarks: it is competitive in localisation with state-of-the-art CLIP grounding methods, its uncertainty tracks localisation error and supports selective prediction under abstention, and its image-level score detects when the referent is absent altogether. Our evidential patch-membership modelling offers a simple, drop-in mechanism for probabilistic verification in frozen vision-language grounding systems.

A live esports broadcast needs a director who chooses which camera goes on air and a caster who talks over that choice, every second and without knowing what comes next. Streaming video language models have learned to commentate, but only over a broadcast that a human director has already produced. We study live broadcast production, in which a model receives the raw camera of every player and must both direct and commentate from the past alone. We present DirCast, a single streaming video language model that does both. Single-image 3D scene construction is a fundamental problem in 3D vision. Recent methods such as SAM-3Dfy enable end-to-end reconstruction of 3D assets and their scene layouts, but still struggle with fine-grained scene geometry and asset quality. 7 Complementing DINO Features with Image Structure for Part Discovery Samyak Rawlekar, Nikhil Chowdary Paleti, Amey Gupta, Narendra Ahuja
Vision Foundation Models Part Discovery Unsupervised Segmentation
cs.CV cs.AI cs.LG
11
Paper 7 / arXiv:2610.32293 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 very closely: it is a vision foundation model (DINO) application that improves part discovery using image structure.

Self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs) like DINO encode rich semantics, enabling training-free segmentation of objects from the background and discovery of their parts via feature clustering. This paper focuses on part discovery: partitioning a foreground object into semantic parts. Existing approaches produce imprecise part boundaries. We trace this to the rigid ViT patch grid used in training DINO, where patches straddling boundaries contain pixels from multiple parts. While DINO's invariance-based training could absorb this pixel-level mixing into clean features, experiments with over 26K foreground patches show it does not: boundary-overlapping patches produce features with higher entropy and lower assignment confidence, blurring predicted part boundaries. Rather than make the massive effort of retraining DINO over geometrically coherent regions, an easier approach is to take boundaries directly from a boundary detector, which supplies the geometric information DINO's features lack. Motivated by this, we introduce Part-DINO, a training-free framework that combines boundaries from low-level image segmentation with DINO's semantic features. We use the boundaries to partition the image into regions, pool DINO features within each region, and group them to discover parts. Without any training, Part-DINO produces structurally coherent part assignments, outperforming training-free baselines across four standard benchmarks (CUB, CelebA, PartImageNet-OOD, PartImageNet-Seg) and remaining competitive with weakly-supervised and unsupervised methods.

10 TileShare: Training-Free Acceleration of Overlapping Tiled ViT Decoding Xiangzhong Ye, Zhongshen Li, Shen You, Ziyang Meng, Liang Xu, Junkai Ji, Qiuzhen Lin, Xiangtao Li, Ka-Chun Wong
Vision Foundation Models Vision Transformers Efficient Inference
cs.CV cs.LG
11
Paper 10 / arXiv:2610.32233 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Close to criterion 4: it targets vision foundation model deployment with a training-free acceleration method for tiled ViT decoding, plus an application to open-vocabulary segmentation.

Fixed-window Vision Transformer (ViT) decoders run at deployment resolution as overlapping tiles: every token in an overlap band is processed by several full decoder instances. In the 2.4B-parameter MiniMax-H3 video decoder, about half of all per-token computation is duplicated. Removing the tiles fails because attention, global tokens and coordinates are bound to them. TileShare keeps each released tile's attention and computes every other operation once per token. The released computation changes at one kind of step only: an overlap token's per-tile results are blended into one shared state with the released weights. Interventions show that this blend costs quality where attention reads the shared states, mainly in the first few layers, which TileShare therefore keeps per tile. Without training, keeping six layers per tile decodes 1.38-1.51x faster than batched and compiled tiling on two GPU types (up to 1.96x with FP8) and is equivalent to the released decoder in PSNR, LPIPS and DISTS on DAVIS videos. On unseen UVG videos with the setting frozen beforehand, it matches PSNR and DISTS and improves LPIPS. TileShare carries over to two further decoders and is again confirmed on unseen data. It is equivalent in PSNR and DISTS on MAGI-1. On the ViTok-v2 image autoencoder at 2K-4K, it matches overlapping tiling in PSNR, improves LPIPS and DISTS and decodes 1.5-1.8x faster. How many layers stay per tile is measured once per model (for decoders, without labels); we report it for 14 decoders and encoders. Optionally, two GPU-hours of self-distillation let MiniMax-H3 share every layer without a loss in PSNR, and a coordinate field trained while the network stays frozen brings fully shared CLIP-based open-vocabulary segmentation to parity with sliding windows on seven benchmarks.

8 FlexGlue: Sparse-to-Dense Feature Matching and Visual Disambiguation Philipp Lindenberger, Paul-Edouard Sarlin, Ondrej Miksik, Marc Pollefeys
Feature Matching Visual Localization Structure-from-Motion

Why selected: Close to criterion 4: it advances vision-based correspondence with sparse-to-dense matching and disambiguation, which is directly useful for vision foundation model pipelines.

To estimate correspondences across images, existing approaches match either sparse interest points, which is highly efficient but limited by repeatability, or dense pixel grids, which is robust but expensive. Furthermore, these approaches are trained with only matching pairs and thus often fail to reject unrelated pairs that exhibit visual symmetries, which is equally important in applications like Structure-from-Motion (SfM). We bridge these gaps by introducing FlexGlue, a neural network for asymmetric sparse-to-dense matching and disambiguation. Given two images and coordinates of points in the first image, FlexGlue estimates the coordinates of the corresponding points in the second image while simultaneously rejecting the pair if non-matching. By searching for a limited set of correspondences among dense pixels, our approach is both fast and effective at both matching and disambiguation: it combines the sub-pixel accuracy of dense matching with the speed of sparse matching, while rejecting incorrect pairs with high precision. Experiments on multiple datasets show that FlexGlue pushes the Pareto frontier in relative pose estimation and significantly outperforms existing approaches for SfM and visual localization. FlexGlue is publicly available at https://github.com/cvg/flexglue .

Real-world 3D reconstruction pipelines often rely on multi-view images degraded by lossy compression. While compression artifacts have been extensively studied in single-image or video restoration, their impact on multi-view geometry remains largely overlooked. In this work, we identify compression-induced cross-view geometric inconsistency as a critical failure mode, where view-dependent artifacts disrupt feature matching, dense correspondence, camera parameter estimation, and downstream 3D reconstruction tasks such as novel view synthesis. Instruction-based image editing models exhibit pronounced sample-specific performance variation: different models can produce substantially different results for the same source image and editing instruction, and the best-performing model often varies across requests. These models also differ substantially in inference latency, making fixed-model deployment inefficient. We therefore study image editing model routing, which dynamically selects an editor from a candidate pool given an input image and editing instruction. We introduce EditJev, a System One routing model that directly predicts sample-level preference scores over candidate image editing models in a single forward pass. Text-enhanced visible-infrared person re-identification (VI-ReID) exploits textual descriptions to compensate for appearance cues that are weak or unavailable under infrared imaging. Existing methods construct compensation from differences between modality-oriented textual representations, implicitly assuming that textual differences are visually complementary. We revisit this assumption through semantic superposition and show that dense textual residuals may entangle useful appearance variations with redundant, making direct residual fusion unreliable. We propose Superposition-Aware Residual Calibration, which transforms dense textual differences into selective and visually grounded complements. Low-Interference Sparse Residual Learning represents modality-oriented text embeddings as sparse activation strengths over a shared dictionary, selects positive strength differences, and suppresses both dictionary-level overlap and activation-weighted interference to reconstruct a compact, selectively controlled residual. Visual Complement and Counterfactual Calibration further calibrates the residual against identity-specific RGB-IR visual discrepancies, encouraging retained semantics to provide meaningful compensation while preserving the residual structure. The pace of a robot demonstration is often determined by the teleoperator rather than by the task itself. As a result, the same motion can appear at widely different speeds across demonstrations, and policies trained on such data may inherit operator-specific timing along with task behavior. We propose to learn a speed prior directly from large-scale demonstration data. Given a task instruction, a motion path and the images along it, it predicts a distribution over execution speed along the path, from which a chosen quantile defines a consistent clock for retiming each demonstration. Existing methods derive the acceleration signal from the very data they accelerate, so each new task costs another fit, annotation, or pair of predefined rates. While multimodal contrastive models like CLIP and CLAP successfully learn shared representations, their embeddings typically exhibit a persistent \emph{modality gap}. We propose addressing this separation through a purely geometric approach: changing the embedding domain from the unit sphere, $S^{d-1}$, to the probability simplex, $\Delta^{d-1}$. Although both manifolds share the same intrinsic dimension, the simplex acts as a structural prior; its nonnegative, unit-mass constraints serve as a cross-modal calibration that forces both modalities to ground themselves in the same coordinate system. By attaching lightweight softmax adapters and using Total Variation similarity, we reduce the centroid $\ell_2$ gap by 97--99\% across five multimodal retrieval benchmarks. This confirms that simplicial geometry induces genuine semantic alignment, matching or exceeding the retrieval performance of standard unconstrained models.
