1 MTEdit: Packed Trajectories Beat Rollouts for Multi-Turn Image Editing Houyuan Chen, Fanzhou Wang, Xiangyu Fan, Songlin Yang, Zhitao Yang, Lvmin Zhang, Pei Yang, Hao Zhao, Lei Yang, Anyi Rao
Image Editing Benchmark & Evaluation Multi-Turn Vision-Language Dataset Construction
cs.CV
16
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.23751 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 8

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 and also criterion 3: it builds a new multi-turn image editing dataset and benchmark, and studies how to use history across turns for image editing.

Multi-turn image editing aims to modify an image according to a sequence of instructions, making it better aligned with human-AI collaborative creation than single-turn editing. However, constructing high-quality multi-turn training data remains challenging, as conventional sequential rollout accumulates errors and introduces visual-quality drift across editing turns. To address this challenge, we introduce MTEdit, a multi-turn image editing dataset built through a pipeline centered on packed trajectory canvas (PTC) generation. By jointly generating multiple editing states within a unified visual context, this pipeline reduces visual-quality drift across turns. Covering both step-wise and context-aware editing scenarios, MTEdit comprises 500K multi-turn image editing trajectories, with up to 8 turns and an average of 5.38 turns per trajectory, making it one of the most extensive datasets for multi-turn image editing. We further introduce MTEdit-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate both scenarios by assessing models' ability to follow editing instructions, maintain content consistency, resist visual-quality drift, and effectively use historical context across turns. Using MTEdit, we fine-tune Qwen-Image-Edit-Causal to obtain Qwen-Image-MTEdit. On MTEdit-Bench, we evaluate a broad range of closed-source and open-source image editing models, including Qwen-Image-MTEdit. Experimental results show that Qwen-Image-MTEdit achieves the best performance among the evaluated open-source models, demonstrating the effectiveness of MTEdit in improving models' multi-turn image editing capabilities.

2 FlexPart: Text-Controlled Semantics-Aware 3D Part Segmentation at Flexible Granularity Yiqun Zhao, Jiachen Xu, Yihao Zhi, Yunfei Zhao, Zibo Zhao, Xintong Han, Chunchao Guo, Shenghua Gao
3D Vision Vision-Language Models Part Segmentation Dataset Construction
cs.CV
15
Paper 2 / arXiv:2610.23659 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 8

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: it uses a VLM pipeline to build multi-granularity 3D part segmentation data and a text-controlled 3D segmentation method.

A 3D asset admits multiple valid part decompositions, while semantics-aware segmentation should reflect both the desired granularity and the parts a user wants to extract. Ideally, a practical segmentation model should strive for a simple principle: what you ask for is what you get. However, realizing this goal is hindered by two core obstacles: the lack of multi-granularity training data and the absence of a holistic control mechanism. We propose FLEXPART, a unified framework for text-controlled 3D mesh segmentation. To establish flexible control, we introduce a holistic control prompt comprising semantic part names with location and shape clauses. To support this mechanism, we utilize an automated Vision-Language Model (VLM) pipeline to construct a large-scale dataset of multi-granularity partitions and their corresponding prompts. Driven by this data, FLEXPART generates a view-independent part-identity field in native 3D space, directly assigning discrete part IDs to mesh faces. To compel the model to respect semantic conditions rather than rely on geometric priors, we train the model using randomized background dropping. Furthermore, an inference-time VLM planner can automatically complete descriptive clauses from 2D renderings, allowing users to simply name the desired parts in ordinary words. FLEXPART with planner-completed prompts outperforms the strongest evaluated baseline by 15% in mIoU, even when that baseline receives ground-truth guidance. On our held-out FLEXPARTBENCH, FLEXPART leads at each granularity and improves average mIoU by 22% over the strongest baseline. With only the requested part names supplied by the user, it also improves IoU by 35% over the strongest click-driven baseline on selective segmentation.

3 FlashControl: One-Step Controllable Generator via Distillation-Friendly Single-Stream Teachers Ngan Nguyen, Dung Nguyen, Quan Dao, Dimitris N. Metaxas, Minh Hoai, Cuong Pham, Anh Tran
Controllable Generation Diffusion Distillation Spatial Conditioning Image Synthesis
cs.CV
14
Paper 3 / arXiv:2610.22537 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: it is a controllable generative model for spatial-condition image generation, with a distilled one-step generator built from a simplified teacher.

Diffusion models have become a leading approach in visual generative modeling, particularly effective in text-to-image and controllable generation. Their main drawback is slow inference as it requires many sampling steps. While recent distillation methods enable one-step text-to-image generation, extending these techniques to controllable generation is still challenging. Existing controllable models, such as ControlNet, rely on dual-branch design that make distillation difficult. In this work, we adopt a simpler strategy. Instead of adding extra conditional branches, we finetune a selected subset of layers in the original text-to-image diffusion model to directly enable controllable generation. This results in DeltaControl, a single-flow, multi-step diffusion model capable of supporting spatial-condition generation while matching the performance of dual-branch approaches. Building on this teacher model, we further apply step-distillation to obtain FlashControl, a one-step model capable of spatial controllable generation with a single forward pass.

6 Knowledge-Guided Distributional Prompt Learning with Visual Hierarchy for Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection Jiyul Ham, Seongho Yoon, Yonggon Jung, Sangjae Lee, Jun-Geol Baek
Vision-Language Models Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection Prompt Learning Localization
cs.CV
12
Paper 6 / arXiv:2610.23172 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: it uses a vision-language-model-based framework for zero-shot anomaly detection with hierarchical prompt learning and localization.

Zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) aims to detect anomalies in previously unseen classes using auxiliary images, with applications such as industrial visual inspection and medical imaging. Recent vision-language-model-based approaches improve ZSAD by aligning textual and visual representations, while distributional prompting further models prompt variability for unseen categories. However, existing methods do not explicitly combine normality-referenced distributional modeling with hierarchy-specific prompt specialization, limiting their ability to model anomalous semantics relative to normality while addressing both image-level detection and fine-grained localization. We propose Hierarchical Knowledge-Guided Distributional Prompt Learning (HiDist), a normality-referenced hierarchical distributional prompt framework for ZSAD. HiDist models each prompt token with a diagonal Gaussian distribution and generates stochastic prompt variations through Monte Carlo sampling. Rather than learning normal and anomalous distributions independently, anomalous prompt distributions are parameterized as deviations from learned normal references, explicitly encoding their dependency. The resulting distributions are specialized into global and local representations for image-level detection and pixel-level localization, respectively, while an adapted global visual representation guides hierarchical alignment. Experiments on seven industrial and medical datasets demonstrate strong cross-dataset performance across anomaly detection and localization tasks. Extensive ablation and representation analyses further support the effectiveness of the proposed distributional modeling and hierarchy-specific representation learning.

7 LonGaussian: Tracking Foundation Priors for Long-Context Feedforward Gaussian Reconstruction Zhoufeng Xie, Guanjun Wu, Shuai Zhang, Xinggang Wang, Wenyu Liu
3D Reconstruction Gaussian Splatting Embodied Perception
cs.CV
11
Paper 7 / arXiv:2610.23250 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 and 4: this is a 3D reconstruction method that improves long-context spatial/scene understanding for embodied intelligence and uses feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction.

Reconstructing high-quality, renderable 3D scenes from long videos is essential for robotic perception and embodied intelligence. Many feed-forward Gaussian reconstruction methods rapidly predict 3D scenes from a small set of images, but extending them to longer sequences presents two bottlenecks: jointly processing all views increases GPU memory requirements, while accumulating pixel-aligned predictions redundantly represents the same surfaces and increases storage and rendering costs. We present LonGaussian, a streaming feed-forward framework that reconstructs compact, globally renderable Gaussian scenes from long image sequences with known intrinsics and unknown camera poses. Built on a pretrained streaming 3D reconstruction backbone, LonGaussian decodes long-context geometric features into dense Gaussians. Within each chunk, multi-frame point tracks link repeated observations of the same surface and guide a learned merger to consolidate the corresponding Gaussian predictions. Rendered-depth consistency filtering further suppresses Gaussians from other chunks that incorrectly occlude locally reconstructed surfaces. On DL3DV and RealEstate10K benchmarks, LonGaussian improves novel-view rendering quality while using substantially fewer Gaussians than the evaluated feed-forward baselines.

8 ESC: Escaping 2D Diffusion Ambiguity with Geometric Constraints for Consistent Novel View Synthesis Ge Ya Luo, Daniel Severo, Brian Karrer, Christopher Pal, Adriana Romero-Soriano, Michal Drozdzal
Novel View Synthesis Geometry-Constrained Diffusion 3D Consistency

11
Paper 8 / arXiv:2610.23732 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: it is about diffusion-based novel view synthesis with explicit geometric constraints, which is directly tied to vision foundation model applications and spatial understanding.

Generating 3D-consistent novel views from 2D diffusion models remains a fundamental challenge in computer vision. In this work, we introduce a framework that enhances geometric alignment for monocular view synthesis under drastic camera changes by jointly generating a target view and its corresponding depth map. By conditioning the diffusion pipeline on the single source image, source depth, and target camera rays, our approach forces the model to inherently reason in 3D space even under extreme viewpoint shifts. To guarantee spatial fidelity, we propose a novel per-pixel depth consistency constraint, which verifies and enforces 3D consistency across generated views. Our framework establishes a robust pipeline for geometrically consistent view synthesis, yielding superior visual accuracy and spatial alignment compared to current state-of-the-art methods. With this work, we pave the road towards robust 3D world models capable of understanding complex environments from a single view image.

9 Can the Parts Fool the Test? Counterfactual Pairing Cycles for Relational OOD Yibo Zhou, Yirong Xiang
Vision-Language Evaluation Out-of-Distribution Relational Reasoning
cs.CV cs.LG
10
Paper 9 / arXiv:2610.23248 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 7

Why selected: No close match to criteria 1-4; this is a careful VLM evaluation method for relational OOD, but it is not a new VLLM/MLLM, embodied AI method, benchmark, or vision foundation model application.

Relational out-of-distribution errors are easy to describe and hard to evaluate cleanly. An image may be natural, a caption may be fluent, and all mentioned objects may be common, yet the caption may describe the wrong relation in the image. Many evaluations intended to test this behavior can be solved without checking the pairing at all, because negatives differ in caption style, template, length, object frequency, or image statistics. We propose counterfactual pairing cycles, a blocked evaluation primitive that reuses the same two images and the same two captions on both sides of the comparison, changing only which image is paired with which caption. This makes image-only, text-only, and additive component shortcuts cancel exactly in finite samples. The same contrast also gives a training loss for relational outlier exposure and a diagnostic for bilinear vision-language scores. Across synthetic controls, GQA, and COCO, cycles separate coarse image-caption matching from directional role-binding failures, while also identifying geometric size relations that remain strongly visible in global features. In a shortcut-contaminated COCO outlier-exposure stress test, a model selected by a near-perfect shortcut validation AUROC retains substantial relational signal but remains below cycle training on exact cycles; cycle training improves CycleAcc from 0.882 to 0.927. The main lesson is simple: to test whether a model understands a pairing, the benchmark should keep the parts fixed, change the pairing, and ask only about coupling. The official implementation is available online.

Audio-Visual Generation Conversational Agents Streaming Multimodal Models
cs.CV eess.AS

Relevance 3 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 loosely: it is a multimodal conversational agent with audio-visual generation, but it is not a vision-language model paper in the usual VLLM/MLLM sense, and it does not target embodied spatial understanding.

Human communication is inherently multimodal, conveying intent not only through linguistic and vocal cues but also through visual signals. However, existing conversational systems have mainly focused on text or speech, leaving out the visual feedback that is essential for natural interaction. In this paper, we propose JAVIS, an end-to-end streaming framework for real-time audio-visual conversational agents. Our key idea is to let the voice and the face speak from a single representation. On top of a shared LLM, a Joint Refined Head produces a unified audio-visual representation, which is fed into dedicated streaming branches for speech and face generation. This end-to-end design avoids intermediate bottlenecks, enabling real-time generation while keeping speech and face tightly synchronized. In addition, we present JAVIS-Syn, a 334-hour synthetic corpus designed to address the lack of expressive audio-visual dialogue data, covering emotionally rich multi-turn conversations and assistant-style exchanges. Experiments show that JAVIS achieves approximately 200$\times$ lower initial latency than a strong baseline, while outperforming baselines in lip synchronization and facial expression appropriateness.

11 FlashReFocus: Interactive Image Refocusing in Seconds Ching-Heng Cheng, Chia-Ming Lee, Ming-Ching Chang, Xin Li, Yu-Lun Liu, Chih-Chung Hsu
Image Restoration Interactive Editing Diffusion Models
cs.CV cs.AI eess.IV
9
Paper 11 / arXiv:2610.23297 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 only loosely: it is an image restoration/editing method built around diffusion, but it is not really a vision foundation model paper or embodied AI work.

Single-image refocusing changes the focal plane and depth of field of a photograph after capture. It is inherently interactive: users try many focal points and blur strengths on one image, so each edit should be fast and change only the blur. Recent generative methods, however, rerun large diffusion models for every edit. We observe that the two sub-problems differ in kind: recovering the all-in-focus image is ill-posed and needs a generative prior, whereas rendering blur is largely determined by depth and the focal plane. We therefore propose FlashReFocus, which runs diffusion once per photograph and renders every edit with a lightweight network. To address the ill-posed restoration, a one-step diffusion deblurrer with a decoder refiner recovers fine detail efficiently while balancing fidelity and perceptual quality. For precise control, the renderer is conditioned on an explicit defocus map and uses defocus-bias attention, which lets pixels at similar defocus share information without interference from pixels at other depths; in-focus content stays unchanged, and editing the map yields tilt-shift and two focal planes. To further test generalization to unseen cameras, we collect 3CReal, a three-camera benchmark of 89 pairs. FlashReFocus achieves the best perceptual fidelity for zero-shot bokeh rendering on EBB! and 3CReal, refocuses 148.3x faster than GenRefocus, and takes about half a second per further edit, 462x faster.

12 STC-MGNet: Spatial-Temporal-Channel Attention-Enhanced Multimodal Graph Network for Pedestrian Intention Prediction Yuanzhe Li, Meng Wang
Autonomous Driving Intent Prediction Spatiotemporal Graphs Multimodal Fusion
cs.CV
9
Paper 12 / arXiv:2610.23356 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 4

Why selected: Matches criterion 1: it is a spatial-temporal multimodal method for pedestrian intention prediction in autonomous driving, which is a spatial understanding problem for embodied/interactive agents.

Accurate prediction of pedestrian crossing intentions plays a critical role in enhancing the safety of pedestrian-vehicle interactions in autonomous driving. However, effectively capturing subtle spatiotemporal patterns in pedestrian pose sequences and integrating complementary visual and ego-vehicle motion information remain challenging. This paper proposes a spatial-temporal-channel attention-enhanced multimodal graph network (STC-MGNet) for pedestrian crossing intention prediction. STC-MGNet introduces a spatial-temporal-channel (STC) attention-enhanced pose graph encoder to learn discriminative representations from pedestrian pose sequences. Three parallel attention branches selectively emphasize informative skeletal joints, observation frames, and feature channels, with their outputs integrated through learnable fusion weights. To leverage complementary visual and ego-vehicle motion information, a multi-stage cross-modal channel modulation mechanism is introduced to adaptively refine pose representations through modality-specific channel gating at successive graph encoding stages. Experimental results demonstrate that STC-MGNet achieves accuracy rates of 70%, 87%, and 88% on the JAADbeh, JAADall, and PIE datasets, respectively, outperforming several representative baseline methods. Ablation experiments further highlight the complementary contributions of visual and ego-vehicle motion information to prediction performance.

Generative Model Evaluation
14 Can We Use Saliency Maps for the Evaluation of Generative Image Models? Eleonora Ristori, Luca Bindini, Marco Lippi, Paolo Frasconi
Generative Model Evaluation Saliency Maps Interpretability
cs.CV
8
Paper 14 / arXiv:2610.23411 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 5

Why selected: No close match to criteria 1-4; this is about evaluating generative image models with saliency maps, which is adjacent to vision foundation models but not a new foundation model or embodied AI method.

Evaluating generative image models remains challenging, as commonly used metrics depend on fixed pretrained feature extractors optimized for different tasks that do not always align with human realism perception across datasets and model families. To address this limitation, we investigate a human-inspired evaluation framework that avoids representations learned for unrelated tasks. Specifically, we train a generator-specific classifier to distinguish real images from samples produced by a given model and analyze its decisions using saliency maps. Across diverse benchmarks, we observe that these classifiers learn generator-specific artifacts rather than a universal notion of "fakeness", exhibiting strong accuracy and limited cross-generator transfer. This makes saliency analysis particularly informative: the resulting attribution maps highlight visual cues and structural irregularities characteristic of each model's outputs. By combining classification accuracy and saliency information, we obtain a composite score aligned with human judgments, without relying on large pretrained backbones and using only real and generated samples from the evaluated model.

4 When to Forget: Risk-Controlled Context Adaptation for Long-Horizon Agents Chenle Chen, Yangbo Wei, Zhijie Zheng, Zhenyu Su, Qifan Wang, Junhong Qian, Chao Yao, Chen Wu, Lei He
Embodied Agents Long-Horizon Planning Context Compression Risk Control
cs.AI cs.LG
13
Paper 4 / arXiv:2610.22897 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3: it is a long-horizon agent method focused on when to retain/compress/forget context during execution, with a risk-controlled policy for context adaptation.

Long-horizon agents learn from failure, but the value of a failure record can change as execution progresses. We study when retaining, compressing, or removing the same history block improves subsequent execution relative to full-history KEEP. We introduce RECAP, a framework that combines matched context interventions, execution-relational prediction, and independent policy certification. Matched environment states and rollout seeds measure success and continuation prompt-cost differences; prefix-only relations between a failure block and the current execution propose promising edits. Independent task bundles then select a policy subject to harmful-intervention and non-positive-benefit risk constraints under a fixed measurement protocol. A five-seed longitudinal case holds both the failure block and its summary fixed: compression fails at an early state and becomes a cost-saving intervention at a later state. Across five backbones, RECAP produces positive paired-success saving estimates with backbone-specific reliability-efficiency tradeoffs. On Hunyuan-A13B, it edits 43.24% of retained checkpoints while matching KEEP's observed 91.89% continuation success and reducing unconditional mean continuation prompt cost by 4,619.8 tokens (2.13%). The framework provides an execution-level reference for deciding when to forget: retain history by its value to what comes next.

5 World Action Critic (WAC) Model: Joint Foresight and Criticism for Policy Learning Xiangteng Zhang, Haoyuan Liu, Yang Guan, Jingliang Duan, Yao Lyu, Tao Zhang, Shuai Zhang, Kehua Sheng, Bo Zhang, Shengbo Eben Li
World Models Embodied AI Autonomous Driving Policy Learning
cs.AI
12
Paper 5 / arXiv:2610.22825 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 closely: it is an embodied-AI-style method paper for autonomous driving/world models, with a novel critic-augmented foresight setup focused on action evaluation.

World models have shown considerable promise in sequential decision-making by anticipating the consequences of actions and providing predictive foresight for policy learning. However, learning to reason about counterfactual actions remains challenging, since predictive objectives may underemphasize information critical for action evaluation, while unexecuted actions lack observed futures for direct supervision. To address these challenges, we propose the World Action Critic (WAC) Model, which proposes multimodal action candidates and jointly learns candidate-conditioned future prediction and consequence evaluation. In our proposed framework, critic supervision directly shapes intermediate predictive representations during rollout, encouraging them to retain information needed to distinguish action quality. Observed futures supervise prediction for executed actions, while external task-quality targets provide evaluation signals for counterfactual candidates. Through joint optimization, critic signals from these candidates propagate into predictive representations without requiring their observed futures. Our theoretical analysis characterizes critic-guided feature retention and bounds candidate-selection regret. Applied to autonomous driving, WAC achieves 93.4 PDMS on NAVSIM-v1, demonstrating the effectiveness of jointly learning predictive foresight and consequence evaluation.

13 From Relevant Memories to Sufficient Evidence: Rethinking Memory Utilization in LLM Agents Shixuan Li, Wei Yang, Peiyu Zhang, Anzhe Cheng, Heng Ping, Xiaole Zhang, Jesse Thomason, Xuezhe Ma, Yue Zhao, Paul Bogdan
LLM Agents Memory Retrieval Evidence Aggregation
cs.AI
8
Paper 13 / arXiv:2610.22668 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 5

Why selected: No very close match to the listed criteria; it is mainly about LLM-agent memory retrieval/evidence construction rather than a new VLLM/MLLM, embodied benchmark, or vision foundation model.

Long-term memory enables LLM agents to reuse past experiences, yet retrieving relevant memories does not necessarily provide sufficient evidence for answering a query. Essential information may remain fragmented across memory records, obscured by abstraction, or disconnected from the relations required for reasoning. We identify this relevance-sufficiency gap and propose QEMem, a framework that reformulates memory utilization as the construction of query-specific evidence sets, shifting the focus from individual retrieval relevance to collective evidential support. QEMem integrates three complementary mechanisms: query-guided discovery retrieves evidence across multiple memory granularities; source-grounded reification organizes it into structured ViewCards with explicit evidential roles and source references; and sufficiency-guided refinement selectively recovers missing information. Operating on existing memory stores without modifying memory formation, QEMem achieves 91.43% LLM-judge accuracy on LoCoMo with Claude Opus 4.5, outperforming the strongest evaluated baseline, MemR$^3$, by 7.6 percentage points and NEMORI by 10.7 points under a shared memory store. Further analyses demonstrate improved evidence coverage and strong performance on questions requiring cross-session evidence integration.
