1 Ask About the Right Moment: Training-Free Object Identity Reasoning for Video-Language Models Di Yang, Xiaochang Li, Muyang Liu, Yufei Wang, Yanhai Xiong
Video-Language Models Object Tracking Identity Reasoning
cs.CV
14
Paper 1 / arXiv:2610.31471 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 and is especially interesting for embodied/video reasoning: it proposes a training-free method for object identity reasoning in video-language models.

Many questions about a video concern one physical object. Where does the ball end up after the cups are shuffled? Which card is the queen? How often was the mug covered? Video-language models (Video-LMs) recognise such objects in single frames but do not keep them apart over time. On real shell-game videos, open models from 8B to 235B parameters, their thinking variants, a reinforcement-learned tool agent and three training-free tool agents all stay within ten points of chance. We separate semantic identity binding from temporal identity maintenance. An object's identity should be fixed at a moment when the evidence for it is visible, and carried afterwards by a tracker. \emph{Track, Then Bind} implements this principle without training. A promptable video tracker follows every candidate object and draws it with a track number. A frozen vision-language model answers a static question about the identifying evidence, which binds the target to a track. The answer is then derived from the tracked state in text, without asking the model to reinterpret the original video during read-out. A single configuration covers all benchmarks. With Qwen3-VL-32B it solves both VET-Bench tasks, where a fine-tuned trajectory model reaches 92\% and 90\% and the same VLM voting over 16 samples stays at 30--32\%. It answers 55.4\% of the hardest Perception Test cup games, where the best published model answers 41.5\%, and, using track-derived timelines, 48.6\% of TOC-Bench questions, against 37.1\% for the same model answering directly. Drawing the same tracks on the video and asking the question directly falls 12--19 points short of the agent on real cup games.

2 DRILL: Training World Models to Improve Policies, Not Predict Pixels Zhaolu Kang, Tailong Luo, Lei Wei, Guangyuan Dong, Sihan Liu, Yingjie He, Siheng Wang, Shuaibo Li, Rongchao Zhang, Zhen Tian, Richeng Xuan, Zhichao Hu
Embodied AI World Models Policy Optimization
cs.CV
14
Paper 2 / arXiv:2610.30814 Open arXiv
Relevance 7 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 3: it proposes a new method for training world models to improve VLA policies in manipulation simulators, with a novel policy-utility objective instead of pixel prediction.

World models are increasingly used to reduce environment interaction when post-training vision-language-action (VLA) policies with reinforcement learning. Yet they are typically trained to predict pixels one step ahead, even though their role here is not merely to render visually faithful videos but to produce learning signals from imagined rollouts that improve the policy. We show that this mismatch is substantial: lower pixel MSE can produce worse policy gradients, and a world model trained for policy utility can yield better downstream policies despite 17% higher pixel MSE on the next frame. We propose the Downstream-Return Imagination Learning Loop (DRILL), a bilevel framework that updates the world model to maximize the policy's return after an inner GRPO update on imagined rollouts. DRILL has two instantiations. DRILL-VWI is a closed-form surrogate, requiring no additional simulator rollouts, that reweights prediction errors by policy visitation, the group-relative GRPO advantage, and model confidence. DRILL-IMG is a full meta-gradient method that differentiates through the inner update using truncated Hessian-vector products. We show that DRILL-VWI recovers the local one-step component of the DRILL-IMG meta-gradient under score-compatibility and small-residual assumptions. Across five manipulation simulators and two VLA backbones, DRILL-IMG raises mean success rate by 13.1 points over WMPO and 7.9 points over RLVR-World, and complements RLVR pretraining rather than competing with it. More broadly, we believe video world models for VLA reinforcement learning can be productively trained by the policy gradients they produce, not only by pixel-level prediction.

3 WeVisOne-VL: Unified Visual Perception from Objects to Pixels Tianming Liang, Junyi Pan, Yuxi Li, Canmiao Fu, Zhipeng Huang, Chong Sun, Chen Li, Jing Lyu

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it presents a unified VLM for object-level and pixel-level perception with detection, segmentation, OCR, and matting capabilities.

Existing vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable for fine-grained visual perception. In this report, we present WeVisOne-VL (WeChat Vision All-in-One), a powerful VLM that unifies object-level and pixel-level perception through structured next-token prediction. It supports multiple visual tasks including object detection, referring localization, counting, OCR, segmentation, and matting, enabling localization at any user-specific granularity, from points and boxes to masks and alpha mattes. Architecturally, we integrate a lightweight pixel decoder into the VLM via a special token to generate instance-level pixel outputs. We train the model with a three-stage curriculum SFT followed by mixed-task RL. Across over 30 benchmarks, WeVisOne-VL demonstrates leading performance on both object-level and pixel-level tasks, while maintaining robust rejection for object-absent queries. By releasing WeVisOne-VL to the open-source community, we hope to advance VLMs from broad visual understanding toward truly reliable perception systems for real-world deployment.

7 MoG-VLM: Efficient Video Anomaly Detection through Motion-Guided Vision-Language Models Khaled Waleed Dawoud, Muhammad Zaigham Zaheer, Khan Muhammad, Muhammad Haris Khan
Vision-Language Models Video Anomaly Detection Efficient Inference
cs.CV cs.AI cs.LG
12
Paper 7 / arXiv:2610.31478 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 very closely: it is a new VLM-based method for video anomaly detection with motion-guided inference and zero-shot evaluation.

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown strong potential for video anomaly detection, but dense frame-level inference makes them impractical for real-time deployment. Existing acceleration commonly relies on uniform sampling, which ignores content dynamics and can either waste computation on static segments or miss localized anomalies. We propose MoG-VLM a motion-adaptive anomaly detection framework that leverages motion purely as a lightweight control signal to decide when VLM inference is necessary. To this end, we introduce Temporal Gradient Magnitude (TGM) which performs content-aware sampling to concentrate computation on informative regions while avoiding redundant processing. We further introduce a hierarchical dual-stream design in which dense multi-frame VLM reasoning is activated only around frames with elevated anomaly likelihood, via anchor-triggered context-window expansion with early termination. A fusion module then reconciles these instantaneous semantic cues with temporal action dynamics. Across four benchmarks, our method achieves 91.91%, 84.30%, 87.88%, and 78.96% AUC-ROC on XD-Violence, UCF-Crime, MSAD, and ShanghaiTech, respectively, establishing new state of the art on three benchmarks under the zero-shot, training-free setting. On XD-Violence, we improve over the previous best result by +0.57% while delivering a 1.31x inference speedup. These results demonstrate that content-aware allocation of VLM capacity, processing fewer frames more intelligently rather than all frames uniformly, is a more effective axis of progress than uniformly densifying VLM inference across frames. Code is available on GitHub: https://github.com/kdawoud91/MoG-VLM.

8 Where Did the Light Come From? Diagnosing Cast-Shadow Reasoning in VLMs and Borrowing the Missing Shadow Prior from Image Generation Models Ryugo Morita, Shunsuke Kitada, Stanislav Frolov, Brian Bernhard Moser, Ko Watanabe, Riku Takahashi, Jinjia Zhou, Andreas Dengel
Vision-Language Models Illumination Reasoning Image Generation Priors
cs.CV
12
Paper 8 / arXiv:2610.30740 Open arXiv
Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4: it evaluates shadow reasoning in VLMs and shows that borrowing priors from image generation models improves performance, a strong vision-language insight.

Shadows are observable consequences of latent physical causes, arising from the interaction between illumination, object geometry, and scene structure. A model that understands shadows should be able not only to read a shadow's direction, but also to infer where a shadow should fall when it is absent, and to judge whether a shadow is physically consistent with the scene. We introduce ShadowBench, built from real photographs, which poses these three questions to vision-language models (VLMs). From each scene, we build a correct-shadow, a shadow-removed, and a human-annotated wrong-direction state. Evaluating 19 open VLMs (2B to 78B) and frontier proprietary models, we find that VLMs read a visible shadow's direction well above chance and improve with scale, yet infer the absent direction only weakly and fall to chance at judging physical consistency even under a bias-free two-alternative test that removes the yes-bias of single-image naturalness questions. Only Gemini-3.1-pro exceeds chance on validity, and even then only modestly and far below humans. Chain-of-Thought (CoT) does not help. The missing ingredient is not reasoning but knowledge that image generation models (IGMs) already hold. Routing a VLM through an IGM lifts its inference well above its direct attempt and helps it flag the wrong-direction shadows it otherwise accepts. Cast-shadow direction is a single facet of illumination understanding, but the sharp gap it reveals between VLMs and IGMs suggests image-generation or reconstruction objectives as a route toward illumination-aware VLMs.

Deep research agents synthesize open-web evidence into long reports, but current benchmarks do not evaluate research centered on a source video. We introduce Video-Centered Deep Research Report Generation. Given a video and user request, an agent identifies research questions, retrieves external multimodal evidence, and produces an interactive report linked to relevant video moments. 5 GraphCAD: Geometry-Aware Diffusion on Edge-Adjacency Graphs for CAD Generation Wenhao Zheng, Xianggen Liu, Li Yue, Jiancheng Shi, Jiancheng Lv
Generative Modeling CAD Generation 3D Geometry

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 very closely: it is a generative modeling paper built around CAD geometry, with a new geometry-aware diffusion representation and dataset.

Generative modeling for Computer-Aided Design (CAD) remains fundamentally challenged by the need to capture the intricate interplay between discrete topological connectivity and precise spatial geometry inherent in Boundary Representations (B-reps). Existing generative approaches largely formulate CAD construction as a sequence modeling problem, but consequently lack an explicit topological inductive bias. In this work, we present GraphCAD, a geometry-aware generative framework that centers modeling on the Edge-Adjacency Graph (EAG), a novel graph-based CAD representation tailored for generative tasks. GraphCAD adopts a hybrid graph diffusion formulation to jointly model the discrete and continuous components of EAG and introduces a geometry-aware denoising mechanism that renders node attributes into spatial point sets. These point sets are encoded by an SE(3)-equivariant backbone and fused into the node representations, thereby grounding the generative process in physical geometry. To support this study, we construct EAGBench, a large-scale dataset of engineering CAD models. Extensive experiments show that GraphCAD can generate complex CAD models with more than 50 faces and achieves state-of-the-art performance in geometric fidelity and distributional alignment compared with sequence-based baselines.

Synthesizing physically grounded dexterous hand-object interactions is important for VR/AR, gaming, and robotics, yet remains challenging because subtle contact dynamics, diverse object geometries, and target trajectories must be jointly respected while maintaining natural grasps. Existing methods either track mocap trajectories with limited transfer to novel objects, or rely on hand-only generative priors that fail to capture coupled hand-object motion, sacrificing contact fidelity and sample efficiency. To tackle these challenges, we introduce PiHand, a Physically grounded Interaction-aware Hand motion synthesis framework. PiHand first trains expert policies that track mocap demonstrations in simulation, distills them into a unified hand-object interaction prior using a goal-conditioned VAE, and finally solve diverse dexterous manipulation tasks through bounded residual exploration in its latent space. Why selected: Matches criterion 1: it proposes a new method for open-vocabulary 3D segmentation that improves spatial understanding by calibrating lifted CLIP features at query time.

Lifting vision-language features such as CLIP into 3D Gaussian reconstructions enables open-vocabulary segmentation through natural-language queries. Although CLIP represents a broad range of semantic concepts, the lifted features of individual scenes can exhibit strong directional concentration, while raw cosine similarity does not account for the scene-specific feature distribution. We show that substantial segmentation gains can be obtained by changing how a fixed lifted feature field is compared with text. Our method partially subtracts a shared scene direction from visual features and text prototypes and whitens text prototypes using a fixed reference vocabulary of generic terms. It then uses soft clustering for multiclass prediction and CSLS with a relevance margin against the reference vocabulary for single-query prediction. Beyond reducing directional concentration, scene centering improves class-relative feature separation in every evaluated scene. Across seven lifting methods and four datasets, the resulting query-time calibration consistently improves mean intersection over union in both multiclass and single-query segmentation. These gains require no retraining, source-image re-encoding, or feature re-lifting, demonstrating the value of scene-conditioned comparison for making better use of existing lifted representations.

11 Squaring-the-Sphere: Distortion-Free and Seamless Panorama Generation via CubeAtlas Xuhui Zuo, Chenjie Cao, Tengfei Wang, Junta Wu, Yisu Zhang, Chunchao Guo
Panorama Generation Diffusion Transformer Spherical Representation
cs.CV
11
Paper 11 / arXiv:2610.31565 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 very closely: vision foundation model / diffusion-based panorama generation with a new representation and attention scheme.

Generating 360-degree panoramas requires both local perspective fidelity and global spherical continuity. Existing representations face a trade-off: equirectangular projection (ERP) enables seamless panorama modeling but suffers from severe distortion and a domain gap to pre-trained image generators, whereas cubemaps preserve perspective geometry but often produce inconsistent faces and visible seams. We present Squaring-the-Sphere, a panoramic image generation framework built on a novel representation CubeAtlas, which unfolds all cubemap faces into a single compact canvas. By arranging orientation-aligned, rotated polar regions alongside the equatorial faces, this representation preserves the perspective geometry of cubemaps while organizing the cross-face relationships required for seamless panorama assembly. It further enables a pretrained diffusion transformer to model the complete panorama jointly in a single pass, without altering the backbone architecture or positional encoding. While CubeAtlas has a token count comparable to ERP, its structured layout makes many dense attention interactions unnecessary. We therefore introduce a layout-aware sparse attention mask that preserves the interactions required for panorama continuity while removing geometrically irrelevant token pairs. As a result, CubeAtlas achieves better quality than ERP modeling with lower attention computation. Furthermore, we introduce a training-free super-resolution technique and a fusion scheme that enhance fine details in distortion-free perspective space before achieving the final ERP panorama. Experiments on various benchmarks demonstrate that our method improves geometric fidelity, cross-view consistency, and high-resolution visual quality over previous methods.

Scaling data, diversity and model capacity has improved embodied learning in physical environments, where diverse experiences remain grounded in shared physical laws. Whether these gains extend to virtual worlds with different visual styles, dynamics, controls, and objectives remains unclear. We investigate this question through a controlled study of continued pre-training (CPT) across three axes: data volume, diversity, and model size. Our 120 training configurations span four model sizes from 0.8B to 27B, six data budgets from 50 to 1,600 hours, and five mixtures ranging from Minecraft-only data to diverse multi-world corpora. Choosing among robot demonstration corpora is a ranking problem: an upstream diagnostic must preserve which candidates produce better downstream policies, not merely correlate with average performance. We study paired instruction dependence, the rate at which a demonstrator changes its first decision when only the goal changes. Across 136 independently seeded quadruped-navigation corpora, predictions developed on 56 corpora are evaluated prospectively on 80 held-out corpora. The audit is reliable and associated with downstream paired correctness, and lowers mean absolute prediction error when added to a correctness-aware baseline, but the primary proper-log-score increment is +0.059 nats (95% corpus-bootstrap CI [-0.027, 0.133]) and the prespecified top-20% comparison does not establish an incremental selection gain. An exploratory response decomposition suggests a structural source of this predictive-ranking mismatch: under the tested predictor specification, separating both-correct and both-wrong responses yields larger held-out gains than their unsigned switch statistic. Seed-to-seed agreement indicates that the held-out endpoint retains recoverable rank signal. These results show that counterfactual response structure can be reliable and predictive while providing limited incremental decision value in this setting when compressed into an unsigned statistic, motivating diagnostics that preserve outcome-relevant structure and are validated against the decisions they are intended to support.

Forming an accurate theory-of-mind (ToM) is key to successful multi-agent collaboration and requires reasoning under uncertainty to capture multiple possible partner states. This is hard to do in the text representation space native to large language model (LLM) agents, which more easily describe a single partner state. To address this challenge, we introduce ToM2Vec, which learns to form abstract theories-of-mind in a rich latent space via a fixed budget of continuous reasoning tokens. During training, supervision for these tokens comes from the partner's actual thoughts, which the ToM tokens are trained to abstract and match. At test-time, ToM tokens are generated by each agent and act as conditioners for that agent's thoughts and actions. Across several environments covering collaborative, competitive, and mixed-motive settings (Overcooked-AI, multiparty negotiation, Decrypto, Hanabi, and Collab Escape/Capture), we evaluate ToM2Vec's ability to support multi-agent interactions and collaboration, finding consistent improvements over the base model and a variety of competitive trained and prompted baselines. We show that latent reasoning is critical, with performance dropping when reasoning is expressed in text. Moreover, we show that ToM2Vec training generalizes to partner model families not seen at train-time, including a proprietary model (GPT-5.4). In our analysis, we provide evidence that the latent tokens encode a ToM, rather than task-specific information: ToM2Vec's tokens improve the agent's ability to directly answer questions about partner beliefs, desires, and next actions.
