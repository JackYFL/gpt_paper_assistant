1 Open-HumanScale: An Open Recipe for Scaling Human and Robot Experience for Embodied Foundation Models Juncheng Ma, Jiaxin Li, Jiankai Tu, Bo Liang, Guodong Lv, Yufan Deng, Junzhe Tang, Liangzhu Yang, Chen Bian, Zicong Cao, Baohao Zhu, Jianxin Bi, Tengfei Yan, Yinyuan Zhang, Kewei Zhang, Xinglong Chen, Kaiqi Chen, Duomin Wang, Xiaotian Tang, Liang Tan, Li Li, Yuan Xu, Haosen Yang, Yuqi Wang, Jingfei Xia, Songyan Huang, Zhiyang Dou, Zhen Dong, Enze Xie, Tat-Seng Chua, Daquan Zhou

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 very closely: an embodied AI paper introducing a new open scaling recipe, new pretraining corpus, and simulator-based evaluation for robot learning.

Egocentric human video offers a scalable source of manipulation priors for robot learning, yet translating raw data scale into generalizable robot policies demands rigorous data curation and training recipes. We introduce Open-HumanScale, the first fully open recipe for scaling egocentric pretraining to 50,000 hours, anchored by Open-HumanScale-50K---a curated egocentric video corpus densely annotated with hand motion and natural language. Leveraging this corpus, we systematically investigate pure human pretraining via closed-loop simulation evaluations and uncover an empirical log-linear scaling law linking pretraining data hours to downstream robot success rates. Grounded in these findings, we develop HumanWAM-Vanilla, a world--action model pretrained exclusively on 50k hours of egocentric human video. Upon robot post-training, HumanWAM-Vanilla reaches 92.08% success on RoboTwin 2.0 Clean2Clean and 68.88% on Clean2Random, alongside a 14.17% mean success rate and a 19.00 mean progress score on RoboDojo. To push the capability frontier further, we explore joint human--robot pretraining with HumanWAM-Goal, a visual-goal-conditioned world--action model. Hybrid data scaling unlocks broad zero-shot generalization across both simulation and physical benchmarks, propelling post-trained RoboTwin 2.0 performance to new state-of-the-art levels: 92.70% on Clean2Clean and a substantial leap to 84.60% on Clean2Random. On physical robots, HumanWAM-Goal further exhibits emergent embodied in-context learning, dynamically adapting its manipulation behavior to unseen visual goals without parameter updates. To support reproducible scaling research in embodied AI, we open-source the Open-HumanScale-50K corpus, data curation and pretraining pipelines, alongside the pretrained and post-trained HumanWAM checkpoints, inference code, and simulation evaluation suites.

2 From Pixels to Actions - Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies Weichen Fan, Haiwen Diao, Penghao Wu, Siyuan Hu, Ziqi Huang, Tianqi Liu, Mingze Sun, Ziyi Wang, Zhongang Cai, Hanming Deng, Quan Wang, Lei Yang, Lewei Lu, Fangzhou Hong, Zhaoxi Chen, Dahua Lin, Ziwei Liu

Paper 2 / arXiv:2611.06918 Open arXiv
Relevance 9 Novelty 8

Why selected: Matches criterion 1 very closely: a new method for spatial understanding in embodied action policies, with analysis that action-critical visual signals appear at intermediate layers rather than only the final encoder output.

Vision-language-action models are typically built on a simple premise: perception is first compressed by visual encoders, and action reasoning begins only afterwards. Yet manipulation often hinges on precisely the visual signals most vulnerable to this abstraction, e.g., local geometry, boundaries, depth, and subtle state changes. This raises a fundamental question: should action policies inherit a fixed visual endpoint, or directly access evolving visual computation? We introduce NEO-VLA, a structurally encoder-free vision-language-action framework. Images are tokenized by lightweight patch convolutional layers and evolve jointly with language throughout the backbone, allowing a flow-matching action expert to draw from instruction-conditioned visual representations at different depths. This simple change reveals a consistent pattern. Native architectures outperform encoder-based counterparts under controlled comparisons; full fine-tuning reduces but does not erase the gap, and additional scale alone cannot recover it. More strikingly, layer-wise probing shows that the action-critical signals often peak well before the final visual representation. Across diverse manipulation settings and distribution shifts, NEO-VLA consequently achieves stronger and more robust control. Together, these results suggest that embodied control benefits from access to visual representations across the computational hierarchy, rather than a fixed encoder output.

Relevance 6 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 3 closely: it is an embodied/robotics-style world-model paper for surgical action transfer, with a new method for latent action learning in a simulator-like surgical setting.

Surgical world models have the potential to provide intra-operative guidance, accelerate risk-free training and facilitate transfer of expert surgical maneuvers. However, the use of world models for transferring surgical expertise has been largely unexplored. Moreover, recent approaches in this domain have mainly relied on ground truth kinematics actions or expert annotations for specific tasks, limiting their applicability beyond the narrow range of tasks with ground-truth annotations. In contrast, existing works in the natural domain have shown promising results in learning latent actions solely from unlabeled videos and transferring the learned actions across contexts. We observe that this paradigm is short-sighted for the surgical domain, failing to capture the hierarchical and goal-directed structure of surgical procedures. Thus, we propose a novel multi-step action learning framework to learn context-invariant latent actions that are easily transferable across various surgical contexts. We perform extensive experiments on the publicly available datasets, SurgToolLoc-2022 and PitVis-2023, verifying our approach with both quantitative metrics and qualitative evaluation by surgical experts. We have published our code and the implementation details at https://github.com/bhattarailab/Surgical-Vision-World-Model.

4 A 3D foundation model unifying CT interpretation and segmentation Yulu Xie, Zilong Li, Shen Xu, Yifan Bai, Liuhong Shi, Jiao Chen, Xianglei Kong, Wenchao Zhu, Na Shen, Xianghua Ye, Yang Yan, Jiaju Zheng, Yunsong Peng, Yanzhou Su, Chaohui Yu, Hao Luo, Fan Wang

Paper 4 / arXiv:2611.06471 Open arXiv
Relevance 8 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 very closely: a 3D foundation model that unifies interpretation and segmentation, directly connecting spatial perception with foundation-model applications.

Radiologists interpret computed tomography (CT) scans by jointly determining what a finding represents and where it lies in three-dimensional (3D) anatomy. However, CT models have treated CT interpretation and 3D segmentation as separate tasks, leaving textual findings spatially unverifiable and overlooking potential synergy between clinical understanding and spatial perception. Here we present HoloCT, a generalist 3D foundation model unifying CT interpretation and voxel-level segmentation by jointly modelling whole-volume context and fine-grained anatomical detail, providing 3D masks as evidence. We curated HoloDATA, comprising 195,921 CT scans from 73 public datasets and six clinical cohorts, with 6.2 million instruction dialogues and 2.8 million CT-mask pairs. Across 346 tasks spanning segmentation, lesion screening, visual question answering, and report generation on 61 internal and 12 external datasets, HoloCT outperformed specialist and foundation models. In multicentre validation across 11,455 clinical CT scans from five hospitals, HoloCT outperformed competing models in lesion segmentation and report generation by 18.2 and 15.3 points. It also achieved 85.1% mean accuracy in screening thyroid tumours and colon lesions, exceeding all four participating radiologists in a retrospective reader study. Further analyses revealed bidirectional semantic-spatial synergy: language-guided segmentation delineated unseen anatomical structures and lesions as well as diffuse free-text findings, outperforming the leading approach by 22.1 points, whereas prompting the model to generate corresponding 3D masks alongside its textual outputs significantly improved interpretation performance. HoloCT shows that clinical understanding and spatial perception can be learned together within a single foundation model and offers a path towards more accurate and reliable CT analysis.

Why selected: Matches criterion 2 closely: it proposes adapting unified multimodal models and improving a VLLM/MLLM-style system for continuous high-fidelity generation.

While discrete tokenization in Unified Multimodal Models (UMMs) establishes strong semantic reasoning, it introduces a strict quantization bottleneck that degrades fine-grained visual details, often resulting in structurally accurate but artifact-heavy images. We introduce FlowShift, a parameter-efficient post-training framework that bypasses this bottleneck by converting pre-trained discrete UMMs into continuous rectified-flow generators. Instead of discarding the backbone's semantic priors, FlowShift replaces discrete visual interfaces with continuous latent projections and applies LoRA to the attention layers. To efficiently boost visual quality without semantic drift, we propose Dual-Objective Velocity Vector Distillation (DOVD), which aligns the model with a high-fidelity continuous teacher and ground-truth continuous trajectories. This approach drastically improves perceptual quality while preserving prompt adherence and reasoning. When applied to leading discrete UMMs, FlowShift significantly improves COCO-30K FID and aesthetic scores, while retaining over 99.5% of multimodal understanding capabilities. Ablations confirm DOVD drives generation quality. Ultimately, FlowShift achieves high-fidelity continuous generation without sacrificing pre-trained semantic grounding.

7 NextGAN: Towards Simple and Scalable GAN Training for Image and Video Generation Xinhao Lou, Dongye Xie, Peng Sun, Chenyi Zhuang, Kaicheng Yu, Tao Lin
Image Generation Video Generation GAN Training
cs.CV cs.AI
12
Paper 7 / arXiv:2611.06270 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 moderately well: a new generative-model training method for image and video generation, relevant to foundation-model-based multimodal generation.

Adversarial training enables one- and few-step image and video generation, but conventional adversarial losses supervise critic values without directly specifying the gradients used to update the generator. We present NEXTGAN, which trains a scalar critic by regressing its input gradient onto displacements between independently paired reference and generated features at interpolated locations in frozen representation spaces. For a fixed generator, this regression is equivalent to an adversarial objective with a gradient-energy penalty, while the generator retains the standard potential-based GAN update. The same objective adapts pre-trained image and video generators with up to 14B parameters, without auxiliary losses, online teacher scores, or trajectories. On ImageNet $256 \times 256$, NEXTGAN achieves one-step FIDs of 2.87 with pixel-space JiT-B and 1.54 with latent-space DiT-XL/1; its adversarial form further reaches 1.36. For text-to-video generation, a single trained Wan2.1-1.3B model supports one-, two-, and four-step sampling, achieving the highest VBench total scores among the compared 1.3B methods at one and two steps. At two steps, NEXTGAN scores 83.13, exceeding rCM and AnyFlow by 1.25 and 0.47 points, respectively. Code is available at https://github.com/LINs-lab/NextGAN.git.

8 Same Layout, Different Count: What Number Words Do Past Ten in Text-to-Image Models Liang Yue, Eric A. Vance
Text-to-Image Empirical Analysis Counting & Composition
cs.CV cs.LG
12
Paper 8 / arXiv:2611.06840 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 7

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 moderately well: it provides an empirical analysis of text-to-image model behavior, especially counting limits and compositional control in generative vision models.

Ask SD3.5-Medium for twelve of an object and then, from the same starting noise, for twenty-five. The picture keeps its layout, but the count changes about as much as it would under a new random seed, and in no consistent direction. We study what number words do once a request passes about ten objects, in five open text-to-image models. Holding the object and the starting noise fixed, we change one thing at a time, and people who never see the request count 3,088 of the images. Every model follows the number for small crowds, but only up to a point, its reach: SD3.5-M and FLUX.1-dev stop following it soon after ten, while FLUX.2 [klein] and Qwen-Image follow it loosely up to twenty-five. Beyond SD3.5-M's reach, the coarse part of the starting noise fixes the composition of the picture but not how many objects it holds: redrawing only the noise finer than an eighth of the image keeps the layout as well as a new number does, and already moves the count two-thirds as far. The number word still reaches the denoiser and acts in its first steps, but nothing we tried gives it a clear direction. Shown instead as a noised grid in the starting latent, the count is drawn: people find it in 59% of images, against 4% when it is only told. Past ten, an exact-match score barely separates a model that follows the number loosely from one that ignores it, and a relative-error score can even prefer a constant guess of sixteen. A same-noise test with a few requests per object tells them apart.

9 TailSteer: Trajectory-Tail Steering for Drift-Resistant Multi-turn Image Editing Ruikang Li, Zixuan Chen, Jiarui Wu, Mingde Yao, Yiyuan Zhang, Yujin Wang, Hanming Deng, Lewei Lu, Tianfan Xue
Image Editing Generative Modeling Multi-turn Robustness
cs.CV cs.AI
11
Paper 9 / arXiv:2611.06741 Open arXiv
Relevance 5 Novelty 6

Why selected: Matches criterion 4 closely: a foundation-model image-editing method for improving long-horizon generative behavior, with a clear focus on drift resistance in multi-turn multimodal generation.

Strong single-turn editing performance does not guarantee reliable multi-turn interaction: as edited outputs are repeatedly reused, small unintended changes can accumulate into severe visual drift. This long-term drift is often mitigated through training on multi-turn data or cross-turn rollouts, potentially altering pretrained priors, or through specialized inference-time mechanisms that are difficult to scale to foundation models. We introduce TailSteer, a final-stage training framework that improves multi-turn robustness while retaining the generative and editing capabilities acquired through earlier training. TailSteer follows a simple principle: keep early denoising dynamics close to those of the base model and concentrate preservation corrections near the trajectory tail, where fine-grained deviations can be suppressed. These corrections are learned from the editor's own on-policy denoising rollouts, using unchanged source content as a preservation reference without requiring curated ground-truth editing pairs. Experiments across the FLUX, SenseNova, and Qwen families show that TailSteer substantially reduces cumulative drift over 15-20 editing turns while largely preserving base-model performance on extensive text-to-image and single-image editing benchmarks. These results demonstrate that long-horizon editing robustness can be unlocked in foundation models after TailSteer, rather than by relearning their existing capabilities.

10 CLEAR: Color-Texture Latent Alignment for Single Image Reflection Removal Tu Vo, Sheir A. Zaheer, Chan Y. Park
Vision Foundation Models Image Restoration Latent Diffusion
cs.CV cs.GR
9
Paper 10 / arXiv:2611.05969 Open arXiv
Relevance 4 Novelty 5

Why selected: Weak match to criterion 4: it applies a pretrained latent diffusion backbone as a visual foundation prior for reflection removal, but it is more of a restoration method than a foundation-model paper.

Single Image Reflection Removal (SIRR) seeks to recover the transmitted scene from a photograph captured through glass. Recent latent restoration methods use pretrained generative priors, but many recover detail by feeding high-frequency features from the degraded input back into the decoder. This works for degradations such as blur, noise, or haze, where input detail mostly belongs to the scene. It is less suitable for SIRR, where reflections add a second structured layer: directly reusing the input's skip connections can sharpen the image while also bringing reflection edges back. Latent compression also introduces color drift and fine-detail loss. We propose CLEAR (Color-Texture LatEnt Alignment for Single Image Reflection Removal), a deterministic, single-timestep, non-iterative latent framework that uses a pretrained latent diffusion backbone as a natural-image prior. A physics-guided separator organizes the prediction around the transmission, reflection, and residue formation model and outputs coarse layer latents in one forward pass. The Hybrid Feature Alignment Module (HFAM) then treats input-derived skips as contaminated layer-mixture features. It rectifies their high-frequency content with a clean-transmission-aligned color query, learned from the input features themselves under ground-truth feature supervision, keeping transmission-consistent texture while attenuating reflection-colored detail. CLEAR achieves the best average across fidelity and perceptual metrics on six real-world benchmarks and the lowest LPIPS on every dataset, with the largest gains on the hardest benchmarks.

11 Semantic Residual Vector Quantization for Sign Language Generation Chang Liu, Ke Han, Niccol\`o Biondi, Gloria Isotton, Elisa Ricci, Davide Talon, Nicu Sebe
Sign Language Generation Multimodal Generation Motion Synthesis
cs.CV
9
Paper 11 / arXiv:2611.06781 Open arXiv
Relevance 3 Novelty 6

Why selected: Only loosely related to criterion 4 via multimodal generation, but it is specifically sign language generation rather than a vision foundation model or VLLM/MLLM paper.

Sign language generation (SLG) maps written sentences into signing motion while preserving both lexical content and fine-grained articulation. This requires a model to decide what signs should be expressed and to realize them through precise handshape, trajectory, facial expression and body coordination. Existing pose-based systems and reconstruction-only motion tokenizers often entangle these two decisions, leaving the generator without an explicit text-aligned intermediate target for what to sign before refining how to sign it. To address this issue, we introduce Semantic Residual Vector Quantization (SRVQ), a framework for SLG that frames generation as a two-level process moving from semantics to kinematics. The newly introduced SRV Quantizer learns a hierarchy in which the first residual level encodes sign content and subsequent levels refine pose, handshape, trajectory, and body coordination, all within a single quantizer and without separate semantic representations or external planning stages. Through word-aware conditioning for hierarchical code generation, a masked semantic generator predicts semantic codes and a kinematic generator completes the residual codes for full SMPL-X motion synthesis. We evaluate using retrieval and FID in a shared text-motion embedding space, alongside standard geometric metrics. On CSL-Daily and Phoenix-2014T, SRVQ achieves R@1 of 82.3 and 70.3, outperforming the previous SOTA by 30 and 46 points, respectively.

We present a shared framework that adapts Cosmos world foundation models for real-time interactive world simulation in Physical AI. By streaming visual observations in response to actions and scene controls, the framework enables users and embodied agents to interact with evolving virtual environments. It supports both predicting the visual consequences of actions and generating observations from physical state maintained by an external simulator. We demonstrate this approach across camera navigation and robot manipulation, controllable video transfer, and multi-view autonomous vehicle (AV) simulation. These examples provide a shared foundation for developing interactive virtual environments for Physical AI training and evaluation. Our code and model checkpoints are available at https://github.com/nvidia/cosmos and https://huggingface.co/collections/nvidia/cosmos3-examples.
