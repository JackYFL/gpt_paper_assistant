## 0. [ZoomGain: Mining Global-to-Zoom Perception Gaps for Fine-Grained Visual Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14076) <a id="link0"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.14076
**Authors:** Haozhan Shen, Liangbo He, Jun Lan, Huijia Zhu, Weiqiang Wang, Tiancheng Zhao, Jianwei Yin

**Abstract:** Fine-grained visual perception requires Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to locate and interpret subtle evidence within complex scenes. Constructing supervision for this ability is challenging: questions must have correct, unambiguous answers that depend on actual fine-grained visual details rather than coarse cues or language priors. Verifying these properties makes manual annotation labor-intensive and automatic generation difficult for models that themselves struggle with fine-grained perception. Yet the same models can often recognize such details once the region is zoomed in. We exploit this global-to-zoom perception gap and introduce ZoomGain, a data construction pipeline that constructs questions about the full image from visual information gained only by zooming in. It verifies each answer on the zoomed view with multiple models, ensures that each question remains unambiguous in the full image and cannot be answered from language priors, and specifically generates high-quality distractors for multiple-choice questions. With this pipeline and human-only screening (no manual QA editing), we build ZoomGainBench, a 1,000-question bilingual benchmark on which the best of 28 VLMs achieves only 61.08. Failure analysis reveals that models favor a particular type of distractor when answering multiple-choice questions incorrectly. Beyond evaluation, training on just 9K ZoomGain samples substantially improves the visual perception capabilities of our 4B/9B models, enabling them to outperform several hundred-billion-parameter models across various perception benchmarks. The trained 9B model achieves SoTA performance on several benchmarks, such as 90.25 on HR-Bench 4K. These results establish global-to-zoom perception gaps as a practical basis for scaling fine-grained visual supervision for both evaluation and training. Our code is available at https://github.com/inclusionAI/ZoomGain.

## 1. [GTAGym: Real AAA Game Tasks Environment for Evaluation and Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13028) <a id="link1"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.13028
**Authors:** Jiajun Chen, Shicheng Fang, Jiahao Lu, Yuxin Wang, Xipeng Qiu

**Abstract:** AAA games offer a rich testbed for evaluating agent perception, reasoning, and control. However, evaluating whether agents can complete tasks in AAA games is challenging and often human-annotated because task outcomes are often difficult to verify automatically, let alone applying it for Reinforcement Learning (RL). ## 2. [From Video Frames to Robot Meta-Frames: A Unified Latent Interface for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14365) <a id="link2"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.14365
**Authors:** Guoqiang Liang, Yiqi Lin, Yanzhe Chen, Kevin Yuchen Ma, Mike Zheng Shou

**Abstract:** Pretrained video diffusion models (VDMs) offer a powerful foundation for World Action Models (WAMs), which jointly predict robot actions and future observations. Building such models requires injecting low-dimensional robot signals (e.g. proprioception, actions, and values) into a latent space shaped by natural videos, and a key design choice is the interface through which these signals enter: existing schemes treat it as a formatting detail, spending a separate latent frame on each signal or bolting on dedicated encoders and decoders. We argue that signal injection is instead a distribution-alignment problem: how well a representation matches the prior of the pretrained VDMs during denoising. Using the relative reconstruction error of a frozen VDM denoiser as a probe, we find that conventional spatial encodings sit far off this prior, whereas frequency-domain packing yields latents that the denoiser corrects far less. Motivated by this diagnostic result, we propose MetaWAM, which folds all robot signals into a single unified meta-frame: each signal is assigned to a disjoint frequency-domain channel band and lifted to the spatial latent by an inverse DCT, yielding a decodable interface aligned with the pretrained VDM prior without any added module or architectural change. A fixed band layout together with slot-aware routing lets the same meta-frame support policy, world-model, and value prediction over a shared set of robot variables. On RoboCasa, MetaWAM reaches 68.9% success while collapsing the per-signal frames into one and cutting inference latency by 39%, and attains 85.0% success on three real-world Franka tasks. Code and models are available at https://github.com/showlab/MetaWAM

## 3. [X Marks the Spot: Diffusion for Image Geolocalization with Location Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13180) <a id="link3"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.13180
**Authors:** Brian Wei, Chih-Chuan Hsu, Tianyi Gao, Daniel Cher, Eric Xing, Danielle Beaulieu, Nathan Jacobs

**Abstract:** Image geolocalization predicts the location at which a photo was taken. Accurate geolocalization requires discriminating between both physically and semantically adjacent areas on Earth. However, prior diffusion-based approaches predict locations by generating coordinates, which are semantically unstable over space. We avoid this limitation by learning geolocalization behavior in the pretrained representation spaces of location encoders, which provide both positional and semantic understanding. To make this space usable for coordinate prediction, we introduce a Location Autoencoder (LAE), which pairs a frozen location encoder with a learned decoder mapping embeddings back to coordinates with kilometer-level accuracy worldwide. In the LAE space, we train DIG, a latent diffusion model that transports noise to location embeddings conditioned on image features. DIG consistently outperforms coordinate-based generative models and sets a new state of the art for parametric geolocalization on three benchmarks: OSV-5M, Im2GPS3k, and YFCC4k. Notably, DIG is the first generative model to outperform retrieval-based methods, and further improves retrieve-and-rerank systems when used as a prior. Code and models will be released.

**Abstract:** Recent work on latent-free diffusion transformers has demonstrated the feasibility of training image generators without a latent VAE. **ArXiv ID:** 2611.13890

**Abstract:** Recent work has shown that pretrained image generators can support diverse visual tasks. ## 15. [Trajectory-Regularized Distribution Distillation for One-Step Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12978) <a id="link15"></a>

## 16. [Adapting a Vision Foundation Model for Fracture Characterization in Acoustic Image Logs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13911) <a id="link16"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.13911
**Authors:** Guangyu Wang, Xiaodong Ma, Xinming Wu

**Abstract:** Natural fractures (NFs) and drilling-induced tensile fractures (DITFs) in acoustic image logs provide insights into rock mass structure and in situ stress, but manual characterization is time-consuming. Deep learning can automate this task, although expert-labeled data are scarce. We investigate whether adapting a pretrained vision foundation model (VFM) outperforms task-specific models trained from scratch with limited annotations. Using 80 annotated acoustic image samples, we fine-tuned DINOv3, pretrained on satellite imagery, and evaluated it on four boreholes excluded from fine-tuning. The adapted model estimated NF and DITF parameters comparably to human interpretation. Relative to U-Net, YOLOv8, and Swin Transformer models trained from scratch, it yielded fewer false positives and negatives and more accurate fracture counts and orientations. However, its performance advantage generally narrowed as training sample size decreased. We also assessed decoder architecture, fine-tuning strategy, and image enhancement. A progressive upsampling decoder with full fine-tuning performed best among the DINOv3 configurations tested, while fracture-focused image enhancement did not consistently improve accuracy. These results demonstrate the potential of VFM adaptation for fracture characterization in acoustic image logs with limited labeled data and suggest a transferable workflow for other annotation-scarce geoscientific imaging tasks.

## 17. [Domain adaptation of a video Foundation model for Heart Failure Diagnosis from Echocardiography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13297) <a id="link17"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.13297
**Authors:** Mahyar Bolhassani, Tijana Geroski, Narayana S. Singam, Amir Amini

**Abstract:** The rapid advancement of foundation models has transformed computer vision, yet their adaptation to medical imaging, particularly purpose built foundation models for spatio temporal echocardiography videos, remains limited. In this work, we explore the feasibility of fine tuning a pretrained foundation model for video (TimeSformer) for automated diagnosis of reduced ejection fraction heart failure (HFrEF) from apical four chamber videos. The model is finetuned on the publicly available RVENet A4C dataset with a phase aware clip generation strategy that captures complementary systolic and diastolic dynamics. We employ divided space time attention, layer wise learning rate decay, and multi clip inference to enhance robustness under limited data. Experimental results show that full fine tuning of the proposed model outperforms classifier only tuning and a 3D CNN baseline. These findings highlight the potential of leveraging foundation video models for echocardiography based heart failure diagnosis and motivates further domain adaptation studies in medical imaging.

## 18. [picoEmergencyNet : Attention-Enhanced Ultra-Small Deep Learning Model for Aerial Image Classification in Emergency Monitoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13638) <a id="link18"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.13638
**Authors:** Christos Kyrkou

**Abstract:** Real-time disaster recognition from aerial imagery requires deep learning models that operate under strict memory, compute, and latency constraints. While recent approaches achieve high accuracy, their computational cost limits deployment on resource-constrained UAV platforms. This work introduces picoEmergencyNet, an ultra-lightweight convolutional neural network designed for aerial image disaster classification. The proposed architecture combines depthwise dilated convolutions with efficient channel attention and channel shuffling to enable multi-scale feature extraction at minimal computational cost. picoEmergencyNet has 23k parameters and 7M FLOPs, requiring only 0.1 MB of model storage. Evaluated on the AIDER and AIDERv2 disaster datasets, it achieves up to 96.1% accuracy, matching or exceeding state-of-the-art lightweight models that are orders of magnitude larger. Deployment experiments on the NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin show that, without hardware-specific optimizations, picoEmergencyNet achieves 258 FPS on GPU and 46 FPS on CPU with a 7x lower activation memory footprint compared to competing models. These results demonstrate that careful architectural specialization, rather than model scaling, enables highly efficient and accurate TinyML-ready disaster recognition for real-time UAV-based emergency monitoring.
