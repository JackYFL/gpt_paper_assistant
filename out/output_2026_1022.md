## 0. [ProactEgoMem: Benchmarking Proactive Memory in Streaming Egocentric Video](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30089) <a id="link0"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.30089
**Authors:** Ruotong Liao, Meikel Kokowski, Xiaoxiao Sun, Yongqin Xian, Laura Bravo S\'anchez, Shiye Su, Federico Tombari, Serena Yeung-Levy, Volker Tresp

**Abstract:** Today's multimodal assistants are typically prompted to interpret the visual evidence presented to them. To offload the cognitive burden of remembering across long horizons, wearable assistants should instead proactively acquire and maintain useful memory as the user's surroundings evolve. Doing so on the user's behalf requires identifying what matters, understanding the scene in real time, and initiating memory maintenance in the background while the user stays focused on their activities. We introduce ProactEgoMem, a benchmark that evaluates proactive memory in streaming egocentric video. Three tasks ground the evaluation in ambient environmental needs: Information Capture (IC) preserves transient yet dense visual information, State Tracking (ST) follows changing object states, and Location Tracking (LT) records object movements. The benchmark defines its protocol at two levels of proactiveness: the Notification Level asks for a single timely notification that satisfies the user's intent, while the deeper Intervention Level asks the agent to maintain memory on the user's behalf through an active start-to-stop tool lifecycle. Parallel ProactEgoMem-Daily and ProactEgoMem-Sci tracks contain 300/100 clips covering daily and scientific use cases, paired with 1000/500 questions in ProactEgoMem-QA, which measures the quality of the proactively acquired memory. Across proprietary, open-source, video-language, and streaming baselines, even the strongest models fail to capture memory both timely and accurately. ProactEgoMem provides a rigorous evaluation foundation for proactive multimodal assistance and for video agents that co-exist with their users.

**Comment:** Strong match to criterion 3: it introduces a new benchmark for proactive memory in streaming egocentric video, focusing on a novel embodied-assistant angle that prior work often ignores.

## 1. [Exploring Temporal Context-Aware Physical Grounding in Latent World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28483) <a id="link1"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.28483
**Authors:** Yilei Man, Yang Chen, Jingcai Guo

**Abstract:** Learning latent dynamics is fundamental to building action-conditioned world models for planning. Among existing families, Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) offer a natural framework by modeling transitions directly in latent space rather than at the pixel level. However, two issues remain in JEPA-based world models: (i) physical grounding lacks explicit aggregation of temporal context and (ii) action planning always overly relies on generic sampling distributions. To this end, we propose $\mathcal{G}$-JEPA, which incorporates temporal context into physical grounding for latent world models and couples the learned models with prior-guided action planning. Specifically, during learning, we incorporate a shared causal temporal module that aggregates temporal context within observation and prediction sequences under physical state supervision, grounding both in task-relevant physical states. When planning, we further introduce action inference through stepwise conditioning of a generative action distribution on the current and goal latent representations together with their evolving discrepancy, yielding anchored proposals that guide model-based online refinement. Together, physical state supervision guides temporal representation learning, while the action prior guides search through the learned world model. Experiments demonstrate improved planning performance on manipulation tasks, including tasks with increasing temporal separation between initial and goal observations. Code is publicly available at https://github.com/myl11045/G-JEPA.

## 2. [ALPS: Visual State Encoders for World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30120) <a id="link2"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.30120
**Authors:** Yanqing Liu, Letian Zhang, Chengzhi Liu, Zijun Wang, Jieru Mei, Hongliang Fei, Yuyin Zhou, Cihang Xie

**Abstract:** World models turn visual representations into predictions and actions. We argue that their visual foundation should combine physical-state information, native language alignment, and pixel reconstruction, so that a single representation can support prediction, instruction, and inspection. However, existing encoders typically specialize in only one or two of these capabilities; we therefore introduce ALPS, a pretrained visual state encoder that unifies all three in a single set of patch tokens. Using only still images and text, we pretrain a ViT-B/16 from scratch with language, reconstruction, and spatial correspondence objectives, and then refine it via cross-view self-distillation. Downstream, frozen ALPS tokens reach the highest mean policy success on LIBERO (85.5%) when paired with our native text tower, match or exceed leading encoders on world-model planning across PushT, Wall, and Granular, and allow predicted future tokens to be decoded directly into pixels for visual inspection. We hope this work helps establish visual pretraining as a shared foundation for understanding, prediction, and action in world models.

## 3. [RealMe: Immersive Volumetric Video from Minimal Captures](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30023) <a id="link3"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.30023
**Authors:** Chenxu Zhou, Zhiyuan Yu, Yudong Jin, Yinji Shentu, Yu Zhang, Jiaming Sun, Xiaowei Zhou

**Abstract:** This paper addresses the problem of creating immersive volumetric videos from sparse and low-resolution capture resources. Conventional dense multi-view reconstruction methods can produce high-quality volumetric videos, but their reliance on costly capture setups makes them impractical in real-world scenarios. Recent approaches use generative models to relax capture requirements, but still suffer from limited resolution and 4D inconsistency. To overcome these constraints, we propose RealMe, a unified framework built around a high-resolution video diffusion model that enhances limited captures into high-resolution (2K), 4D-consistent videos for volumetric video reconstruction. Specifically, RealMe constructs auxiliary data from capture videos and encodes them into latents along with layout-aware positional embeddings. The video diffusion model adapts factorized spatio-temporal attention to the multi-view capture layout, denoises these latents across viewpoints and time, and decodes them into enhanced videos to refine the 4D representation. This adaptation helps the model capture temporal dynamics and multi-view relationships, leading to more 4D-consistent generation. To reduce the data gap between generated videos and real captures, we apply a latent frequency loss that provides frequency-domain supervision in latent space. Extensive experiments on the public DNA-Rendering benchmark and our self-captured RealVV dataset demonstrate that RealMe is able to create immersive volumetric videos and outperforms state-of-the-art methods in visual fidelity and 4D consistency.

## 4. [Agentic Ensemble with Vision-Language Models for Visual Anomaly Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28560) <a id="link4"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.28560
**Authors:** Shangquan Sun, Wenjun Luo, Xu He, Yoonsoo Lee, Shijian Lu

**Abstract:** Visual anomaly detection aims to detect abnormal objects, while anomaly samples are often scarce during model training. Assembling multiple pretrained detectors can help by leveraging their transferable image scores and anomaly maps. However, most existing ensemble methods apply fixed fusion rules over saved predictions which struggle when pretrained detectors clearly disagree due to different training objectives, priors, and visual representations. To this end, we design AnomalyAgent, a post-training fusion framework for accurate visual anomaly detection. AnomalyAgent aligns heterogeneous scores and maps, summarizes score- and region-level disagreement, and treats fusion as a finite action-selection problem. It implements two types of actions: 1) score actions that determine how to fuse image-level detector confidence via consensus, robust aggregation, detector weighting, or score-map consistency; 2) map actions that fuse dense anomaly responses via consensus, detector weighting, geometric aggregation, or disagreement correction. A constrained vision-language model (VLM) is introduced to inspect high-conflict samples and select valid score action and map action to produce the final prediction. We evaluate AnomalyAgent on MVTec AD and VisA as primary benchmarks, together with seven additional public anomaly benchmarks and Real-IAD for large-scale score-level evaluation. Across these benchmarks, AnomalyAgent consistently improves over equal-weight fusion and achieves the best mean image AUROC and pixel AP among the compared fusion methods on MVTec AD and VisA, while demonstrating robust transfer across additional anomaly domains.

## 5. [A Unified Evaluation Framework of Physical Adversarial Robustness in the Edge VLM Era](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29196) <a id="link5"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.29196
**Authors:** Eric Liang, Alan Luo, Kaiwen Yuan

**Abstract:** Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) enable end-to-end visual grounding via language prompts and generative decoding, demonstrating largely improved zero-shot and open-vocabulary detection capabilities. As a result, VLMs are rapidly being deployed at the edge in safety-critical real-world systems. However, the physical adversarial robustness of edge VLMs has not been systematically studied, and the fundamental differences in output with modern object detectors create methodological challenges for fair evaluation. To compare the two families directly, we evaluate every selected representative model on one outcome: whether an adversarial patch worn on a person evades detection of the model. We report this by introducing a unified metric, conditional attack success rate (cASR), counted only on images the model still localizes under a matched gray occluder, and partition every failure into omission (no box), substitution (a box on the patch), and displacement (a box on neither). We benchmark the physical adversarial vulnerability of compact grounding VLMs against a range of object detectors, and find that the VLMs can be as vulnerable as the detectors. We disclose that failure mode is largely associated with the output format of a model rather than its architecture family. Of the three, only omission is visible to a downstream system; displacement and substitution return confident, well-formed boxes, making them potentially more dangerous failures. Further, we find that, under distribution shift, VLMs that primarily failed by omission instead shift to failing more by displacement/substitution. Thus, a grounding VLM can inherit the detector's vulnerability while showing the possibility to adopt more dangerous failure modes. Our code and data are available at https://github.com/ericliang1/physical-adversarial-robustness.

## 6. [LiveAct: Towards Real-Time Multi-Agent World Models with Heterogeneous Action Interfaces](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29408) <a id="link6"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.29408
**Authors:** Xinyu Wang, Huafeng Shi, Yue Ma, Zijie Meng, Teng Xi, Yuzhuo Chen, Yucheng Wang, Ruicheng Zhang, Guoxin Zhang, Pengfei Wan, Xiu Li, Fangneng Zhan

**Abstract:** Interactive video world models predict how an environment evolves under user actions, but most existing systems assume one controlled agent and one task-specific action interface. We study multi-agent video world modeling, where one model generates a joint visual future for multiple controlled agents while interpreting heterogeneous controls ranging from binary game buttons to continuous robot commands. This task presents two complementary challenges. First, every agent-indexed action stream must control its intended visual entity independently. Second, the model must adapt the meaning of each control variable across different tasks, where the same tensor dimension may denote an attack, steering command, or robot command. We introduce LiveAct, centered on a Heterogeneous Action Interface (HAI) whose two components mirror this decomposition. To support independent control, Agent-Aware Spatial Modulation uses language-driven spatial gates and agent-specific attention to bind each action stream to its intended visual agent. To accommodate heterogeneous controls, Schema-Guided Action Adaptation describes each control variable in language and modulates its semantic token with the synchronized numerical value. We further transfer the complete interface to a blockwise causal generator through teacher forcing and rollout-aware distribution matching, enabling efficient streaming generation while retaining agent-specific control and action semantics. We instantiate the formulation on six multi-agent sources spanning games and multi-robot manipulation, and evaluate visual quality, action following, agent attribution, and latency. Across games and robot manipulation, LiveAct reaches 0.898 and 0.942 agent-attribution accuracy without agent locations or identity crops, and the causal generator attains 17.70 FPS at $512\times 448$.

## 7. [Rethinking Video Autoencoders for Interactive World Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30129) <a id="link7"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.30129
**Authors:** Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Yutong Bai, Nicolas Dufour, Patrick P\'erez, David Picard

**Abstract:** Video world models predict future frames from past frames and actions. They serve as simulators in which agents train, plan or are evaluated, and that humans can control interactively. They generate in the latent space of a video autoencoder, whose compression sets their cost per frame and how long each action waits to take effect. Yet world models often reuse the autoencoders of text-to-video generators, built for offline generation with no agent waiting and, without their training data and code, not retrainable at another compression. We therefore ask how, where and how much a world model should compress. We build CADRE, an open family of causal diffusion representation autoencoders spanning multiple spatio-temporal compressions. Its members differ only in a small bottleneck on frozen features and share a diffusion decoder self-distilled to one or a few steps. We train the same world model on each autoencoder in two domains, manipulation and navigation, with two environments each. CADRE forms most of the latency-quality frontier, especially with a moving camera. Our study also leads to four findings. At strong compression, a diffusion decoder is sharper than perceptual regression. In robot manipulation, temporal compression is more effective in the autoencoder than in the world model. Manipulation and navigation call for opposite compressions: temporal compression costs little when the camera is still, and spatial compression when it moves. Surprisingly, with a still camera, coarser spatial tokens hurt motion far more than image quality. Code, models and a live demo are at https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-10-21-cadre

## 10. [Single-Image Food Mass Estimators Do Not Transfer Their Gram Scale Across Datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29025) <a id="link10"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2610.29025
**Authors:** Yongsheng Lian

**Abstract:** Estimating food mass from a photograph is central to image-based dietary assessment, but whether trained models transfer to new cameras and foods is unclear. We fine-tuned DINOv2-base on 2,253 weighed Nutrition5k dishes to predict mass from one RGB photograph and compared it with a zero-shot vision-language model, Qwen3-VL-32B, on Nutrition5k and three external datasets: ASA24 studio portions, ECUSTFD whole foods and Food Portion Benchmark meal trays. Prediction intervals that held the true mass for 90% of Nutrition5k dishes held it for at most half of external photographs. Nutrition5k often photographed the same plate several times, so a random test split shared near-identical photographs with training and overstated accuracy. Single-image mass estimators therefore need labeled photographs from the setting where they are used.

**Abstract:** Code agents increasingly work on evolving real repositories, and memory systems aim to help them reuse earlier experience.
