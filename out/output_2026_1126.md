## 0. [Towards 360$^\circ$ Embodied UAV Tracking Without Turning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33934) <a id="link0"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.33934
**Authors:** Bingyu Li, Wu Du, Zhiyuan Zhao, Junyu Gao, Xuelong Li

**Abstract:** Conventional embodied UAV tracking couples target observability with heading control because targets may leave the field of view of forward-facing cameras. We investigate a complementary paradigm that uses full-spherical perception to follow designated targets without active heading adjustment. To support this setting, we introduce 360UAV-ETrack, a simulation benchmark comprising 2,652 sequences, over 1.73 million synchronized observations, eight tracking tasks, and 150 held-out closed-loop scenarios. ## 1. [PoseVLN: Learning Geometric State Evolution for Vision-Language Navigation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32218) <a id="link1"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.32218
**Authors:** Pengyu Hou, Mingfei Han, Haihong Hao, Xiaolong Wu, Bo Dong, Xiaojun Chang, Salman Khan, Bin Shi

**Abstract:** Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) requires an embodied agent to relate past experience, current spatial state, and future motion while following natural-language instructions. Recent approaches improve these capabilities through long-horizon memory, state-aware reasoning, and future prediction, yet these components are often modeled separately, leaving navigation state evolution insufficiently connected across time. We argue that camera pose provides a natural geometric representation for bridging past, present, and future states. Based on this insight, we propose PoseVLN, a pose-aware VLN framework that jointly learns navigation decisions and geometric state evolution. PoseVLN organizes visual memory and the current observation in an episode-relative coordinate frame, and jointly predicts the next K navigation actions together with a local $\Delta$Pose over the same horizon. The predicted $\Delta$Pose is composed with the current trajectory-level pose to obtain the future pose state, which is recursively propagated throughout navigation, forming a continuous geometric state evolution from past memory to future motion. Experiments on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE Val-Unseen splits demonstrate the effectiveness of explicitly modeling geometric state evolution, with PoseVLN outperforming the strongest compared baselines by 2.6 and 2.9 percentage points in success rate, respectively, using only single RGB observations.

## 3. [Dynamic Forcing: Learning Action-Grounded Sparse Dynamic Representations with Dense Future Guidance for World Action Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33799) <a id="link3"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.33799
**Authors:** Ziqiang Li, Shisheng Chen, Yunnan Wang, Wenyao Zhang, Zili Lin, Yuyang Zhang, Xudong Yang, Fangzhuo Jin, Chenyu Zhou, Wenjun Zeng, Xin Jin

**Abstract:** World action models (WAMs) support robot control through visual prediction, but dense future modeling repeatedly encodes static context already available in the current observation, while action-relevant changes are often spatially sparse. We study sparse dynamic representation learning, retaining the current observation as a contextual anchor and using a small set of compact dynamic tokens to represent anticipated changes. We develop CompactWAM, a sparse-dense joint predictive framework that enables sparse representation learning within a video-supervised shared backbone, without requiring a reconstruction objective for the sparse pathway. Since a limited token budget alone does not specify which information to retain, we introduce Dynamic Forcing, which pairs direct action supervision with privileged dense future guidance to promote action relevance and dynamic fidelity. While action prediction gradients directly drive compact tokens to capture control-relevant cues, dense future guidance further shapes them via action functional guidance that matches sparse and dense contributions to the action expert, and dynamics-aware distribution matching, which aligns temporal relational changes relative to a static reference. At inference, a single visual forward pass encodes current observations and compact dynamic tokens for action generation, eliminating dense future modeling. CompactWAM achieves 98.4\% success on LIBERO and 77.9\% on LIBERO-Plus, retaining respective success rates of 98.3\% and 76.6\% with only 9.2\% of the dense future token budget. Evaluations on RoboTwin~2.0 and real-world manipulation further demonstrate the effectiveness of the learned sparse predictive representations.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 3 closely: a new method for world action models in embodied robotics, with sparse dynamic representations and action-grounded future guidance.

## 4. [ArtKit: Motion-Driven Re-Segmentation and Link Re-Generation for Articulated Objects from RGB-D Video](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33403) <a id="link4"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.33403
**Authors:** Sylvia Yuan, Dan Wang, Vivian Yu, Ravi Ramamoorthi, Xinrui Cui

**Abstract:** An RGB-D camera that films an object being moved, often from one viewpoint, sees one side of it and how its parts move. We present ArtKit, which takes such a video as input to create an articulated model with complete parts, a kinematic tree, and typed joints. Our central idea is generating the hidden geometry and verifying it against what the camera sees. A generative part prior proposes complete, part-decomposed geometry based on the first frame. Point tracks, grouped into rigid trajectories, split and merge the generated parts into links. We then fit revolute and prismatic joints, with a visibility-aware energy ensuring that the articulated model explains the depth observed across the video. A rule checks against the video to choose between synchronous group fits and per-frame chain fits, so that one pipeline serves a diverse range of articulated objects, such as household items and robotic hands. Finally, we regenerate each link of the chosen fit as one part and keep it wherever it agrees better with the observations. We evaluate on Articulate-100 and on HArt, a new benchmark of 60 PartNet-Mobility objects and 8 robotic hands and grippers rendered as RGB-D videos with ground-truth parts and joints. ArtKit reduces the axis and part motion errors of the strongest baseline by more than half on the objects, as well as the axis error on the hands. ArtKit also runs on real recordings and generated videos.

## 5. [EffectWAM: Learning Action-Grounded Effects for Robot Manipulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32053) <a id="link5"></a>

**Abstract:** World-action models connect visual prediction with executable robot control. We present EffectWAM, which develops this connection through three complementary components: Effect, camera positional encoding (CaPE), and Action Grounding. Effect represents visual change as the difference between independently encoded future observations and a shared current-observation anchor. CaPE conditions endpoint pooling on relative camera pose when reliable extrinsics are available, while sharing the same learned parameters with pose-free pooling for uncalibrated trajectories. **Abstract:** We present SPARC, a geometry-guided attention pruning framework that turns the native self-attention of pretrained video diffusion transformers into a unified mechanism for camera control and persistent scene memory. ## 11. [ArtTet: Watertight Part Meshes of Articulated Objects from Rigid-Body Rules and Partitioned Marching Tetrahedra](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33382) <a id="link11"></a>
**ArXiv ID:** 2611.33382
**Authors:** Sylvia Yuan, Dan Wang, Ravi Ramamoorthi, Xinrui Cui

**Abstract:** We present ArtTet, a method that reconstructs an articulated object from multi-view observations of two articulation states and outputs one watertight mesh per rigid part together with the joints that move them. Mesh splatting gives explicit triangles, but it treats the object as one scene. It extracts a single global surface and cannot keep the triangles of different parts apart; the mesh it exports is layered, self-intersecting, and full of holes. ArtTet follows a divide-and-conquer strategy instead. We first divide the object into rigid parts with dynamic- and geometric-constrained articulation and ownership inference. A multibody registration of the two states recovers the parts and their screw joints. Rigid-body rules on attachment, contact, and swept motion then correct joint types and part labels without any part annotation. We then divide space into part cells with an articulated tetrahedral partition, and conquer each cell with its own differentiable signed distance field on a shared tetrahedral grid. In these partitioned marching tetrahedra the fields are mutually exclusive by construction, so the extracted part meshes are closed and never overlap. All part meshes are optimized jointly from the images of both states through the recovered joints. On Articulate-100, ArtTet gives the most accurate axis directions, part motions, and part geometry over all objects among methods that use no part masks, and its part meshes have virtually no open boundary edges. We also show reconstructions of real captures from the SplArt dataset.

**Comment:** Matches criterion 3 very closely: embodied AI / articulated-object reconstruction with a new method for building watertight part meshes and recovering joints from multi-view observations.

## 14. [ArtLang-4D: Articulation-Aware Dynamic Language Fields from Monocular Videos](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33511) <a id="link14"></a>
